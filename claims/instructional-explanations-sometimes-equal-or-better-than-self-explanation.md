# Self-explanation beats unguided studying for equal time, but instructional explanations, problem solving and retrieval practice can be equally or more effective

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A meta-analysis of six studies comparing self-explanation prompts plus worked examples versus instructional explanations found no effect of condition, with mixed findings across studies. [→ Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017](#bethany-rittle-johnson-abbey-m-loehr-2017)
`q2 i?` Retrieval practice supported long-term retention substantially better than prompted self-explanation, though both beat unsupported studying. [→ Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (2)](#bethany-rittle-johnson-abbey-m-loehr-2017-2)
`q2 i?` When time on task is controlled, prompted self-explanation continues to improve comprehension and transfer relative to unguided studying. [→ Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (3)](#bethany-rittle-johnson-abbey-m-loehr-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Wittwer & Renkl (2010) meta-analysis of six worked-example studies: "there was no effect of condition," reflecting findings that variously favored instructional explanations, self-explanations, or neither.

> "In a meta-analysis of six studies that compared learning from
worked examples in conjunction with self-explanation
prompts vs. instructional explanations, there was no effect of
condition (Wittwer & Renkl,2010)."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (2)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Larsen et al. (2013) with medical-school students over a 6-month delay. Both techniques beat unsupported studying, but repeated testing's "effect... was much stronger"; adding self-explanation to testing yielded a non-reliable difference.

> "Both repeated-testing and
prompted self-explanation supported medical-school stu-
dents' comprehension 6-months later better than unsupported
studying. However, the effect of repeated-testing was much
stronger."

### Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr 2017 (3)

Bethany Rittle-Johnson & Abbey M. Loehr. (2017). Eliciting explanations: Constraints on when self-explanation aids learning. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24, 1501–1510. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1079-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review notes that although self-explanation takes far more time (e.g., nearly twice as long in Chi et al., 1994), time-controlled studies show prompts still improve comprehension and transfer relative to unguided studying for the same amount of time.

> "In studies that have controlled for time on task, self-
explanation prompts continued to improve comprehension
and transfer"
