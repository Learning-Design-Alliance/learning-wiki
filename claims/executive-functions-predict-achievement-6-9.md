## Evidence

### Quílez-Robres A. 2021

Quílez-Robres A., Moyano N., Cortés-Pascual A. (2021). Task Monitoring and Working Memory as Executive Components Predictive of General and Specific Academic Achievements in 6–9-Year-Old Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18136681

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Linear regressions using the step forward method in a convenience sample of 133 children aged 6–9, with parent-rated BRIEF-2 executive function scores predicting "14.7% (GAA), 12.3% (AAL), and 12.2% (AAM)" of achievement variance. Adjusted R² values are printed in Table 2 with p < 0.001, and Table 1 gives the correlations of the executive-function score with general, Language Arts and Mathematics achievement (r = −0.392, −0.361 and −0.361, p < 0.01).

> "Overall, executive functions accounted for 14.7% (GAA), 12.3% (AAL), and 12.2% (AAM) of the variance in student achievement (Table 2)."
