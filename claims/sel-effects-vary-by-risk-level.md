## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Program effects are larger for those least at risk in some cases (for example, students not in low-income schools) and larger for those most at risk in other cases (for example, students beginning with poorer skills). [→ Stephanie M. Jones 2017](#stephanie-m-jones-2017)

## Evidence

### Stephanie M. Jones 2017

Stephanie M. Jones, Sophie P. Barnes, Rebecca Bailey, and Emily J. Doolittle. (2017). Promoting Social and Emotional Competencies in Elementary School. The Future of Children, Vol. 27, No. 1, Spring 2017. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144815

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review synthesis of subgroup findings across the randomized studies, which examined racial/ethnic background, socioeconomic status, and baseline risk or ability. Examples printed include weaker Fast Track PATHS effects in low-income schools for acceptance of authority, cognitive concentration, and social competence, and larger Making Choices gains for children considered at risk at study start. No effect sizes are printed for the overall pattern.

> "Thus in some cases program effects are larger for those least at risk, such as those not in low-income schools, and in other cases they're larger for those most at risk, such as students who begin with poorer skills at the start of the year."
