# Learner–content interaction was perceived as significantly more helpful for learning than learner–learner interaction despite equivalent measured performance

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Students in the learner–content group reported a significantly greater perception of learning than students in the learner–learner group, with a medium–large effect size. [→ Hauert 2026](#hauert-2026)

## Evidence

### Hauert 2026

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

`q4 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Two-tailed independent samples t test in the same randomized trial (n = 66, 33 per group) on adapted Kuo et al. perception-of-learning surveys. The learner–content group mean (M = 4.30, SD = .59) was greater than the learner–learner group mean (M = 3.86, SD = .81), with "Cohen's d was .63".

> "The results supported a statistically significant difference in perception of learning between the two interaction groups, t(64) = 2.57, p = .013. Cohen's d was .63, suggesting a medium–large effect size"
