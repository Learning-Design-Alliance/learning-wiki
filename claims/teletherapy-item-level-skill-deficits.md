# Specific skills — verbal fluency, hope, persuasiveness, emotional expression, empathy and alliance bond capacity — are lower in teletherapy, while warmth and rupture-repair responsiveness show no overall difference

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Six of eight FIS items were rated lower in teletherapy, with negligible effect sizes (d = 0.11–0.15). [→ Lin 2025](#lin-2025)

## Evidence

### Lin 2025

Lin, T., Aafjes‐van Doorn, K., Heckman, T. G., Antebi‐Lerman, E., & Anderson, T. (2025). Are Therapists Less Skilful in Teletherapy Than In‐Person Therapy Scenarios? A Latent Profile Analysis of Facilitative Interpersonal Skills. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70047

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Item-level paired t-tests on coder ratings in the same 223-participant simulation study, with Bonferroni correction. All six item differences were significant with negligible printed effect sizes; the article reports "d = 0.15" for alliance bond capacity as the largest.

> "therapists were had lower levels of observer‐rated verbal fluency (p < 0.001, d = 0.12), hope (p < 0.05, d = 0.14), persuasiveness (p < 0.01, d = 0.13), emotional expression (p < 0.001, d = 0.11), empathy (p < 0.01, d = 0.14) and alliance bond capacity (p < 0.01, d = 0.15)"

### Lin 2025 (2)

Lin, T., Aafjes‐van Doorn, K., Heckman, T. G., Antebi‐Lerman, E., & Anderson, T. (2025). Are Therapists Less Skilful in Teletherapy Than In‐Person Therapy Scenarios? A Latent Profile Analysis of Facilitative Interpersonal Skills. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70047

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null item-level findings from the same paired comparison of tele‐FIS versus in-person FIS coder ratings; no effect sizes were reported for these two items, so equivalence was not tested.

> "There were no differences in observer‐rated warmth or alliance rupture repair responsiveness between the two formats."
