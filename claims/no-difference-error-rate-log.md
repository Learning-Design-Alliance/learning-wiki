# Error rates per completed problem-solving step did not significantly differ between conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2026 · checked against its sources 2026-10-20 (automated, 1 of 1 entry) · not reviewed by a person

In Rief et al. (2026), seventh graders practised algebra in the "Math with Matt" tutoring system. The Mindful version added an LLM-based mindful chat, in which the pedagogical agent Matt gave context-sensitive emotional support, and mindful (not only evaluative) feedback and hint messages; the Cognitive version gave cognitive support only.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Error rate per completed step was statistically comparable across conditions. [→ Rief 2026](#rief-2026)

## Evidence

### Rief 2026

Rief, V., Hladký, M., Yoo, M., Heel, S., Sato, S., Nagashima, T. (2026). Beyond Problem Solving: Large Language Models for Emotional and Reflective Support in Mathematics Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02611 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.02611](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.02611)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory log-data analysis from all 252 participating students found error rates of M=0.46 (Cognitive) versus M=0.47 (Mindful) per completed step, not significantly different (W=7257, p=.606); equivalence was not tested.

> "Error rate did not significantly differ between groups withM= 0.46 (SD= 0.38) for the Cognitive andM= 0.47 (SD= 0.45) for the Mindful group (Mann-Whitney-U,W= 7257,p= .606)."
