## Evidence

### Kristina Armbruster 2026

Kristina Armbruster, Gintaras Duda, and Thomas G. Wong. (2026). Improving Student Self-Confidence in Quantum Computing with the Qubit Touchdown Board Game. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1119/5.0258097](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0258097)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey study of 107 public high school students in Precalculus, AP Statistics, and/or AP Physics 1 classes; Likert responses to confidence questions Q2, Q4, and Q8 at three time points. The article reports "a net improvement in self-confidence of 32.8%" with Wilcoxon p<0.001 for Q2→Q4, Q4→Q8, and Q2→Q8.

> "Their self-confidence improved after playing the game and again after learning the game's connection to quantum computing."

### Kristina Armbruster 2026 (2)

Kristina Armbruster, Gintaras Duda, and Thomas G. Wong. (2026). Improving Student Self-Confidence in Quantum Computing with the Qubit Touchdown Board Game. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1119/5.0258097](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0258097)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Friedman test on ordinal Likert responses to confidence questions Q2, Q4, and Q8 across the three time points, followed by pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with continuity correction; the article reports "χ 2 =49.004,d f =2,p<0.001".

> "we found a stronger effect: there was a significant main effect of time point for confidence in their ability to learn quantum computing by utilizing a Friedman test (χ 2 =49.004,d f =2,p<0.001)."
