## Evidence

### Armstrong G 2010

Armstrong G, Blashki G, Joubert L, Bland R, Moulding R, Gunn J, Naccarella L. (2010). An evaluation of the effect of an educational intervention for Australian social workers on competence in delivering brief cognitive behavioural strategies: A randomised controlled trial. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6963-10-304

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression analysis of questionnaire-measured self-perceived competence in the RCT (n = 30), adjusting for baseline confidence; the adjusted difference was 1.28 points (95% CI 0.84–1.72, p < 0.001). Confidence in each individual strategy was also significantly higher in the intervention group. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "On average, at follow-up participants in the intervention group rated their overall confidence in using cognitive behavioural strategies 1.28 points higher than the control group on the five-point Likert scales, when controlling for baseline confidence ratings."
