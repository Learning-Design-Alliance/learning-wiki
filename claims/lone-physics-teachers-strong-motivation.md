## Evidence

### Hestenes 1997

Hestenes, D. & Jackson, J. (1997). Partnerships for Physics Teaching Reform — a crucial role for universities and colleges. Proceedings of the International Conference on Undergraduate Physics Education, College Park, August 1996. [doi:10.1063/1.53143](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.53143)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The AIP nationwide survey of high school physics teachers, as reported in the article, found that "Of the 18,000 physics teachers nationwide, 81% are the only physics teacher in the school." This statistic grounds the authors' argument that teachers lack peer contact in their schools. No effect size is printed.

> "Of the 18,000 physics teachers nationwide, 81% are the only physics teacher in the school."

### Hestenes 1997 (2)

Hestenes, D. & Jackson, J. (1997). Partnerships for Physics Teaching Reform — a crucial role for universities and colleges. Proceedings of the International Conference on Undergraduate Physics Education, College Park, August 1996. [doi:10.1063/1.53143](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.53143)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The same AIP survey data, cited in the article's section on empowering teachers, shows strong motivation despite isolation: "More than 90% want to teach physics as often as possible, though only 35% teach it every year." The authors use this to argue teachers are eager, not unwilling, participants in reform. No effect size is printed.

> "More than 90% want to teach physics as often as possible, though only 35% teach it every year."
