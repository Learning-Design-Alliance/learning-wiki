# Q14 on forces between charges inside a conducting shell shows virtually indistinguishable performance across introductory, upper-level, and graduate students due to Newton's 3rd law misconception

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On CSEM Q14, which asks about forces experienced by two charges with a conducting shell between them, performance is comparable across all levels (38% introductory, 44% upper-level, 38% graduate), with the most common incorrect answer being that the forces are equal and opposite per Newton's 3rd law. ## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2023

Alexandru Maries, Mary Jane Brundage and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Using the Conceptual Survey of Electricity and Magnetism to investigate progression in student understanding from introductory to advanced levels. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2311.17170](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.17170)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of CSEM Q14 responses across three student populations: 401 introductory post-test, 85 upper-level post-test, and 87 first-year graduate students before graduate EM instruction. The authors state this is "the question most resistant to change from the introductory level all the way to the graduate level."

> "the percent of students who answered this question correctly was 38% (intro ductory), 44% (upper-level) and 38% (graduate), and the most common incorrect answer choice for all three groups of students was due to the application of  Newton's 3 rd law, namely that the two charges should feel equal and opposite forces"
