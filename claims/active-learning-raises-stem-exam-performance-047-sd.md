# Active learning raises undergraduate STEM examination and concept-inventory performance by about 0.47 SDs compared with traditional lecturing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q4` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` Across 158 independent comparisons, student performance on identical or equivalent examinations, concept inventories, and other assessments increased by a weighted standardized mean difference of 0.47 under active learning versus lecturing. [→ Freeman S 2014](#freeman-s-2014)

## Evidence

### Freeman S 2014

Freeman S, Eddy SL, McDonough M, Smith MK, Okoroafor N, Jordt H, Wenderoth MP. (2014). Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. PNAS 111(23):8410-8415. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 158 independent comparisons of examination/concept-inventory scores in undergraduate STEM courses, contrasting active learning with traditional lecturing. The pooled Hedges's g was 0.47, "Z = 9.781, P << 0.001". The article translates this to roughly a 6% score increase.

> "The overall mean effect size for performance on identical or equivalent examinations, concept inventories, and other assessments was a weighted standardized mean difference of 0.47 ( Z = 9.781, P << 0.001)"
