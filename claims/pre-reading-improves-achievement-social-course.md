## Evidence

### Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011

Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu. (2011). The Effects of Pre-reading and Sharing Mechanisms on Learning with the Use of Annotations. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANCOVA in phase 1 of the 8-week quasi-experiment with 125 sixth-graders in four classes, controlling pre-test scores; the pre-reading group (Experiment and Control B) mean was 80.11 versus 73.50 for the no-pre-reading group. The article reports "the main effect on pre-reading is significant".

> "Fourth, the main effect on pre-reading is significant ( F (1,120)  = 12.921, p<.01), while controlled for the pre-test scores."

### Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu 2011 (2)

Wu-Yuin Hwang & Guo-Liang Hsu. (2011). The Effects of Pre-reading and Sharing Mechanisms on Learning with the Use of Annotations. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 10(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ932242

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-way ANCOVA in phase 2, with the sharing mechanism in place for VPEN classes; the pre-reading group mean was 80.8 versus 75.58 for the group not asked to pre-read. The article reports "learning achievements are significantly affected by pre-reading".

> "the results indicate that a main effect of pre-reading is F (1,120) =11.699, p<.01, which means that the pre-reading group and the group that did not ask for pre-reading have a statistical difference."
