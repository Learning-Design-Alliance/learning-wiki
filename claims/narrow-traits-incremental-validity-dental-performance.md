## Evidence

### Jaber Mohamed 2022

Jaber Mohamed, Al-Samarrai Basim, Salah Afraa, Varma Sudhir Rama, Karobari Mohmed Isaqali, Marya Anand. (2022). Does General and Specific Traits of Personality Predict Students' Academic Performance? BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9422299

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression analyses (Table 7, N = 144) regressing each performance criterion on global conscientiousness at Step 1, then the six narrow traits at Step 2. The article prints variance-explained increments (ΔR² = 0.15 academic, ΔR² = 0.10 clinic) but no standardised effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect); increases were statistically significant "across all performance criteria".

> "For academic performance, the variance explained by the narrow traits over global conscientiousness was substantial (Ѧ R2 = 0.15), as was the percentage of variance in clinical performance explained by the narrow traits above global conscientiousness (Ѧ R2 = 0.10)."

### Jaber Mohamed 2022 (2)

Jaber Mohamed, Al-Samarrai Basim, Salah Afraa, Varma Sudhir Rama, Karobari Mohmed Isaqali, Marya Anand. (2022). Does General and Specific Traits of Personality Predict Students' Academic Performance? BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9422299

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Step 2 of the hierarchical regressions (Table 7, N = 144). The article states dutifulness "appeared to be the optimal predictor" with beta 0.37 academic and 0.27 clinic; these are regression beta weights, not standardised effect sizes, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect); self-discipline and deliberation also contributed to academic prediction.

> "In particular, dutifulness appeared to be the optimal predictor of academic and clinical performances, demonstrating a considerable beta weight of 0.37 for academic and 0.27 for the clinic."
