## Evidence

### Sommet Nicolas 2013

Sommet Nicolas, Pulfrey Caroline, Butera Fabrizio. (2013). Did My M.D. Really Go to University to Learn? Detrimental Effects of Numerus Clausus on Self-Efficacy, Mastery Goals and Learning. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0084178

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Secondary analysis in the Study 2 medical-student sample: the gender effect on self-efficacy had partial eta-squared η2 = .08, and "Male participants were found to report more self-efficacy" than female participants.

> "Unexpectedly, analyses also revealed a positive effect of gender, B = 0.60, F(1, 136) = 13.21, p < .001, η2 p = .08. Male participants were found to report more self-efficacy (M = 5.25, SE = 0.14) than female participants (M = 4.65, SE = 0.09)."

### Sommet Nicolas 2013 (2)

Sommet Nicolas, Pulfrey Caroline, Butera Fabrizio. (2013). Did My M.D. Really Go to University to Learn? Detrimental Effects of Numerus Clausus on Self-Efficacy, Mastery Goals and Learning. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0084178

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bootstrap mediation in the Study 3 experiment: "the indirect effect of gender on learning score through self-efficacy was also significant", with a BCa 95% CI of .09 to .48; no standardized effect size is printed for this indirect effect.

> "the indirect effect of gender on learning score through self-efficacy was also significant, B = 0.23, SE = 0.09, with a BCa 95% CI of .09 to .48, R 2 MED = .001."
