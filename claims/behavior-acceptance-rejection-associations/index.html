Evidence¶

Krijnen 2024¶

Krijnen, M. A., Wansink, B. G. J., van den Berg, Y. H. M., van Tartwijk, J., & Mainhard, T. (2024). Citizenship in the Elementary Classroom Through the Lens of Peer Relations. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09870-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Torrente et al. (2014) and Wang et al. (2019), that "children who receive more nominations on prosocial behavior are more accepted and less rejected," with the association stronger in high-prosocial-norm classrooms; a longitudinal study (Wentzel, 2003) showed accepted children later behaved more prosocially.

"children who receive more nominations on prosocial behavior are more accepted and less rejected (Torrente et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2019), especially in classrooms with a high prosocial descriptive norm"

Krijnen 2024 (2)¶

Krijnen, M. A., Wansink, B. G. J., van den Berg, Y. H. M., van Tartwijk, J., & Mainhard, T. (2024). Citizenship in the Elementary Classroom Through the Lens of Peer Relations. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09870-5

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing multiple studies, that children "are less accepted and more rejected by classmates when they are perceived as aggressive," but notes a few studies found positive aggression-acceptance associations in classrooms where aggression was generally accepted or popularity was concentrated.

"studies generally indicate that children are less accepted and more rejected by classmates when they are perceived as aggressive"
