## Evidence

### Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers 2011

Björn B. de Koning & Huib K. Tabbers. (2011). Facilitating Understanding of Movements in Dynamic Visualizations: an Embodied Perspective. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9173-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports Singer and Goldin-Meadow's experiment on mathematical equivalence instruction with no gestures, matching gestures, or mismatching gestures; benefit emerged only for gestures conveying a different strategy than speech. The review attributes this to multiple modalities reducing cognitive load; no effect size is printed.

> "It was found that learners profited from instruction with gestures but only when the gestures conveyed a different strategy than the one expressed in speech. These results suggest that if a problem-solving strategy needs to be learned, providing a second strategy can be helpful for learning"
