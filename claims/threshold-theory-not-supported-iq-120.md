# Threshold theory is not supported: correlations below and above IQ 120 do not differ significantly, and the threshold variable does not explain variance in the correlation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean correlations below IQ 120 (r = .201) and above IQ 120 (r = .235) were not statistically different, and adding the threshold variable to a weighted regression did not significantly change R². [→ Kim 2005](#kim-2005)

## Evidence

### Kim 2005

Kim, K. H. (2005). Can Only Intelligent People Be Creative? A Meta-Analysis. The Journal of Secondary Gifted Education, XVI(2/3), 57-66. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Can+Only+Intelligent+People+Be+Creative

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic moderator analysis comparing 65 correlation coefficients below IQ 120 with 14 above IQ 120. The a priori contrast was non-significant (χ2 = 2.004, p = .157); equivalence was not tested. In the weighted regression, adding threshold yielded "R? Change = .004, F Change (1) = 1.269, p = .261."

> "On an a priori basis, IQ below (r = .201) and above 120 (r = .235) were not statistically different either, χ2 = 2.004, p = .157."
