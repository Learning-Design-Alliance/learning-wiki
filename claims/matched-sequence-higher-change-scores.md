## Evidence

### Kasee L. Smith & John Rayfield 2017

Kasee L. Smith & John Rayfield. (2017). A Quasi-Experimental Examination: Cognitive Sequencing of Instruction Using Experiential Learning Theory for STEM Concepts in Agricultural Education. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(4), 175-191. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.04175

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple main effects tests following the significant ANOVA interactions in the crossover quasi-experiment (n = 121). The article reports significantly higher scores for preference-matched sequences on both units; it prints no effect size for these tests.

> "The results of the simple main effects tests revealed that, for both units of instruction, students had significantly higher scores in the unit sequenced to begin with their preferred method of grasping information."

### Kasee L. Smith & John Rayfield 2017 (2)

Kasee L. Smith & John Rayfield. (2017). A Quasi-Experimental Examination: Cognitive Sequencing of Instruction Using Experiential Learning Theory for STEM Concepts in Agricultural Education. Journal of Agricultural Education, 58(4), 175-191. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2017.04175

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors' summary of the three main findings from the quasi-experiment, stated in the Conclusions/Implications section. Descriptive means in Table 2 show, for example, apprehension-preferring students averaging M = 41.82 (SD = 24.57) on the water unit change score.

> "students in this study who preferred to grasp experience through apprehension had higher change scores from pretest to posttest when the units were sequenced to begin with a concrete experience, students who preferred to grasp experience through comprehension had higher change scores when the units were sequenced to begin with abstract conceptualization"
