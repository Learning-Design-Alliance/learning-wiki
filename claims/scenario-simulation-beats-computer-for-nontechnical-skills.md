## Evidence

### Yu Jiafeng 2026

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group AS-NTS comparison: Group C rose from 5.90 ± 2.43 to 10.67 ± 2.33 (P < 0.001); the article reports similar significant gains for Groups M and SM.

> "In Group C, the T0 score was 5.90 ± 2.43, which was significantly lower than the T1 score of 10.67 ± 2.33 (P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 5, Fig. 3A)."

### Yu Jiafeng 2026 (2)

Yu Jiafeng, Wang Qian, Yin Ling, Yuan Fang, Kan Yufei, Wang Zengkun, Yu Yonghao, Li Qing, Yu Yang. (2026). Prospective randomized controlled trial of computer simulation versus high-fidelity mannequin simulation in advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) training for anesthesiology novice clinicians. BMC Anesthesiology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04080-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison at T1 on the AS-NTS scale found Group M and Group SM significantly higher than Group C (P < 0.05), indicating an advantage for scenario-based simulation in non-technical skills.

> "However, a statistically significant difference was detected at T1, with scores in both Group M and Group SM significantly higher than those in Group C (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3B)."
