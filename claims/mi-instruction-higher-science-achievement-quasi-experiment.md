# Multiple-intelligences-based science instruction produced significantly higher post-test achievement than traditional instruction in a quasi-experiment with fifth graders

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fifth-grade students taught science with a teaching strategy based on multiple intelligences scored significantly higher on a 30-item achievement test than students taught with traditional teacher-directed instruction (ANCOVA F = 6.969, p = .012). [→ Ali abdi 2013](#ali-abdi-2013)

## Evidence

### Ali abdi 2013

Ali abdi, Soosan Laei, Hamze Ahmadyan. (2013). The Effect of Teaching Strategy Based on Multiple Intelligences on Students’ Academic Achievement in Science Course. Universal Journal of Educational Research 1(4): 281-284. https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2013.010401

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANCOVA on post-test scores of a 30-item teacher-made achievement test, comparing 20 experimental and 20 control fifth graders after 8 weeks of instruction, controlling for pre-test. The article reports "a significant difference" between the multiple intelligence teaching strategy and the conventional approach groups.

> "Table 2 indicates that the (F) value was (6.969) and it was significant value at the level (.012). This means that there is a significant difference in the means score of students taught sciences education using multiple intelligence teaching strategy and those taught using conventional approach."
