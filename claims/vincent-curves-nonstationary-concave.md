## Evidence

### Suppes 1963

Suppes, P., & Ginsberg, R. (1963). A fundamental property of all-or-none models, binomial distribution of responses prior to conditioning, with application to concept formation in children. Psychological Review, 70(2), 139-161. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/rev/70/2/139

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Vincent-curve reanalysis of responses prior to the last error in five experiments and five Stoll subgroups: "Nonstationarity and concavity are the two striking characteristics of all five curves in Figure 10"; six curves show statistically significant nonstationarity on quartile blocks, in sharp contrast to the earlier nonsignificant tests.

> "Nonstationarity and concavity are the two striking characteristics of all five curves in Figure 10. Two of the five curves in Figure 11 also exhibit these properties, whereas the other three are approximately stationary."

### Suppes 1963 (2)

Suppes, P., & Ginsberg, R. (1963). A fundamental property of all-or-none models, binomial distribution of responses prior to conditioning, with application to concept formation in children. Psychological Review, 70(2), 139-161. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/rev/70/2/139

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Authors' interpretation of the Vincent-curve results: because the nonstationary curves are relatively stationary for the first two or three quartiles, they offer the rough estimate that the one-element model accounts for "about two-thirds to three-fourths of the data".

> "A rough but intuitive way of putting it is that the one-element all-or-none conditioning model seems to be accounting for about two-thirds to three-fourths of the data."
