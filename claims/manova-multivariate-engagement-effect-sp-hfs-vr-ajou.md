## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Yu Jihye 2026

Yu Jihye, Lee Sukyung, Kim Mira, Lee Janghoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2026). Evaluating medical students’ engagement and confidence across three simulation-based education methods: standardized patient, high fidelity simulator, and virtual reality. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08634-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

MANOVA of post-module engagement surveys (McCoy Exit Survey items; 5-point Likert) from 43 fifth-year medical students who completed SP-, HFS-, and VR-based modules in a respiratory rotation. The article reports "Wilks' Lambda = 0.842, F(8, 246) = 2.762, p < .01".

> "The MANOVA revealed that the multivariate effect of simulation methods on the dependent variables was significant (Wilks' Lambda = 0.842, F(8, 246) = 2.762, p < .01)."

### Yu Jihye 2026 (2)

Yu Jihye, Lee Sukyung, Kim Mira, Lee Janghoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2026). Evaluating medical students’ engagement and confidence across three simulation-based education methods: standardized patient, high fidelity simulator, and virtual reality. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08634-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up one-way ANOVAs on the same engagement survey data. The article reports "no significant differences in individual engagement or its sub-factors—relevance, interest, and flow"; no effect sizes are printed.

> "However, subsequent one-way ANOVA analyses revealed no significant differences in individual engagement or its sub-factors—relevance, interest, and flow."
