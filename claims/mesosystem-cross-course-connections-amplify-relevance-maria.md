## Evidence

### Abhilash Nair and Vashti Sawtelle 2019

Abhilash Nair and Vashti Sawtelle. (2019). Operationalizing relevance in physics education: using a systems view to expand our conception of making physics relevant. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview data from case study of Maria, a microbiology major, in the BLiSS Physics course. She states that taking "the microbiology course alongside physics, where things like work and torque and force are coming up, in a field that I know about" helped her see physics as a tool for microbiology. Qualitative interview evidence; no effect size.

> "I think taking this course, the microbiology course alongside physics, where things like work and torque and force are coming up, in a field that I know about. That helps you see..."

### Abhilash Nair and Vashti Sawtelle 2019 (2)

Abhilash Nair and Vashti Sawtelle. (2019). Operationalizing relevance in physics education: using a systems view to expand our conception of making physics relevant. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020121)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

In the paramecium resistive-force activity, Maria reasoned from osmosis that the paramecium's density could be approximated as water; the professor had not thought of it that way, validated the idea, and had her tell it to the class. The authors call this "an observable display of relevance." Qualitative case-study evidence.

> "This participatory act of Maria bringing in and successfully incorporating her biology content knowledge into a physics activity is an observable display of relevance."
