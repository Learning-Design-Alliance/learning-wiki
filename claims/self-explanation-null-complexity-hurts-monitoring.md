## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the Baars et al. (2018a) null result: self-explanation "did not affect secondary education students' retrospective monitoring accuracy" after example study or problem solving. No effect size is printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "While self-explaining had been found to be effective for improving monitoring accuracy of text comprehension (Griffin et al. 2008), it did not affect secondary education students' retrospective monitoring accuracy (i.e., judging how well they performed a task just completed, also known as self-assessment) when applied after studying examples or solving problems (Baars et al. 2018a)."

### Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel 2020 (2)

Tamara van Gog & Vincent Hoogerheide & Milou van Harsel. (2020). The Role of Mental Effort in Fostering Self-Regulated Learning with Problem-Solving Tasks. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09544-y

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the complexity effect from the same study: judgments were "more accurate retrospective monitoring judgments on less complex problems than on more complex problems". No effect size is printed.

> "Baars et al. (2018a) did find that problem complexity affected monitoring accuracy: students made more accurate retrospective monitoring judgments on less complex problems than on more complex problems."
