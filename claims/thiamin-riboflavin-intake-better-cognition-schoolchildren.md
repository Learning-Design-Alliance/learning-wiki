## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In adjusted multivariable logistic regression, children in higher thiamin tertiles had greater odds of better cognitive performance (AOR = 6.3) than those in lower tertiles, as did children in higher riboflavin tertiles (AOR = 2.2). [→ Tia Achil 2025](#tia-achil-2025)

## Evidence

### Tia Achil 2025

Tia Achil, Konan Amoin G., Hauser Jonas, Ndri Kouassi Y., Ciclet Olivier, Esso Lasme E., Nindjin Charlemagne. (2025). A Cross-Sectional Study of the Relationship Between Dietary Micronutrient Intake, Cognition and Academic Performance Among School-Aged Children in Taabo, Côte d’Ivoire. Nutrients 17(22):3602. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17223602

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional observational study of 252 schoolchildren aged 6–12 in Taabo, Côte d’Ivoire; adjusted logistic regression comparing higher versus lower intake tertiles found "greater odds of better cognitive performance compared with lower tertiles". Odds ratios are printed but no standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "In multivariable regression analysis, higher tertiles of thiamin (AOR = 6.3; 95% CI: 2.5–16.0; p < 0.001) and riboflavin (AOR = 2.2; 95% CI: 1.5–7.8; p = 0.003) were associated with greater odds of better cognitive performance compared with lower tertiles."
