## Evidence

### Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026

Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Striking the Balance: How Variability Shapes Retrieval Practice and Worked Examples for Transfer Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2's mixed-effects ANOVA on final-test accuracy showed a significant strategy × variability interaction with printed effect size ηp² = .06 ("F(1,72) = 4.78, p = .032, ηp 2 = .06"), contrasting with the non-significant interaction in Experiment 1.

> "The interaction between variability and learning strategy was also statisti-cally significant, F(1,72) = 4.78, p = .032, ηp 2 = .06, as depicted in Fig. 3."

### Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026 (2)

Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Striking the Balance: How Variability Shapes Retrieval Practice and Worked Examples for Transfer Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple-effects analysis in the varied-items condition of Experiment 2 found no significant strategy difference, "t(72) = −0.48, p = .635"; equivalence was not formally tested and no effect size was printed.

> "varied items, there was no significant difference between those who practiced with retrieval practice ( M = 0.64, SE = 0.06) and those who practiced with worked examples (M = 0.67, SE = 0.06), t(72) = −0.48, p = .635."

### Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026 (3)

Meng Cao & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Striking the Balance: How Variability Shapes Retrieval Practice and Worked Examples for Transfer Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the retrieval practice strategy of Experiment 2, varied items beat repeated items on the final test, "t(126) = − 2.16, p = .033"; no effect size was printed.

> "participants who engaged in varied retrieval practice performed significantly better than those who engaged in repeated retrieval practice, t(126) = − 2.16, p = .033."
