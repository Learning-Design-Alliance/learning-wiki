## Evidence

### Ibrahim Nahla Khamis 2014

Ibrahim Nahla Khamis, Banjar Shorooq, Al-Ghamdi Amal, Al-Darmasi Moroj, Khoja Abeer, Turkistani Jamela, Arif Rwan, Al-Sebyani Awatif, Musawa Al-anoud, Basfar Wijdan. (2014). Medical students' preference of problem-based learning or traditional lectures in King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2014.128

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the 460-student survey, the eight-item skills scale total was PBL 47.22 [10.65] vs lectures 33.0 [9.94], reported with t test = 20.93 (abstract: 20.9), P<.001. Self-reported perceptions, no effect size printed.

> "The mean total skill gained from PBL (47.2 [10.6]) was much higher than that of lectures (33.0 [9.9]), and a highly statistical significant difference was observed (t test=20.9, P<.001)."

### Ibrahim Nahla Khamis 2014 (2)

Ibrahim Nahla Khamis, Banjar Shorooq, Al-Ghamdi Amal, Al-Darmasi Moroj, Khoja Abeer, Turkistani Jamela, Arif Rwan, Al-Sebyani Awatif, Musawa Al-anoud, Basfar Wijdan. (2014). Medical students' preference of problem-based learning or traditional lectures in King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia. Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2014.128

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Table 3 of the same survey lists each skill item favouring PBL, e.g. critical thinking PBL 4.13 vs lectures 2.86 (t = 14.68, P<.001) and effective communication PBL 4.12 vs lectures 2.41 (t = 23.07, P<.001).

> "Table 3 shows that students preferred PBL more to traditional lectures for providing better learning skills, with the presence of highly statistical significant differences (P<.001)."
