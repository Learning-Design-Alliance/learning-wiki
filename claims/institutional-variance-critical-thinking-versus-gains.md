# Variance in first-year critical thinking is far more institutional (32.0%) than variance in self-reported gains (3.1-6.2%)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Null HLM models attributed roughly one third of critical thinking variance to institutions, but under 7% of variance in each self-reported gain scale. [→ Reason 2010](#reason-2010)

## Evidence

### Reason 2010

Reason, R. D., Cox, B. E., McIntosh, K., & Terenzini, P. T. (2010). Deep learning as an individual, conditional, and contextual influence on first-year student outcomes. Paper presented at the Annual Forum of the Association for Institutional Research, Chicago, IL. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED520486

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null (unconditional) random-intercept HLMs partitioned variance for the four outcomes; the level-2 component was statistically significant for all four, supporting the multilevel modeling approach.

> "roughly one third (32.0%; [9.081/(9.081+19.252)]) of the variance in critical thinking scores occurred between institutions (level 2). Institutional variance accounted for a much smaller percent of the overall variance for each of the three self-reported gain scales (Table 3): General Education (4.6%), Practical Competence (3.1%), and Personal and Social Development (6.2%)."
