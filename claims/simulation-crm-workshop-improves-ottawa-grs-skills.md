# Scores for all skills assessed by the Ottawa CRM Global Rating Scale rose from pre-test to post-test after a simulation-based CRM workshop in pediatric interprofessional teams, in a single-group pre-post study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ratings for all Ottawa GRS skills (leadership, problem solving, resource utilization, situational awareness, communication) were significantly higher after the workshop than before it, in one single-group pre-post study, with overall scores rising from 3.44 ± 0.92 to 6.00 ± 0.76 on the seven-point scale. [→ Saeed Sana 2024](#saeed-sana-2024)

## Evidence

### Saeed Sana 2024

Saeed Sana, Hegazy Nagwa Nashat, Malik Marib Ghulam Rasool, Abbas Qalab, Atiq Huba, Ali Muhammad Maisam, Aslam Aashir, Hashwani Yasmin, Ahmed Farzana Bashir. (2024). Transforming the delivery of care from “I” to “We” by developing the crisis resource management skills in pediatric interprofessional teams to handle common emergencies through simulation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05459-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Evaluator ratings on the seven-point Ottawa GRS (1 = novice, 7 = clearly excel) pre- and post-workshop; Table 4 reports overall mean rater scores rising from 3.44 ± 0.92 to 6.00 ± 0.76 with p < 0.001 and adjusted alpha = 0.008; no effect size printed.

> "The intervention was successful in significantly improving all skills assessed by the Ottawa GRS."
