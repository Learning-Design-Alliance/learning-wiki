# Principal stratification finds students with higher latent hint-requesting proclivity may have experienced larger CTA1 treatment effects, contradicting the other two analyses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A Bayesian principal stratification analysis stratifying all students by latent potential hint usage η(1) finds that students who would request more hints may have experienced larger overall treatment effects, in tension with the negative observational and mediation estimates. [→ Adam C Sales and John F Pane 2018](#adam-c-sales-and-john-f-pane-2018)

## Evidence

### Adam C Sales and John F Pane 2018

Adam C Sales and John F Pane. (2018). Student Log-Data from a Randomized Evaluation of Educational Technology: A Causal Case Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.02528](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.02528)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian principal stratification model (per Sales and Pane 2019a) using a latent Rasch hint-usage parameter η(1) defined for both treatment and control students; the article frames the result with hedging ("may have experienced") and notes τ(r) is only partially identified.

> "these analyses appear to give contradictory results—the observational study and mediation analysis estimate a negative eﬀect of requesting hints, while the principal stratiﬁcation analysis ﬁnds that students who requested more hints may have experienced larger overall treatment eﬀects."
