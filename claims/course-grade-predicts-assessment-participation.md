## Evidence

### Manher Jariwala 2018

Manher Jariwala, Jayson Nissen, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Participation rates of in-class vs. online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive participation rates by grade (Table I) show, for example, F-student PPT posttest participation at 22% versus A students at 89%. The HGLM found course grade a reliable predictor of participation in all four conditions; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "With the exception of the PPT pretest (which is a proxy for attendance on the second day of class), there is a substantial range of participation rates (>25%) across grades for A to F students (Table I)."

### Manher Jariwala 2018 (2)

Manher Jariwala, Jayson Nissen, Xochith Herrera, Eleanor W. Close, and Ben Van Dusen. (2018). Participation rates of in-class vs. online administration of low-stakes research-based assessments. PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.044)

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Student-level interaction terms between course grade and instructor practices in the HGLM. Coefficients of -0.04 and 0.07 were not statistically reliable (p>0.05), a negligible printed effect.

> "the effects were small and inconsistent (-0.04 and 0.07) and were not statistically reliable (p>0.05), indicating that using more recommended practices did not differentially motivate high and low performing students."
