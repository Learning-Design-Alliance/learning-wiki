# Attendance mailings corrected guardians' miscalibrated beliefs about their students' absences but did not change beliefs about the importance of attendance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Guardians receiving Norms and/or Self components improved accuracy about their students' absences by 18% (p=.01), and Norms-condition guardians improved accuracy about relative absence by 14% (p<.01), but beliefs about the importance of attendance did not change (p-values>.4). ## Evidence

### Rogers and Feller 2016

Todd Rogers, Avi Feller. (2016). Parent Beliefs and Student Absences: Large Absence-Reduction Field Experiment. SREE Spring 2016 Conference Abstract. https://www.sree.org/conferences/2016/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Live phone survey of guardians after Mailing 5 (6/20/2015-6/25/2015) assessing internal validity and parental beliefs. Guardians in treatment arms recalled receiving attendance mail at more than twice the control rate (25% vs. 57% recall, p<.001).

> "accuracy improved by 18% (p=.01). Guardians in the Norms condition also grew more accurate (by 14% [p<.01]) in reporting that their student was absent more than the typical student in their student’s school and grade. However, we saw no indication that guardian beliefs about the importance of attendance changed (p-values>.4)."
