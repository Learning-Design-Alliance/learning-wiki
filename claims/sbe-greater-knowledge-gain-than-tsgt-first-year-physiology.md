## Evidence

### Channegowda Nalini Yelahanka 2025

Channegowda Nalini Yelahanka, Pai Dinker Ramanand, Manivasakan Shivasakthy. (2025). Simulation-based teaching versus traditional small group teaching for first-year medical students among high and low scorers in respiratory physiology, India: a randomized controlled trial. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2025.22.8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Block-randomized controlled trial with 107 first-year medical students (intervention n=55, control n=52) using paired t-tests. Intervention pre-test 23.49±6.62, post-test 26.71±7.83, t=–2.64 (P=0.01); control 24.17±6.21 to 25.77±6.51, t=–1.48 (P=0.14).

> "The post-test scores of the intervention group were significantly higher than the pre-test scores, whereas the control group’s post-test scores, although higher than their pre-test scores, did not show a statistically significant difference"

### Channegowda Nalini Yelahanka 2025 (2)

Channegowda Nalini Yelahanka, Pai Dinker Ramanand, Manivasakan Shivasakthy. (2025). Simulation-based teaching versus traditional small group teaching for first-year medical students among high and low scorers in respiratory physiology, India: a randomized controlled trial. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2025.22.8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent t-test comparing knowledge gain between groups: intervention gain 3.22±1.2 versus control gain 1.60±0.3, t=9.38 (P=0.01), as reported in the trial's results table.

> "A comparison of knowledge gain (post-test minus pre-test scores) between the intervention and control groups revealed a statistically significant difference using an independent t-test"
