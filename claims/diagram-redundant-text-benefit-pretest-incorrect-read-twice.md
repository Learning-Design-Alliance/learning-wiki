# For students who answered the pretest incorrectly, diagrams helped only in the read-twice control condition and only when the diagram information was also presented in the text

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A diagram condition × questioning condition interaction occurred for pretest-incorrect students: diagram condition affected posttest performance in the read-twice control condition (d = .63) but not in the questioning conditions, and the benefit required redundant text. [→ Clinton 2013](#clinton-2013)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2013

Clinton, V., Alibali, M. W., & Nathan, M. J. (2013). The effects of diagrams and questioning-while-reading on learning from a statistics lesson. In Martinez, M. & Castro Superfine, A. (Eds.), Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Chicago, IL: University of Illinois at Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584483

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA interaction in the randomized experiment, pretest-incorrect subgroup (N = 198). In the read-twice control, "participants in the diagram with redundant text condition had higher scores than did participants in the text-only condition"; no reliable benefit of diagram without redundant text.

> "However, there was an interaction between diagram condition and questioning condition, F(2, 196) = 3.04, p = .02, such that diagram condition affected performance in the read-twice control condition, t(61) = 2.56, p = .04, Cohen’s d = .63, but not the other questioning conditions."
