## Evidence

### Emily M. Stump 2023

Emily M. Stump, Matthew Dew, Gina Passante, and N. G. Holmes. (2023). Context affects student thinking about sources of uncertainty in classical and quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020157)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey comfort data (Fig. 3): about 25% of respondents for Stern-Gerlach and Brownian motion reported being uncomfortable, but "there was not a sta- tistically significant difference in the number of sources they listed relative to their comfortable counterparts." Equivalence was not otherwise tested.

> "Although these students were likely less familiar with the experiments from their previous course experience, there was not a sta- tistically significant difference in the number of sources they listed relative to their comfortable counterparts."

### Emily M. Stump 2023 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Appendix C analysis (Fig. 8) checking for carryover from the randomly assigned upper-level scenario to generic-question responses: "no distinguishable differences in students’ listing of limitations and principles based on what upper-level experiment students saw."

> "We observed no distinguishable differences in students’ listing of limitations and principles based on what upper-level experiment students saw"
