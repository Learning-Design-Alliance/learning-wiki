## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Thomaes 2020

Thomaes, Tjaarda, Brummelman, and Sedikides. (2020). Effort Self‐Talk Benefits the Mathematics Performance of Children With Negative Competence Beliefs. Child Development, 91(6), 2211–2220. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13347

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical regression in the same randomized field experiment (N = 212) testing the effort versus ability self-talk contrast: its interaction with competence beliefs "was, t = −2.084, p = .038, b = −.064".

> "the analogous interaction involving the effort versus ability self‐talk contrast was, t = −2.084, p = .038, b = −.064 (Figure 1)."

### Thomaes 2020 (2)

Thomaes, Tjaarda, Brummelman, and Sedikides. (2020). Effort Self‐Talk Benefits the Mathematics Performance of Children With Negative Competence Beliefs. Child Development, 91(6), 2211–2220. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13347

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple slopes for the effort versus ability contrast: effort self-talk "benefited the performance of children holding negative competence beliefs" (1 SD below the mean) but not children holding positive competence beliefs.

> "Engaging in effort self‐talk (compared to ability self‐talk) benefited the performance of children holding negative competence beliefs (1 SD below the mean, t = 2.798, p = .006, b = .215), but not that of children holding positive competence beliefs"
