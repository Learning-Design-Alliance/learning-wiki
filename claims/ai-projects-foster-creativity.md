## Evidence

### Nathan Wang 2022

Nathan Wang, Paul Tonko, Nikil Ragav, Michael Chungyoun, and Jonathan Plucker. (2022). A Perspective on K-12 AI Education. Technology and Innovation, Vol. 22. http://dx.doi.org/10.21300/23.1.2023.2

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument: because designing an AI model involves feature selection, data pre-processing, algorithm choice such as ensemble learning, and integration into broader solutions, AI projects offer many design decisions and thus "make it easier to do something original".

> "In general, the capacity for originality is proportional to the multiplicity of potential design decisions. AI projects present students many unique avenues for solving a problem and thus make it easier to do something original."

### Nathan Wang 2022 (2)

Nathan Wang, Paul Tonko, Nikil Ragav, Michael Chungyoun, and Jonathan Plucker. (2022). A Perspective on K-12 AI Education. Technology and Innovation, Vol. 22. http://dx.doi.org/10.21300/23.1.2023.2

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument building on AI's versatility: AI projects are framed as "an effective enrichment activity that reinforces class topics with real-world applications" rather than an excursion from class time; humanities examples include analyzing GPT-3 chatbot outputs.

> "Hence, one can readily find relevant and task-appropriate AI applications in math, biology, chemistry, and physics. Thus, making time for AI projects in a STEM course is not an inappropriate excursion from class time but an effective enrichment activity that reinforces class topics with real-world applications."
