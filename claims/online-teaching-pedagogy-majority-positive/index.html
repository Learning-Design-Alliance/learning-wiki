Sidi Yael 2023¶

Sidi Yael, Shamir-Inbal Tamar, Eshet-Alkalai Yoram. (2023). From face-to-face to online: Teachers' perceived experiences in online distance teaching during the Covid-19 pandemic. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104831

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative interview analysis, pedagogical category (N=156, 33% of statements). Positive statements referenced "diversity in teaching strategies (25%)" and effectiveness (26%); negative statements (57, 36%) covered ineffectiveness (19%), assessment difficulty (10%), and techno-pedagogical challenges (8%).

"The analysis showed that the majority of respondents (N = 99; 64%) perceived online distance teaching as an opportunity to diversify teaching methods"

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers' concerns in online distance teaching fall into five categories — pedagogical, social, emotional, cognitive, and system support — identified from grounded-theory analysis of interviews. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Cognitive experiences of online teaching were mixed: 53% of statements reported workload and stimulus overload while 47% expressed a sense of self-efficacy. — related
  • Emotional experiences in online distance teaching were dominated by negatives — frustration, sadness, and detachment driven by lack of student feedback and visual contact. — related
  • System support was only marginally present in teachers' accounts, and most teachers who raised it felt they did not receive proper support. — related
