## Evidence

### Gupta 2026

Gupta, M.W., Pan, S.C., & Rickard, T.C. (2026). Semantic relatedness and the efficacy of retrieval practice. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00416-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Model-based ANOVA on observed vs. predicted test performance (Data Type × Relatedness) in the cued-recall experiment; the interaction was significant, F(1, 229) = 16.2, p < 0.0001. Pairwise tests showed "a robust difference in the high relatedness group" but none in the low group.

> "There was a non -significant difference in the low relatedness group, t(117) = 1.42, p = .16, BF01 = 3.65, but a robust difference in the high relatedness group,  t(112) = -4.74, p < .0001, BF10 = 2411. In summary, there wa s evidence from the model fits that the efficacy of testing is reduced in the high relatedness group compared to the low relatedness group."

### Gupta 2026 (2)

Gupta, M.W., Pan, S.C., & Rickard, T.C. (2026). Semantic relatedness and the efficacy of retrieval practice. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00416-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' summary of the mean-level model misfit in the high relatedness group: "the efficacy of testing was reduced by about 26%" relative to the dual-memory model's predicted mean testing effect. No standardized effect size is printed for this reduction.

> "Nevertheless, the efficacy of testing was reduced by about 26% in the high relatedness group (based on the difference between mea n TE in that group and the model’s predicted mean)"
