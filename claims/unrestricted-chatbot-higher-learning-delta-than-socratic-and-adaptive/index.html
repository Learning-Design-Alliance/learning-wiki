Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026¶

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.00584

q2 · i3 · causal · r2

Controlled experiment with 50 participants randomized by sequential allocation to three modes (17 Full, 17 Socratic, 16 Adaptive). One-way ANOVA on per-participant mean delta showed a significant main effect of mode (F(2,47)=3.948, p=.026); Welch's t-tests gave a large effect of "𝑑= 0.80(large effect)" for Full vs. Socratic and d = 0.85 for Full vs. Adaptive.

"Full vs. Socratic: 𝑡= 2.345,𝑝=. 025,𝑑= 0.80(large effect). Participants with unrestricted access achieved significantly higher deltas than those in the Socratic condition."

Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026 (2)¶

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Linear mixed model on all 500 per-question learning-delta change scores with participant as random intercept; Socratic/Full estimate = -9.60, p=.009; Adaptive/Full estimate = -8.92, p=.017; Adaptive/Socratic estimate = 0.68, p=.855. No effect size printed for the LMM contrasts.

"The LMM confirms the same pattern as the ANOVA/Welch analyses : the Full condition significantly outperforms the 2 other conditions, while Socratic and Adaptive do not differ significantly."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Intelligent tutoring systems improve achievement over non-individualised instruction — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • The adaptive mode showed a progressive per-question learning-delta increase across the session (from Δ = 3.16 on Q1 to Δ = 19.97 on Q9), suggesting a warm-up effect as participants learned the system — related
  • No significant difference in perceived learning across the three modes (p = .845), despite the unrestricted mode's nearly double objective learning delta — related
  • Socratic-mode participants progressively disengaged from the chatbot: message counts declined after question 5 and EEG engagement fell to its lowest value at question 10 — related
  • Socratic participants required nearly twice as many attempts to reach the 80% mastery threshold as Full participants (d = -2.01), and 58% of their abandoned exercises never reached the threshold — related
  • The EEG-adaptive exercise condition produced significantly higher cognitive engagement during training than the unrestricted chatbot condition (p = .018), and was the only condition above the 0.5 baseline — related
  • Most unrestricted-mode interactions were direct answer retrieval: 52.9% of exercise interactions were copy-paste of the question, and participant answers were linguistically near-identical to chatbot text in 25.3% of cases — related
  • Guidance, immediate feedback, and system-directed control showed unadjusted moderator signals that did not survive multiplicity adjustment, making them hypothesis-generating only — related
  • The customized agent's advantage in short-term task improvement emerges under directive feedback but not under Socratic feedback — related
  • Socratic feedback is associated with higher task orientation and comprehension monitoring than directive feedback; on feedback regulation it has no main effect, but its effect depends on agent type — reports the opposite
  • Socratic dialogue by a skilled but inefficient teaching assistant underperformed: lengthy, unsummarized checkpoints may leave students without clear understanding — related
  • Prompt-literacy guidance was associated with higher transcript-coded rule-following compliance than unrestricted LLM access, mainly in process-over-answer and stepwise-hint behaviors — related
