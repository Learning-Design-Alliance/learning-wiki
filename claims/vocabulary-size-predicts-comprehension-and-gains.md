# Learners' vocabulary size is significantly and positively related to both L2 video comprehension and incidental vocabulary learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Vocabulary size had a large positive effect on comprehension scores (ηp² = .18). [→ Montero Perez 2014](#montero-perez-2014)
`q3 i3` Vocabulary size was significantly related to vocabulary learning with a very large effect (ηp² = .37), exceeding the effect of captioning type for meaning recognition and recall. [→ Montero Perez 2014 (2)](#montero-perez-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Montero Perez 2014

Montero Perez, M., Peters, E., Clarebout, G., & Desmet, P. (2014). Effects of Captioning on Video Comprehension and Incidental Vocabulary Learning. Language Learning & Technology 18(1), 118–141. http://llt.msu.edu/issues/february2014/monteroperezetal.pdf

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Covariate effect in the comprehension MANCOVA with 131 participants; positive b-values indicated "the larger one's vocabulary, the better the comprehension score."

> "The covariate vocabulary size was significantly related to the comprehension scores, Wilks's lambda, F(3, 124) = 8.81, p < .001, and had a large effect: ηp² = .18."

### Montero Perez 2014 (2)

Montero Perez, M., Peters, E., Clarebout, G., & Desmet, P. (2014). Effects of Captioning on Video Comprehension and Incidental Vocabulary Learning. Language Learning & Technology 18(1), 118–141. http://llt.msu.edu/issues/february2014/monteroperezetal.pdf

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Covariate effect in the vocabulary MANCOVA; b-values indicated a positive relationship — "the larger one's vocabulary, the more vocabulary gains" — with effect sizes for vocabulary size exceeding those of captioning type for the deeper knowledge components.

> "Vocabulary size was significantly related to vocabulary learning, Wilks's lambda, F(4, 114) = 16.64, p < .001, ηp² = .37."
