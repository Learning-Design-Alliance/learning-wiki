# The ST Math evaluation's quasi-experimental design cannot rule out selection bias because treatment grades volunteered to participate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 causal `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` The authors state that because principals at schools with treatment grades volunteered to implement ST Math, unmeasured factors such as a greater focus on mathematics achievement may have contributed to the observed improvements, an issue amplified for the full-implementation sample. [→ Staci Wendt 2014](#staci-wendt-2014)

`q1 i?` The authors caution that districts and schools opted into ST Math, so unmeasured underlying factors such as an emphasis on improving math or technology integration may have contributed to the score improvements. [→ Wendt 2019](#wendt-2019)

## Evidence

### Staci Wendt 2014

Staci Wendt, John Rice, Jonathan Nakamoto. (2014). Evaluation of the MIND Research Institute's Spatial-Temporal Math (ST Math) Program in California. WestEd. https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/elsi/

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' stated evaluation limitation (type e), not a tested result: the evaluation's matched-comparison design relied on volunteer treatment schools, and the article names possible unmeasured differences such as "a greater focus on mathematics achievement" as threats to causal interpretation.

> "because grades that were provided with ST Math elected to participate in the program, there may have been factors other than participating in ST Math (such as a greater focus on mathematics achievement) that contributed to improvements in mathematics outcomes in these grades. This is even more of an issue for the sample of grades that fully implemented ST Math because these schools not only volunteered to participate in ST Math, but these grades also chose (or were able) to fully implement the program."

### Wendt 2019

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' stated limitation in the Discussion section: without randomization, group differences on unmeasured characteristics impede causal conclusion, and fidelity schools may differ in teacher quality, principal support, or concurrent schoolwide math reforms.

> "One limitation is that schools that participated in the ST Math program, or that implemented it with fidelity, may have been different in some unknown way(s) in relation to the comparison schools."
