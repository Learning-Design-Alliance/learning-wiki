# Presenting supportive information during learning task clusters tended to yield higher quality knowledge construction than presenting it before the clusters

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who received supportive information during the learning task clusters tended to show higher quality of knowledge construction than students who received it before each cluster, though the difference was only marginal (p = 0.07). [→ Noroozi 2012](#noroozi-2012)

## Evidence

### Noroozi 2012

Noroozi, O., Busstra, M. C., Mulder, M., Biemans, H. J. A., Tobi, H., Geelen, A., van't Veer, P., Chizari, M. (2012). Online discussion compensates for suboptimal timing of supportive information presentation in a digitally supported learning environment. Education Tech Research Dev (2012) 60:193-221. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-011-9217-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on test 1 scores in a randomized experiment with 87 students (84 analyzed) in a real course setting at Wageningen University, comparing information-before (IB) and information-during (ID) conditions. The article reports the difference only 'tended to' favor ID, with F (1, 79) = 3.34; p = 0.07 and means of 2.92 (IB) versus 3.07 (ID).

> "Timing of supportive information presentation tended to inﬂuence quality of knowledge construction: F (1, 79) = 3.34; p = 0.07. The average quality of knowledge construction tended to be higher for students who received supportive information during (ID) the learning task clusters than for students who received this information before (IB) the learning task clusters (M IB = 2.92; SDIB = 0.34; MID = 3.07; SDID = 0.32)."
