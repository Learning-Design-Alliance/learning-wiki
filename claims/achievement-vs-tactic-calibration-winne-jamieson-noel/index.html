Evidence¶

Rovers 2019¶

Rovers, S. F. E., Clarebout, G., Savelberg, H. H. C. M., de Bruin, A. B. H., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2019). Granularity matters: comparing different ways of measuring self-regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09188-6

q2 · i3 · review · r2

Review reports Winne and Jamieson-Noel (2002), in which students studied a lightning-formation chapter in PrepMate and achievement calibration was assessed against traced study tactics; median calibration r = .88 with variability across items.

"It was found that despite a consistent general tendency for overconfidence, students were quite well calibrated in terms of their achievement, with a median calibration of r= .88"

Rovers 2019 (2)¶

Rovers, S. F. E., Clarebout, G., Savelberg, H. H. C. M., de Bruin, A. B. H., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2019). Granularity matters: comparing different ways of measuring self-regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09188-6

q2 · i? · review · r2

Same review-reported study: tactic-report calibration showed no relation to achievement whereas achievement calibration and prior knowledge did; the review interprets this as two forms of calibration tapping different constructs.

"calibration of study tactics was not related to achievement, while prior knowledge and calibration of achievement were in fact related to achievement"
