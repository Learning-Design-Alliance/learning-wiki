## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors interpret that a disconnect may exist between managers' self‐assessment of competence and staff experiences, so self‐assessments alone cannot reliably assess competence. [→ Karsikas 2025](#karsikas-2025)

## Evidence

### Karsikas 2025

Karsikas, E., Meriläinen, M., Koivunen, K., & Kanste, O. (2025). Health and social care managers' self‐assessed competence in knowledge management: A descriptive cross‐sectional study. Journal of Advanced Nursing, 81, 787–797. https://doi.org/10.1111/jan.16237

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, citing Lunden et al. (2021) that nearly 40% of nurses occasionally felt they performed tasks for which their competence was insufficient; no new data test this interpretation in the study.

> "In any case, it appears that a person's competence cannot be reliably assessed solely from their self‐assessments. Therefore, the development of additional methods beyond self‐assessment is necessary to obtain more trustworthy information about managers' competence in KM."
