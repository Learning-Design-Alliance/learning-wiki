# The concept model of vocabulary instruction produced larger gains in content-area vocabulary test performance than the definition/sentence-writing model for all six middle school students with learning disabilities

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across six students with learning disabilities, mean percent correct on weekly vocabulary tests rose from 63.75% under the definition model to 90.67% under the concept model, a 42.23% increase. [→ Fore III 2007](#fore-iii-2007)

## Evidence

### Fore III 2007

Fore III, C., Boon, R. T., & Lowrie, K. (2007). Vocabulary Instruction for Middle School Students with Learning Disabilities: A Comparison of Two Instructional Models. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 5(2), 49-73. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ797664

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple-baseline-across-participants study of six seventh-grade students with LD in resource language arts classes; weekly 10-item matching tests scored as percent correct. The article reports "an increase of 42.23%" in group mean from baseline (definition model) to intervention (concept model); no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the mean score for all students during baseline was 63.75% correct. The mean score for all students during intervention was 90.67% correct, which was an increase of 42.23%."
