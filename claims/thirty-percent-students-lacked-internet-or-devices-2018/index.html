q2 · i? · review · r2

The report cites a Common Sense Media and Boston Consulting Group report using 2018 census data, stating that 'roughly 30% of the 50 million k–12 students' lacked internet or devices, and that of these, 'nearly two thirds lacked both high-speed internet and a usable device.'

"Based on data from the 2018 census, roughly 30% of the 50 million k–12 students in the United States lacked either high-speed internet or devices with the capacity they need for easy access to digital learning at home."

q2 · i? · review · r2

A spring 2020 national ParentsTogether survey of parents, reported in the Priority 1 section, found low-income parents 'nearly 10 times more likely to say their children were doing little or no remote learning than those from affluent homes (38% vs. 4%).' No effect size is printed.

"13% of parents from low-income homes (earning less than $25,000 annually) reported lacking devices or internet connections, and they were nearly 10 times more likely to say their children were doing little or no remote learning than those from affluent homes (38% vs. 4%)."
