# Students rating learning conditions most positively are more than twice as likely to earn a B or better in math than students rating them most negatively

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students averaging in the Strongly Agree range across learning conditions are more than two times as likely to earn a B or better in math than students in the Strongly Disagree range. [→ Gripshover 2022](#gripshover-2022)
`q2 i?` Each increment in the composite learning conditions score is associated with 6% more students earning A's and B's, on average. [→ Gripshover 2022 (2)](#gripshover-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Gripshover 2022

Gripshover, S., Londerée, A., Ahuvia, I., Shyjka, A., Kroshinsky, F., Ryan, N., Farrington, C., & Paunesku, D. (2022). Learning Conditions Are an Actionable, Early Indicator of Math Learning. PERTS & University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publication/learning-conditions-are-an-actionable-early-indicator-of-math-learning

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of survey and grade data from over 4,000 U.S. 8th–12th graders collected October–March of 2019–20. The report states students in the “Strongly Agree” range were “more than two times as likely to earn a B or better” than those in the “Strongly Disagree” range; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Students who average in the “Strongly Agree” range across all learning conditions—indicating strongly positive learning conditions—are more than two times as likely to earn a B or better in math that term compared with students in the “Strongly Disagree” range."

### Gripshover 2022 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 1 caption reporting the per-increment association between the composite learning conditions score and the percent of students earning A's or B's in math; descriptive, no test statistic printed.

> "Each increment in the composite score is associated with 6% more students earning A’s and B’s, on average."
