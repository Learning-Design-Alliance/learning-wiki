## Evidence

### KC Ashish 2017

KC Ashish, Wrammert Johan, Nelin Viktoria, Clark Robert B., Ewald Uwe, Peterson Stefan, Målqvist Mats. (2017). Evaluation of Helping Babies Breathe Quality Improvement Cycle (HBB-QIC) on retention of neonatal resuscitation skills six months after training in Nepal. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-017-0853-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Logistic regression on the 137-worker cohort found pre-birth resuscitation preparation gave a "two-fold increased likelihood of retaining the skills" (RR-2.4, 95% CI 1.0–5.6), a confidence interval touching 1.0; only the relative risk is printed.

> "Health workers who prepared for resuscitation before every birth had a two-fold increased likelihood of retaining the skills than those who did not (RR-2.4, 95% CI 1.0–5.6)."

### KC Ashish 2017 (2)

KC Ashish, Wrammert Johan, Nelin Viktoria, Clark Robert B., Ewald Uwe, Peterson Stefan, Målqvist Mats. (2017). Evaluation of Helping Babies Breathe Quality Improvement Cycle (HBB-QIC) on retention of neonatal resuscitation skills six months after training in Nepal. BMC Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-017-0853-5

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same regression found weekly review meeting attendance carried a "2.6 times increased likelihood of retaining their skills" (RR-2.6, 95% CI 1.0–7.4); the unadjusted Table 6 comparison was p = 0.065 and only the relative risk is printed.

> "Health workers that attended weekly meetings had a 2.6 times increased likelihood of retaining their skills than those who did not attend (RR-2.6, 95% CI 1.0–7.4)."
