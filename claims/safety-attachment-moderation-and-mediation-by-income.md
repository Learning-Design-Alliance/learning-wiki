# Perceived neighborhood safety is protective for school attachment among low-income youth in high-disorder neighborhoods, and mediates the affluence-school attachment link only among non-low-income youth

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For low-income youth, perceived neighborhood safety interacted with physical disorder to predict school attachment (beta = .24, p < .05), with high perceived safety associated with higher attachment in high-disorder neighborhoods. [→ Martin-Storey 2014](#martin-storey-2014)
`q3 i?` A significant indirect association between neighborhood affluence and school attachment via neighborhood safety was observed only among adolescents whose families were not low-income (β = .03, p < .01, CI [.01, .07]). [→ Martin-Storey 2014 (2)](#martin-storey-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Martin-Storey 2014

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction probed in multi-group SEM (n = 199 low-income; n = 725 other): high perceived neighborhood safety was associated with higher school attachment for low-income youth in neighborhoods with high levels of physical disorder. This interaction was not found for non-low-income youth.

> "When school attachment was the outcome, a significant interaction was observed for youth from low-income families (beta = .24, p < .05)."

### Martin-Storey 2014 (2)

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped mediation models estimated by family income group: the indirect effect via perceived safety was significant only in the non-low-income group; no other indirect effects were observed, and none were observed in the full sample without moderators.

> "A significant indirect association was observed between neighborhood affluence and school attachment (β = .03, p < .01, CI [.01, .07]), via neighborhood safety, among adolescents whose families were not low-income."
