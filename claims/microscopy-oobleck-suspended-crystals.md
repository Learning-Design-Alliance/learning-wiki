## Evidence

### D. R. Dounas-Frazer 2012

D. R. Dounas-Frazer, J. Lynn, A. M. Zaniewski, and N. Roth. (2012). Learning about non-Newtonian fluids in a student-driven classroom. [doi:10.1119/1.4772035](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4772035)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Classroom microscopy session following a hypothesis-forming discussion of microscopic differences between oobleck and corn syrup. The article states students "observed that corn starch crystals were not dissolved but suspended in the water".

> "After forming hypothe-ses of what they expected to see, students exam-ined a thin sample of oobleck under a microscope. They observed that corn starch crystals were not dis-solved but suspended in the water, as can be seen in Fig. 4."

### D. R. Dounas-Frazer 2012 (2)

D. R. Dounas-Frazer, J. Lynn, A. M. Zaniewski, and N. Roth. (2012). Learning about non-Newtonian fluids in a student-driven classroom. [doi:10.1119/1.4772035](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4772035)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

During agitation of the microscope sample, students observed packing and jamming of the crystals, which the article names as "two mechanisms for the shear-thickening nature of suspensions".

> "Packing and jamming of the crystals, two mechanisms for the shear-thickening nature of sus-pensions [2], were observed by agitating the sample."
