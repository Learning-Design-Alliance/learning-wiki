# Copying was sensitive to described demonstrator ability: participants copied choices of Best demonstrators more than Worst demonstrators

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was an interaction between Description and Demonstrator choice, with a higher tendency to select the same choice as the demonstrator when it was described as Best. [→ Selbing 2025](#selbing-2025)
`q2 i?` The three-way interaction with Block was not significant, so copying behavior did not change systematically over the experiment. [→ Selbing 2025 (2)](#selbing-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Selbing 2025

Selbing, I., Becker, N., Pan, Y., Lindström, B., & Olsson, A. (2025). Effects of described demonstrator ability on brain and behavior when learning from others. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00292-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc GLMM on 38 participants' choices adding Demonstrator choice and its interaction with Description. Adding the interaction improved model fit (p = 0.025) and produced β = 0.71, Z = 3.30, p < 0.001; copying was inefficient because demonstrators behaved randomly.

> "there was an interaction between Description and Demonstrator choice (β = 0.71, SE = 0.22, Z = 3.30, p< 0.001) caused by a higher tendency to select the same choice as the demonstrator following observation of a demonstrator described as Best compared to Worst"

### Selbing 2025 (2)

Selbing, I., Becker, N., Pan, Y., Lindström, B., & Olsson, A. (2025). Effects of described demonstrator ability on brain and behavior when learning from others. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00292-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up GLMM testing the three-way Description × Demonstrator choice × Block interaction on the same sample found no evidence that the copying effect changed over time.

> "Our analysis did not show any such interaction with Block (p = 0.61). Participants thus did not appear to change their behavior systematically over the course of the experiment."
