# Contextualizing teacher self-report personality items with an at-school frame of reference yields little predictive benefit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contextualized versus non-contextualized self-reports did not differ significantly in correlations with effectiveness measures for 38 of 40 analyses, and the two significant differences ran opposite to the hypothesis. [→ Kim 2018](#kim-2018)
`q2 i?` Contextualized and non-contextualized self-reports did not differ in correlation with mean student-reports for nine of ten analyses, so H1b was also not supported. [→ Kim 2018 (2)](#kim-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Kim 2018

Kim, Lisa, Dar-Nimrod, Ilan and MacCann, Carolyn (2018) Teacher Personality and Teacher Effectiveness in Secondary School: Personality Predicts Teacher Support and Student Self-Efficacy but not Academic Achievement. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 309-323. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000217

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Steiger's z-tests comparing level-2 correlations of contextualized and non-contextualized teacher self-reports with all effectiveness measures (H1a). The two exceptions favored the non-contextualized form (English academic support for conscientiousness, z = -2.21; English PSE for agreeableness, z = -2.31); "Overall, H1a was not supported".

> "Contrary to our hypothesis, differences between contextualized and non-contextualized self-reports were not significant for 38 of the 40 analyses."

### Kim 2018 (2)

Kim, Lisa, Dar-Nimrod, Ilan and MacCann, Carolyn (2018) Teacher Personality and Teacher Effectiveness in Secondary School: Personality Predicts Teacher Support and Student Self-Efficacy but not Academic Achievement. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 309-323. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000217

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Steiger's z-tests comparing the two self-report frames' correlations with mean student-reports of teacher personality (H1b). Only mathematics openness favored contextualization; the article concludes "little benefit of contextualizing self-reports".

> "There were no significant differences in the strengths of the correlations for nine of the ten analyses. The exception was mathematics teachers' openness, where contextualized self-reports were more strongly related to mean student-reports than non-contextualized self-reports (z = 2.06, p < .05)."
