# AI chatbot access lowers teachers' perceived cognitive effort during learning design

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers reported significantly lower perceived cognitive effort when designing learning activities with AI access than without AI. [→ Pishtari 2027](#pishtari-2027)

## Evidence

### Pishtari 2027

Pishtari, G., Gnadlinger, F., Ley, T. (2027). When Teachers Use AI Chatbots and Are Trained for It: Impact on Learning Design Quality and Cognitive Effort. In: Blanchard, E.G., Chen, G., Chi, M., Isotani, S. (eds) Artificial Intelligence in Education. AIED 2026. Lecture Notes in Computer Science, vol 16585. Springer, Cham. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29770-9_28

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-subjects comparison of cognitive-effort measures across conditions without AI and with AI access among thirteen higher education teachers. The article reports a statistically significant lowering of perceived cognitive effort; no effect size is printed in the available text.

> "Results show that AI access improved design quality and significantly lowered perceived cognitive effort."
