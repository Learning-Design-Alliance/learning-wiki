# Students of color show fewer absences and less exclusionary discipline when taught by race-matched teachers

## Evidence

### Carver-Thomas 2018

Carver-Thomas, D. (2018). Diversifying the teaching profession: How to recruit and retain teachers of color. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/diversifying-teaching-profession [doi:10.54300/559.310](https://doi.org/10.54300/559.310)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The review reports a longitudinal study of North Carolina k–5 students and teachers, 2006–2010, comparing race-matched and non-matched assignments on absences. It adds that boys of color with White teachers were more likely to be chronically absent and suspended.

> "A study using longitudinal data on North Carolina k–5 students and teachers between 2006 and 2010, found that students with teachers of another race had more unexcused absences and an increased likelihood of being chronically absent than students with race-matched teachers."

### Carver-Thomas 2018 (2)

Carver-Thomas, D. (2018). Diversifying the teaching profession: How to recruit and retain teachers of color. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/diversifying-teaching-profession. [doi:10.54300/559.310](https://doi.org/10.54300/559.310)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The review reports a study of North Carolina elementary, middle, and high school students finding lower exclusionary discipline for Black students taught by more Black teachers, including for subjective incidents like "willful defiance." Non-Black students also showed lower discipline likelihood, though less extremely.

> "Black students with more Black teachers were less likely to experience exclusionary discipline; that is, suspension and expulsion."
