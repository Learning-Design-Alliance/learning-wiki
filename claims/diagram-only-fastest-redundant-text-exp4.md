# A diagram-only format is processed faster than integrated or conventional formats when text is redundant, and integrated formats are processed faster than conventional split-source formats

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The diagram-only group required significantly less instruction time than the conventional and modified groups studying a mixed circuit. [→ Chandler 1991](#chandler-1991)

## Evidence

### Chandler 1991

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Laboratory experiment with 20 first-year apprentices randomly allocated to diagram-only, conventional, or modified groups studying mixed-circuit wiring. The short instruction time for the diagram-only group is consistent with reduced cognitive load from attending to a diagram only.

> "The diagram-only group required significantly less time to process the instructions than did the other two groups, t(27) = 3.08, SEdijf = 14.43."

### Chandler 1991 (2)

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Planned contrasts using an ANOVA compared instruction times across the three groups. Although the modified group had an integrated format requiring no mental integration, they still had to process redundant written material, so their instruction time exceeded the diagram-only group's while remaining below the conventional group's.

> "The modified instructions group spent significantly less time processing instructions than the conventional instructions group, t(27) = 4.34, SEdiff = 24.99."
