Evidence¶

Jake O'Brien 2026¶

Jake O'Brien, Margaret Ellis, Alma Robinson, John Simonetti, and Naren Ramakrishnan. (2026). Skepticism vs. Convenience: Physics Students' Perceptions and Use of Large Language Models Before and After Instruction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21037 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.21037

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Pre/post comparison using Mann-Whitney U tests on unmatched survey responses (53 pre, 42 post). The article reports these shifts as statistically significant in Table II but prints no effect size within this passage; the shifts are treated as hypothesis-gathering rather than confirmatory.

"We found increased agreement in usage-reduced-conceptual (from 63% to 85%), usage-dependence (from 81% to 88%), and usage-false-confidence (from 58% to 88%)."

Jake O'Brien 2026 (2)¶

Jake O'Brien, Margaret Ellis, Alma Robinson, John Simonetti, and Naren Ramakrishnan. (2026). Skepticism vs. Convenience: Physics Students' Perceptions and Use of Large Language Models Before and After Instruction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21037

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Post-survey Likert results for capability statements. Agreement that LLMs outperform the average physics student dropped from "54% agreement before the lesson and 32% agreement after the lesson"; the other two capability statements showed no significant shift. No effect size is printed in this passage.

"we only observe a shift toward disagreement in accuracy-above-students, which had 54% agreement before the lesson and 32% agreement after the lesson."
