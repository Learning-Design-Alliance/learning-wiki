## Evidence

### Cristian Raduta 2005

Cristian Raduta. (2005). General Students' Misconceptions Related to Electricity and Magnetism. The Ohio State University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132)

`q1 · i?` · `review · r1`

Survey with university level students in France and Sweden (Rainson et al., Ref. 16) on causal interpretation of relationships. Students interpret formulas as if the quantities on the right of the equal sign were the cause of those on the left. No effect size is printed.

> "In the case of Gauss's theorem, this suggests a response such as " to calculate the electric field, I only need the internal charges," or " the electric field is due only to the internal charges. " But the students fail to say anything about the charges situated in the exterior of a symmetrically charged sphere (more than 80% of students' responses were attributed this kind of reasoning)"

### Cristian Raduta 2005 (2)

Cristian Raduta. (2005). General Students' Misconceptions Related to Electricity and Magnetism. The Ohio State University. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503132)

`q1 · i?` · `review · r2`

Conclusions of the Rainson et al. survey (Ref. 16) on two named misconceptions: 'field if mobility' and 'cause in the formula'. The study also concluded that for most students, electrostatics and electric circuits are unconnected subjects. No effect size is printed.

> "Rainson et al. ( Ref. 16) conclude that the above misconceptions (" field if mobility "-in order to accept the existence of a field the student needs to see a motion; " cause in the formula "-an erroneous interpretation of a mathematical relationship: the quantities on the right side of one relation are seen as the cause for the quantities from the left side) are determinant for the difficulties"
