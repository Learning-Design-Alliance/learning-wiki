## Evidence

### Keller 2024

Keller, T. A., Mason, R. A., Legg, A. E., & Just, M. A. (2024). The neural and cognitive basis of expository text comprehension. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00232-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Whole-brain paired t-tests across the three passage repetitions among all participants showed overall activation increases between first and second reading in bilateral occipito-temporal cortex, left STS, and right temporal pole, with these same areas decreasing from second to third reading.

> "This inverted U-shaped function of activation in temporal language areas and the corresponding increase in lateral and medial frontal areas may reﬂect a shift from semantic to executive processing with repeated presentations."
