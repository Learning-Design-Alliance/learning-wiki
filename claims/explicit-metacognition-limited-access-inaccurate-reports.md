# Explicit metacognition has severely limited access to underlying cognitive processes, and metacognitive reports can be inaccurate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` People have little or no direct conscious access to higher order cognitive processes and can justify choices they did not actually make. [→ Frith Chris D 2012](#frith-chris-d-2012)

## Evidence

### Frith Chris D 2012

Frith Chris D. (2012). The role of metacognition in human social interactions. Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0123

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A choice-blindness experiment in which participants chose between two jams, then re-tasted and justified their choice; on some trials the rejected jam was secretly substituted. The review reports that on more than half of these occasions "the switch was not noticed and people justified a choice they had not actually made".

> "on some occasions, a trick was used so that participants were presented with the jam they had rejected. On more than half of these occasions, the switch was not noticed and people justified a choice they had not actually made"
