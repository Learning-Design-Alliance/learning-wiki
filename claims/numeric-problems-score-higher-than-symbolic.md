## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2011

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2011). How numbers help students solve physics problems. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Large-population final-exam study in a calculus-based introductory physics course: 765 students saw one of two versions of a question (Figure 3). The numeric version averaged "79.6%" versus 63.4% symbolic, "significant at the p< 0.001 level". No effect size is printed.

> "A total of 765 students saw one of the two versions of this question. The numeric version had a average score of 79.6%. The symbolic version had an average score of 63.4%. The 16.2% diﬀerence between the versions is signiﬁcant at the p< 0.001 level."
