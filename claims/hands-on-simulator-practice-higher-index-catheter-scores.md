## Evidence

### Waters Peadar S 2014

Waters Peadar S, McVeigh Terri, Kelly Brian D, Flaherty Gerard T, Devitt Dara, Barry Kevin, Kerin Michael J. (2014). The acquisition and retention of urinary catheterisation skills using surgical simulator devices: teaching method or student traits. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0264-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised three-group teaching study of 42 second-year medical students; index OSCE scores measured within 1 hour of teaching. The interactive group scored "74% ±6.09%, 68% ±3.84%, 60% ±9.14%" with p = 0.002; no effect size printed.

> "The interactive group (n = 19) had significantly higher scores at index examination compared to the observed (n = 12) and didactic (n = 11) teaching groups (74% ±6.09%, 68% ±3.84%, 60% ±9.14%, respectively p = 0.002"
