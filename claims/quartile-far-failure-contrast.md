# Upper-quartile and lower-quartile learning-gain players showed opposite correlations between cell-cycle activity and learning, and lower-quartile players had more far failures (7 vs 2.3 average cycles)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Upper-quartile players' cell starts correlated positively with gains (r = .7059) while lower-quartile players' correlated negatively (r = -.8641); lower-quartile players averaged 7 far-failure cycles versus 2.3 for upper-quartile players, with comparable total failures. ## Evidence

### Richard Halverson 2012

Richard Halverson, Elizabeth Owen, Nathan Wills, R. Benjamin Shapiro. (2012). Game-Based Assessment: An integrated model for capturing evidence of learning in play. ERIA Working Paper. http://www.eriainteractive.com/index.php

`q2 · i3` · `design · r2`

Quartile comparison of 10 upper-quartile students (averaging a 33% increase) and 10 lower-quartile students (averaging a 17% decrease) in pre-post gain (Table 4, α = .10). Far-failure cycles correlated positively with gains in the upper quartile (r = .5796) and negatively in the lower (r = -.9408).

> "On average, students in the lower quartile had 7 cycles of far failure, while upper quartile students only had 2.3 cycles. Since both groups had comparable total NUMBERS of failures, the lower quartile had a greater proportion of far failures;"
