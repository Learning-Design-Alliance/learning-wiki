## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Graduate and undergraduate parental education, compared with up-to-primary, showed higher adjusted odds of moderate and high perceived e-learning stress. [→ Kabir Humayun 2021](#kabir-humayun-2021)

## Evidence

### Kabir Humayun 2021

Kabir Humayun, Hasan Md. Kamrul, Mitra Dipak Kumar. (2021). E-learning readiness and perceived stress among the university students of Bangladesh during COVID-19: a countrywide cross-sectional study. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2021.2009908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Adjusted results of the multinomial logistic regression in the 1145-student cross-sectional survey; e.g. "graduate-level study: AOR = 2.44, 95% CI: 1.27–4.72" for moderate stress. No standardised effect size was printed. The authors suggest educated parents' higher expectations may explain the pattern.

> "Higher educational level of parents compared to primary level was more likely to perceive moderate (graduate-level study: AOR = 2.44, 95% CI: 1.27–4.72; undergraduate level study: AOR = 2.18, 95% CI: 1.26–3.76) and high level of e-learning stress (graduate-level study: AOR = 2.63, 95% CI: 1.12–6.18; undergraduate level study: AOR = 2.91, 95% CI: 1.45–5.86)."
