## Evidence

### Marsh 2008

Marsh, H. W., Seaton, M., Trautwein, U., Lüdtke, O., Hau, K. T., O'Mara, A. J., & Craven, R. G. (2008). The Big-fish–little-pond-effect Stands Up to Critical Scrutiny: Implications for Theory, Methodology, and Future Research. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-008-9075-6

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Multilevel reanalysis (Seaton et al. 2008c) of Blanton et al. (1999) data from 876 ninth-grade students in 33 classes across four Dutch schools, using streamed class-average ability. Individual-achievement effects varied from 0.32 to 0.78; the class-average effect "was significantly negative for all seven academic subjects, varying from −0.34 to −0.62." No standardised effect size is printed, so no magnitude is coded.

> "The negative effect of class-average ability was significantly negative for all seven academic subjects, varying from −0.34 to −0.62"

### Marsh 2008 (2)

Marsh, H. W., Seaton, M., Trautwein, U., Lüdtke, O., Hau, K. T., O'Mara, A. J., & Craven, R. G. (2008). The Big-fish–little-pond-effect Stands Up to Critical Scrutiny: Implications for Theory, Methodology, and Future Research. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-008-9075-6

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Multilevel reanalysis (Seaton et al. 2008c) of Huguet et al. (2001) data from 1,156 students in 51 classes across 12 French high schools, using class-average grades. The review reports the class-average effect "varied from −0.15 to −0.42, and was statistically significant for two of the three school subjects" (French and Math, not History/Geography). No standardised effect size is printed, so no magnitude is coded.

> "The negative effect of class-average ability (the BFLPE) varied from −0.15 to −0.42, and was statistically significant for two of the three school subjects"
