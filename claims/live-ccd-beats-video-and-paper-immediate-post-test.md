# Live clinical case discussions outperform video-recorded and printed case formats on an immediate clinical reasoning post-test

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a randomised controlled trial, the Live-CCD group scored higher on the knowledge application post-test than both the Video-CCD and Paper-Cases groups (partial η2=0.384). [→ Weidenbusch M 2019](#weidenbusch-m-2019)

## Evidence

### Weidenbusch M 2019

Weidenbusch M, Lenzer B, Sailer M, Strobel C, Kunisch R, Kiesewetter J, Fischer MR, Zottmann JM. (2019). Can clinical case discussions foster clinical reasoning skills in undergraduate medical education? A randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-025973

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Single-centre RCT with 90 medical students randomised to Live-CCD (n=30), Video-CCD (n=27) or Paper-Cases (n=33). The live format won on the immediate post-test, "F(2,87)=27.07, p<0.001, partial η2=0.384", with all pairwise Bonferroni differences significant.

> "Experimental groups differed significantly with respect to the knowledge application post-test (see table 1), F(2,87)=27.07, p<0.001, partial η2=0.384. The Live-CCD group (M=14.10; SD=3.32) outperformed both the Video-CCD (M=11.69; SD=3.34) and the Paper-Cases group (M=8.50; SD=2.44)."
