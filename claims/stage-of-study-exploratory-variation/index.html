Jason Zagami 2026¶

International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education 23:47. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00625-6

q2 · i1 · associational · r1

Exploratory undergraduate year-level analysis (Table 4, ρ = 0.170, p = 0.007). The article presents this as modest, suggesting later-year undergraduates may become somewhat more likely to report dependency-prone AI use.

"Within the undergraduate cohort, year level showed a positive association with AI-related disengagement, as shown in Table 4. No comparable year-level associations were observed for active engagement, positive affect, negative affect, or perceived positive learning impact."

Jason Zagami 2026 (2)¶

Jason Zagami. (2026). Guarded adoption of generative AI in higher education: high-achieving students, successful-student identity, and epistemic agency in a single-university mixed-methods survey. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education 23:47. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00625-6

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Exploratory GPA-by-cohort interaction models comparing undergraduate and postgraduate/research cohorts (Table 5 shows cohort-specific correlations, e.g. perceived learning impact ρ = −0.515 postgraduate vs −0.318 undergraduate). Only p values are printed for the interactions.

"Exploratory interaction models suggested that the GPA slope was significantly stronger in postgraduate or research students for active AI engagement ( p = .001) and perceived positive learning impact ( p = .002). No significant interaction was observed for positive affect."
