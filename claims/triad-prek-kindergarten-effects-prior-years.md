# In the project's earlier reported years, TRIAD students learned more mathematics than controls in pre-K (g = .72) and follow-through students outperformed controls in kindergarten (ITT g = .33)

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The article reports that in the pre-K year TRIAD students learned more mathematics than control students (g = .72), and that at kindergarten only the follow-through condition significantly outperformed control (g = .33, ITT). [→ Clements 2013](#clements-2013)

## Evidence

### Clements 2013

Clements, D. H., Sarama, J., Wolfe, C. B., & Spitler, M. E. (2013). Longitudinal Evaluation of a Scale-Up Model for Teaching Mathematics With Trajectories and Technologies: Persistence of Effects in the Third Year. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212469270

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

The article's review of its own previously reported evaluations; the pre-K result is attributed to Clements et al., 2011. A second entry quotes the kindergarten result from the same review section.

> "Students in the TRIAD groups learned more mathematics than the control students (effect size, g = .72; see Clements et al., 2011)."

### Clements 2013 (2)

Clements, D. H., Sarama, J., Wolfe, C. B., & Spitler, M. E. (2013). Longitudinal Evaluation of a Scale-Up Model for Teaching Mathematics With Trajectories and Technologies: Persistence of Effects in the Third Year. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212469270

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

The article's review of its kindergarten-year evaluation, attributed to Sarama, Clements, et al., 2012; TOT analyses reportedly showed both groups outperformed control (g = .38 and g = .30).

> "ITT analyses showed that at the end of kindergarten, students in the follow-through condition, but not the non–follow-through condition, scored statistically significantly higher than students in the control condition (g = .33; for a full report, see Sarama, Clements, et al., 2012)."
