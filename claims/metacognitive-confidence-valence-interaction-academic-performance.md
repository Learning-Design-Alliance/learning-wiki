# Metacognitive confidence moderates the effect of thought valence on academic performance: confidence amplifies positive thoughts' benefits and negative thoughts' harm

## Evidence

### Moreno 2022

Moreno, L., Briñol, P., & Petty, R. E. (2022). Metacognitive Confidence Can Increase but Also Decrease Performance in Academic Settings. Metacognition and Learning, 17, 139–165. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09270-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple-slopes analysis of the Study 1 regression showed opposite directions by valence: positive thoughts gained accuracy as validity rose (B = 0.411, p = .020) while negative thoughts lost accuracy (B = -0.374, p = .032). Only unstandardized coefficients are printed, so no effect size is reported.

> "among participants who were randomly assigned to generate positive thoughts, as perceived thought validity increased, more questions were solved correctly, B = 0.411 (95% CI: 0.067, 0.754) , t (123) = 2.365, p = .020. In contrast, for participants in the negative thought condition the effect was the opposite."
