## Evidence

### Reavley Nicola J. 2018

Reavley Nicola J., Morgan Amy J., Fischer Julie-Anne, Kitchener Betty, Bovopoulos Nataly, Jorm Anthony F. (2018). Effectiveness of eLearning and blended modes of delivery of Mental Health First Aid training in the workplace: randomised controlled trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-018-1888-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Personal Stigma Scale subscale in the 608-participant RCT, analysed with mixed-effects models; the article reports the reductions versus the PFA control as statistically significant, with no significant difference between the two MHFA modes and no effect size printed in the reporting sentence.

> "For beliefs in dangerousness/unpredictability, those in the blended and eLearning MHFA groups were significantly more likely to show reduced stigma towards people with depression and PTSD than those in the PFA eLearning group. There were no significant differences between the MHFA eLearning and blended courses."

### Reavley Nicola J. 2018 (2)

Reavley Nicola J., Morgan Amy J., Fischer Julie-Anne, Kitchener Betty, Bovopoulos Nataly, Jorm Anthony F. (2018). Effectiveness of eLearning and blended modes of delivery of Mental Health First Aid training in the workplace: randomised controlled trial. BMC Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-018-1888-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Weak-not-sick subscale scores were highly skewed and dichotomised at the median (the maximum score); no course showed significant improvement, a null secondary result.

> "Belief that people with depression or PTSD are 'weak not sick' was low at baseline, with no significant differences in improvement between courses."
