# Puzzlets players constructed longer sequences over successive sessions, though grade-level differences in tiles used were not statistically significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average tiles used in final Puzzlets sequences rose from about three on day 1 to over five on day 5; grade 2 students used slightly more tiles on average than grade 1 and kindergarten students, but this difference was not statistically significant. [→ Gadgil 2016](#gadgil-2016)

## Evidence

### Gadgil 2016

Gadgil, S., & Louw, M. (2016). Rapid Cycle Pilots: Improving Ed-Tech Products through Feedback. Learning Media Design Center, Carnegie Mellon University. Report to Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5364ec3a-0d6d-4252-8885-4484e657fde7

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Log-data analysis of 56 K-2 students' Puzzlets gameplay, using average tiles per final sequence as a complexity proxy, shown by grade in Figure 13 with standard error bars. The report describes the rise "from the graph" across the five days of play.

> "From the graph, it is seen that on day 1, students used close to three tiles on average to construct the final sequence, while on day 5, they used over five tiles on average to construct their final sequence."
