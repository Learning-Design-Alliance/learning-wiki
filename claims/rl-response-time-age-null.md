## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There was no significant linear decrease in log-response times with age in the preregistered analysis. [→ Bamberg 2025](#bamberg-2025)

## Evidence

### Bamberg 2025

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Preregistered regression of log-response times on age in the analysed sample found no significant decrease: "coeff =−0.01; SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [−0.03, 0.001], p = 0.086"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Against our expectations, we did not ﬁnd a signiﬁcant linear decrease in log-response times with age (coeff =−0.01; SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [−0.03, 0.001], p = 0.086)."

### Bamberg 2025 (2)

Bamberg, C., Weigelt, S. & Hagelweide, K. (2025). Reversal learning is influenced by cognitive flexibility and develops throughout early adolescence. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00308-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory analysis excluding responses below 200 ms found the age relation "signi ﬁcant (coeff = −0.02; SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [ −0.03, −0.001], p = 0.011)". The article notes this indicates quality control is necessary for online experiments.

> "when excluding response times below 200 ms., since they are too fast to be serious responses to the task, the relation with age is signi ﬁcant (coeff = −0.02; SE = 0.01, 95% CI = [ −0.03, −0.001], p = 0.011)."
