## Evidence

### Hamza K 2026

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Attitude items on a 1–5 agreement scale in the 709-student cross-sectional survey; percentages are descriptive, no effect size printed.

> "Most students agreed or strongly agreed that evidence-based medicine is important for the practical work of physicians (73.7%) and that it improves patient care (71.8%). Additionally, 73.2% expressed a desire to further improve their EBM skills during medical training, while only 16.3% thought that evidence-based practice imposes a burden on healthcare professionals."

### Hamza K 2026 (2)

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mann-Whitney U comparisons of attitude items by EBM training status in the same survey; only the patient-specific care item reached significance (p = 0.002), no effect size printed.

> "Mean attitude scores were generally higher in EBM-trained groups, though differences were not statistically significant. A notable exception was the perception that EBM facilitates patient-specific care, which was significantly higher among trained students (mean: 3.94 vs. 3.75, p value = 0.002)"
