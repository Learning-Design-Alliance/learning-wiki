# Math-related word primes facilitate arithmetic processing in children with DD but inhibit it in typically-developing controls

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the DD group, math primes and negative primes did not differ significantly for addition and multiplication, and math primes facilitated processing in all four operations. [→ Rubinsten O 2010](#rubinsten-o-2010)
`q3 i?` In the control group, math primes acted as affectively unrelated, inhibiting processing in division and slowing responses relative to positive primes in the other operations. [→ Rubinsten O 2010 (2)](#rubinsten-o-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Rubinsten O 2010

Rubinsten O, Tannock R. (2010). Mathematics anxiety in children with developmental dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions 6:46. http://www.behavioralandbrainfunctions.com/content/6/1/46

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Planned comparisons in the priming experiment (Results section). The article reports "no significant difference between negative and math primes for addition and multiplication" in DD, and that "in all 4 arithmetic procedures, math prime words significantly facilitated processing compared to positive prime words".

> "in the DD group there was no significant difference between negative and math primes for addition and multiplication. Furthermore, in the case of division, math prime words facilitated processing even more than the negative prime words"

### Rubinsten O 2010 (2)

Same experiment, control group. Math primes were slower than positive primes in addition, multiplication and subtraction, and in division "math prime words inhibited processing", acting as affectively unrelated to targets.
