## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The summative PT-group more often found the feedback not useful only after passing: failing was the incentive to act on feedback. [→ van Wijk 2024](#van-wijk-2024)

## Evidence

### van Wijk 2024

van Wijk, E. V., van Blankenstein, F. M., Donkers, J., Janse, R. J., Bustraan, J., Adelmeijer, L. G. M., Dubois, E. A., Dekker, F. W., & Langers, A. M. J. (2024). Does 'summative' count? The influence of the awarding of study credits on feedback use and test-taking motivation in medical progress testing. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10324-4

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative interview finding: students consulted feedback as a thermometer to check they were on track, and acted on it only when an insufficient grade, "because you still want to get those study credits", created urgency.

> "Suppose, if I had failed I would think oh dear, then I will really look at what I did wrong, which subject and really do that because you still want to get those study credits.” (Interview #18)."
