## Evidence

### Spreckelsen C. 2017

Spreckelsen C., Juenger J. (2017). Repeated testing improves achievement in a blended learning approach for risk competence training of medical students: results of a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1016-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial in the mandatory Evidence-based Medicine course: 114 students balanced-randomized to intervention or control (both n = 57), five dropped out. ANCOVA with baseline score as covariate showed a significant intervention effect; the article reports "d = .94, r = .42 (95% CI: [.26, .57])".

> "ANCOVA indicated that the covariate (pre-training score at baseline) was not related to the post-training score, F(1, 106) = 2.88, p > .05, while there was a significant impact of the intervention (repeated testing) on learning achievement, F(1106) = 12.72, p < .05, d = .94, r = .42 (95% CI: [.26, .57])."

### Spreckelsen C. 2017 (2)

Spreckelsen C., Juenger J. (2017). Repeated testing improves achievement in a blended learning approach for risk competence training of medical students: results of a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1016-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same RCT, ANCOVA-adjusted post-training means with non-parametric bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals: "7.03 (95% CI: [6.60, 7.47])" for control versus "8.13 (95% CI: [7.71, 8.56])" for intervention. No effect size is printed for this adjusted-means comparison.

> "Calculation of the adjusted means yielded 7.03 (95% CI: [6.60, 7.47]) and 8.13 (95% CI: [7.71, 8.56]) for the control and intervention group, respectively."
