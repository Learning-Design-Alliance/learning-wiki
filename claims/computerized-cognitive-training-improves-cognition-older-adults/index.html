Evidence¶

Kueider Alexandra M. 2012¶

Kueider Alexandra M., Parisi Jeanine M., Gross Alden L., Rebok George W. (2012). Computerized Cognitive Training with Older Adults: A Systematic Review. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0040588

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Systematic review of 38 studies of computerized cognitive training in cognitively healthy older adults, classified into three program types. The review concludes that "all three approaches to computerized training" appear to hold promise for improving cognitive abilities in this population.

"Based on this review, all three approaches to computerized training – classic cognitive training tasks, neuropsychological software, and video games – appear to hold promise for improving cognitive abilities in cognitively normal, community-dwelling older adults who have a higher risk of cognitive decline as they age."

Related Claims¶

  • Neuropsychological software programs positively impacted cognitive performance, with memory benefits across nearly all reviewed studies — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Computerized cognitive training produces a small but statistically significant improvement in cognitive performance in healthy older adults — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Brain games produce statistically significant cognitive performance gains in older adults, but gains are modest and do not detect improvements over control tasks or produce far transfer — related
  • Video games improved reaction time, processing speed, executive function, and global cognition in older adults, with largest effects on reaction time and processing speed — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Classic cognitive training tasks improved reaction time, processing speed, working memory, executive function, memory, visual spatial ability, and attention in older adults — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Computerized cognitive training effect sizes are comparable to or better than non-computerized cognitive training interventions — related
  • Older adults do not need to be technologically savvy to successfully complete or benefit from computerized cognitive training — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
