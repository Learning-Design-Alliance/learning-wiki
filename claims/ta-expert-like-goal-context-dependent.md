## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin 2013

Shih-Yin Lin, Charles Henderson, William Mamudi, Chandralekha Singh, and Edit Yerushalmi. (2013). Teaching assistants' beliefs regarding example solutions in introductory physics. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.9.020115

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

GAIQ study of 24 first-year physics graduate TAs in a semester-long training course; open-ended general-context responses were coded and counted. The article reports that "Only 21 percent of the TAs mentioned developing an expert -like problem solving approach" in the general context.

> "Only 21 percent of the TAs mentioned developing an expert -like problem solving approach when asked to generally describe their goals for example solutions."

### Shih-Yin Lin 2013 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin, Charles Henderson, William Mamudi, Chandralekha Singh, and Edit Yerushalmi. (2013). Teaching assistants' beliefs regarding example solutions in introductory physics. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.9.020115

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same GAIQ study, concrete artifact-comparison context with three example solutions. The article reports "71 percent, corresponding to 17 out of 24 TAs" endorsed the expert-like problem-solving goal, versus 75 percent (18 of 24) for physics knowledge.

> "approximately equal numbers of TAs considered example solutions as a means to help students develop an expert -like problem-solving approach (71 percent, corresponding to 17 out of 24 TAs) and as a means to help students construct content -specific physics knowledge (75 percent, corresponding to 18 out of 24TAs)"
