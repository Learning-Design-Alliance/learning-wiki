Evidence¶

Tae Lee Lee 2023¶

Tae Lee Lee, Hanall Lee and Nyeonju Kang. (2023). A meta-analysis showing improved cognitive performance in healthy young adults with transcranial alternating current stimulation. npj Science of Learning 8:1. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00152-9

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis of 93 cognitive-performance comparisons drawn from 50 qualified studies comparing active tACS with sham, finding "a significant low overall effect of tACS protocols on cognitive performance improvements" with SMD = 0.161. The 56 included studies comprised 1797 healthy young adults.

"The random-effects meta-analysis on 93 total comparisons from the 50 studies identified a significant low overall effect of tACS protocols on cognitive performance improvements ( SMD = 0.161; SE = 0.027; 95% CI = 0.109–0.214; Z = 6.038; P < 0.001)."

Related Claims¶

  • tACS cognitive benefits concentrate in executive function for theta protocols and in executive and perceptual-motor function for online gamma protocols — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • tACS effects on cognitive performance depend on targeted brain region: theta on PFC and PPC and online gamma on PPC are effective — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Offline-tACS with theta frequency band significantly improves cognitive performance, but offline alpha, beta and gamma bands do not — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Online-tACS with theta and gamma frequency bands significantly improves cognitive performance, while delta, alpha and beta bands do not — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Side effects of tACS are mild and common: about 46.2% of participants in studies reporting them may experience effects such as itching, tingling and phosphenes — related
