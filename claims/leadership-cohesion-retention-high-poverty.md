# Dissatisfaction with leadership and staff cohesion predicts staying only in high-poverty schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers in high-poverty schools dissatisfied with both leadership and staff cohesion are less likely to stay than satisfied peers; in low-poverty schools dissatisfaction with both does not reduce staying. [→ Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley 2012](#sarah-almy-and-melissa-tooley-2012)

## Evidence

### Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley 2012

Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley. (2012). Building and Sustaining Talent: Creating Conditions in High-Poverty Schools That Support Effective Teaching and Learning. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543216

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

An analysis of the 2007-08 SASS data finds that in low-poverty schools teachers unhappy with both conditions stay at the same rate, while “teachers in high-poverty schools who are dissatisfied with both of these conditions are less likely to stay than those who are satisfied.”
