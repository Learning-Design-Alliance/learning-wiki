## Evidence

### Liang 2026

Liang, F., Zhu, Y., Li, R. & Fu, Q. (2026). Foil Type Modulates Developmental Changes in Statistical Learning Across Childhood to Adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00434-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Group × Foil Type GLMM (M2) on Grade 5 versus adult accuracy (N = 66); the omnibus interaction was significant (OR = 1.11, 95% CI [1.009, 1.216]), while the Group main effect itself was not significant (p = 0.052).

> "a significant Group × Foil Type interaction was observed, 𝐵 = 0.102, 95% CI [0.009, 0.196], 𝜒2(1) = 4.63, 𝑝 = 0.031"

### Liang 2026 (2)

Liang, F., Zhu, Y., Li, R. & Fu, Q. (2026). Foil Type Modulates Developmental Changes in Statistical Learning Across Childhood to Adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00434-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple-effect analysis of the Grade 5 versus adult interaction: the adult advantage was significant only for partword foils (pHolm = 0.004, BF10 = 8.26), consistent with supplementary above-chance tests showing children performed at chance on partword foils while adults showed robust above-chance accuracy.

> "for partword foils, Adults (𝐸𝑀𝑀 = 0.653, 95% CI [0.588, 0.713]) performed significantly better than Grade 5 children (𝐸𝑀𝑀 = 0.538, 95% CI [0.471, 0.605])"
