# Rescaling percent-scale grades onto a 4-point scale cut the fail rate from 8.3% to 1.3%, indicating the heavy averaging weight of low F's is the main driver of percent-scale failures

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mapping CLASP10 exam-item grades onto the CLASP4 scale without changing any assigned grades reduced the fail rate by a factor of 6.5 ± 1.4, consistent with the overall 5.3 ratio. [→ Webb 2020](#webb-2020)

## Evidence

### Webb 2020

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Re-analysis of eight CLASP10 classes (2006-2007) with complete grade calculations, covering 1839 course grades to 1272 students. Grades above 6.0 were shifted down by 5.5 and all F's set to 0, then the original weighted averages recomputed, giving a fail-rate increase of a factor of 6.5 ± 1.4 under percent grading. The authors conclude the heavy effective weight of low F's "is the main factor increasing the failure rates."

> "The original percent graded courses had a fail rate of 8.3% ± 0.6% and the 4-point re-scaled courses had a fail rate of 1.3% ± 0.3%."
