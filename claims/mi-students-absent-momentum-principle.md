# M&I students almost never mentioned momentum when reasoning about the elevator problem, despite the curriculum's emphasis on the momentum principle

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Only one of the 14 M&I participants mentioned momentum, and did so incorrectly, concluding that constant velocity means zero momentum. [→ Keith R Bujak 2010](#keith-r-bujak-2010)

## Evidence

### Keith R Bujak 2010

Keith R Bujak, Richard Catrambone, Marcos D. Caballero, M Jack Marr, Michael F Schatz, Matthew A Kohlmyer. (2010). Comparing the Matter and Interactions Curriculum with a Traditional Physics Curriculum: A Think Aloud Study. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1011.5449](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1011.5449)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative transcript coding of the elevator problem in the think-aloud study found the momentum principle, the first principle emphasized in the M&I course, was nearly absent from M&I students' verbal reasoning.

> "the most surprising finding from our analysis is the near complete absence of the word “momentum” or the phrase “change in momentum” by M&I students.  Only one of the 14 M&I participants mentioned momentum, albeit incorrectly."
