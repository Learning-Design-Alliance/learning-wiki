# Novices made fewer total errors in VR than in cross-sectional viewing, with a trend approaching significance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Novices committed fewer total errors with VR (99) than with cross-sectional viewing (127), a difference that did not reach significance (p = 0.054). [→ Timonen Tomi 2022](#timonen-tomi-2022)
`q3 i?` Novices made significantly fewer fiducial measurement errors in VR than in PACS (p = 0.049). [→ Timonen Tomi 2022 (2)](#timonen-tomi-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Timonen Tomi 2022

Timonen Tomi, Dietz Aarno, Linder Pia, Lehtimäki Antti, Löppönen Heikki, Elomaa Antti-Pekka, Iso-Mustajärvi Matti. (2022). The effect of virtual reality on temporal bone anatomy evaluation and performance. European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology, 279(9), 4303–4312. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-021-07183-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Poisson loglinear comparison of total errors across landmark, fiducial and anatomical measurement tasks for five novices in both viewing methods. The article reports "99 errors vs. 127 errors, p = 0.054", a trend towards fewer errors in VR.

> "In total, the novices made fewer errors with VR than with cross-sectional viewing (99 errors vs. 127 errors, p = 0.054)."

### Timonen Tomi 2022 (2)

Timonen Tomi, Dietz Aarno, Linder Pia, Lehtimäki Antti, Löppönen Heikki, Elomaa Antti-Pekka, Iso-Mustajärvi Matti. (2022). The effect of virtual reality on temporal bone anatomy evaluation and performance. European Archives of Oto-Rhino-Laryngology, 279(9), 4303–4312. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-021-07183-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 2 reports novice fiducial error percentages of 2.32 in PACS versus 1.36 in VR, mean difference 1.09, significant at "0.049*" by Poisson loglinear test. Landmark (p = 0.066) and anatomical (p = 0.903) error differences were not significant.
