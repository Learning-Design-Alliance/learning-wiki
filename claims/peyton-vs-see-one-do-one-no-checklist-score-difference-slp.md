# Peyton's 4-step method and "see one, do one" produce no statistically significant difference in simulated lumbar puncture checklist scores among first-year medical students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In an RCT of 35 first-year medical students learning simulated lumbar puncture, checklist scores did not differ significantly between the "see one, do one" group (13.95 ± 1.03) and the Peyton's group (14.56 ± 1.26). [→ Dugan L 2025](#dugan-l-2025)

## Evidence

### Dugan L 2025

Dugan L, Rei KM, Ueno A, Mahutga J, Varma M. (2025). Assessing Best Practices in a Simulated Lumbar Puncture Workshop With Medical Students. The Permanente Journal. https://doi.org/10.7812/TPP/24.155

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 35 first-year medical students randomized to "see one, do one" (n = 19) or Peyton's 4-step (n = 16) teaching in a simulated lumbar puncture workshop, scored on a validated 16-point binary checklist. The article reports "Cohen's d = 0.540; P = .121", not significant at P < .05.

> "Univariate analysis comparing "see one, do one" and Peyton's method groups showed checklist score (13.95 ± 1.03 vs 14.56 ± 1.26 points, respectively; Cohen's d = 0.540; P = .121) and the change in confidence (1.03 ± 0.59 vs 1.13 ± 0.50 rating, respectively; Cohen's d = 0.164; P = .631) were not significant"
