# Total written assessment time at medical school correlates positively with graduates' mean first-attempt postgraduate examination performance, including after controlling for entry qualifications.

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Devine OP 2015

Devine OP, Harborne AC, McManus IC. (2015). Assessment at UK medical schools varies substantially in volume, type and intensity and correlates with postgraduate attainment. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0428-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Exploratory institutional-level correlational analysis relating validated assessment-volume data to published mean first-attempt MRCGP and MRCP(UK) percentile ranks; the article reports "rS = .515, p = .014, n = 22".

> "Overall there was a significant correlation between total minutes of assessment time and mean postgraduate attainment (rS = .515, p = .014, n = 22)."

### Devine OP 2015 (2)

Devine OP, Harborne AC, McManus IC. (2015). Assessment at UK medical schools varies substantially in volume, type and intensity and correlates with postgraduate attainment. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0428-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spearman partial correlation using all schools including Oxford, Cambridge and Queen Mary; the article concludes the relationship "is not due to confounding with entry qualifications".

> "The partial correlation of mean postgraduate attainment with minutes of assessment time, after taking entry qualifications into account remained significant (partial rS, = .456, p = .038, 19 df)."
