Evidence¶

Lemke 2023¶

Lemke, M., Murphy, D., Soo Ping Chow, A., Spencer, H., & Zhang, A. (2023). Comparing early literacy screening assessment benchmarks in Massachusetts. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED638508

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Regression-based linking study of grade 3 screening benchmarks to MCAS ELA (2021/22 data, more than 4,000 students). Estimates of association show 'strong to very strong associations between the tests,' yet about 40 percent of benchmark-meeting students did not meet or only partially met MCAS expectations.

"About 8 percent of students identified as at any level of risk on a literacy screening assessment at end of year (EOY) in grade 3 met or exceeded MCAS ELA standards, while about 60 percent who met screening assessment benchmarks met or exceeded expectations on MCAS (see Figure 3)."

Related Claims¶

  • At-risk students show substantially lower achievement than non-At-risk peers on state tests — related
  • Screening assessment benchmarks indicating significant risk shift up and down on the MCAS scale within and across grades, so students with the same skills may be classified differently at different times — related
  • The predictive value of grade 3 screening benchmarks for MCAS ELA proficiency varies widely: 15 to 69 percent of students classified as on track did not reach MCAS proficiency, and students flagged at risk rarely met proficiency — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Grade 3 screener benchmark scores marking any level of risk map to MCAS ELA scores from mid Partially Meeting Expectations to the beginning of Meeting Expectations — related
  • Equipercentile linking shows grade 3 screening benchmark cut scores identifying at-risk students cluster in the MCAS Partially Meeting Expectations performance level, with variation among assessments — related
  • Early screening benchmark status predicts later performance: most beginning-of-year at-risk students stay at risk, and meeting benchmarks by end of grade 1 is associated with much higher grade 3 MCAS odds — related
  • Among students below benchmark at the start of grade 1, meeting screening benchmarks earlier is associated with higher grade 3 MCAS proficiency rates — related
  • Most students at risk at BOY remain at risk later, but students who meet benchmark by midyear tend to stay on track — related
  • Few students identified as significantly below benchmark in grades 1–3 met MCAS expectations 1 or 2 years later, with higher rates for earlier identification — related
