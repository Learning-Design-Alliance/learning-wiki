# The published norms allow normative interpretation of both status and fall-to-spring growth, e.g., an Algebra 1 student at the 55th fall percentile has an expected gain of 8 RIT with growth SD of 9, and an 11-RIT gain places the student at the 62nd growth percentile.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Conditional growth norms give the expected fall-to-spring gain and its SD for any fall score, and a student's actual gain can be located in a growth percentile distribution. [→ Thum 2019](#thum-2019)

Worked example using the Algebra 1 norms snapshot (Figure 3.5). The report states the expected fall-to-spring gain at the 55th fall percentile is 8 RIT with growth SD 9, and that a student improving 11 RITs from 233 to 244 falls at the 62nd percentile on the specific growth scale; values are descriptive norms, not test statistics.

> "The expected fall-to-spring gain for a student who starts in the fall at the 55th percentile score (233) is 8 with an associated SD of growth of 9"
