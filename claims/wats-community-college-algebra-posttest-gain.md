# Using a WATS in community college elementary algebra corresponds to a statistically significant 2.59-point post-test gain (Hedges g = 0.32) controlling for pretest

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Controlling for students' pretest scores, WATS use corresponded to a 2.59 point increase in end-of-semester MDTP post-test scores, statistically significant at p < .05, equivalent to about 5 percentage points on a 50-point test. [→ Shandy Hauk 2017](#shandy-hauk-2017)
`q3 i1` The effect's Hedges g is 0.32 with a 95% confidence interval of .14-.50, which the authors call a small but noteworthy effect for studies of this size. [→ Shandy Hauk 2017 (2)](#shandy-hauk-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Shandy Hauk 2017

Shandy Hauk, Bryan Matlen, Larry Thomas. (2017). Exploration of the Factors that Support Learning: Web-based Activity and Testing Systems in Community College Algebra. 20th Annual Conference on Research in Undergraduate Mathematics Education. https://sigmaa.maa.org/rume

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical linear modeling of MDTP post-test scores from 510 students of 29 instructors across 18 colleges in a multi-site cluster randomized trial, with random teacher effects and student- and teacher-level pretest covariates. The WATS coefficient was 2.59 (SE 1.17, p = .04); the article reports "a statistically significant positive effect (p < .05)" but no effect size in this model result.

> "Controlling for students' pretest scores, we found that using WATS corresponded to a 2.59 point increase in students' post-test scores, a statistically significant positive effect (p < .05)."

### Shandy Hauk 2017 (2)

Shandy Hauk, Bryan Matlen, Larry Thomas. (2017). Exploration of the Factors that Support Learning: Web-based Activity and Testing Systems in Community College Algebra. 20th Annual Conference on Research in Undergraduate Mathematics Education. https://sigmaa.maa.org/rume

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Standardized effect size reported for the same HLM analysis of the cluster randomized trial's first cohort: "The Hedges g value for this effect is 0.32", with a 95% confidence interval of .14 - .50, characterized by the authors as small but noteworthy.

> "The Hedges g value for this effect is 0.32, which is considered a small but noteworthy effect in educational research for studies of this size (Cheung & Slavin, 2015; Hill et al. 2008). The 95% confidence interval of the Hedges g value is .14 - .50."
