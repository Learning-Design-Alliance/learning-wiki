# Self-explanation effects hold on far-transfer but not near-transfer questions in a geometry classroom study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When ninth graders self-explained while studying a geometry theorem, performance improved on far-transfer questions but not on near-transfer questions. [→ Dunlosky 2013](#dunlosky-2013)

## Evidence

### Dunlosky 2013

Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Classroom study by R. M. F. Wong et al. (2002) reported in the monograph's educational-contexts section: ninth graders studied an unstudied theorem with self-explanation prompts or nonspecific think-aloud instructions, with a review and final test the following week. The dissociation between near- and far-transfer is printed without a test statistic.

> "Self- explanation did not improve performance on near-transfer questions but did improve performance on far-transfer questions."
