## Evidence

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Grading activity in a mandatory semester-long PD course at a U.S. research university: 15 first-year physics TAs graded two solutions with a CA-inspired rubric, and their scores were compared with the investigators' agreed "expert" scores. The study reports the scores were "not in agreement with “expert” graders using a rubric".

> "However, TAs’ scores on SSD and SSE when using a rubric were not in agreement with “expert” graders using a rubric (see Table II)."

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016 (2)

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Within the same PD-course grading activity, comparison of TAs' rubric-assigned and pre-activity scores found the brief solution SSE score "approximately 1 point lower when using the rubric as compared to the pre grading activity", while the elaborated solution SSD score was approximately the same as its pre score.

> "TAs’ average score on the brief solution SSE was approximately 1 point lower when using the rubric as compared to the pre grading activity."
