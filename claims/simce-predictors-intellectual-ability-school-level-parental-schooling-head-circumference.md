# Intellectual ability, school achievement level, parental schooling, head circumference-for-age Z-score and sex are the most relevant predictors of 2009 SIMCE outcomes in Chilean students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In 2010 5th-grade students, school achievement level (high), sex (females), paternal schooling (≥14y), head circumference-for-age Z-score (>0) and intellectual ability (grades I and II) predicted language SIMCE (R2 = .354), and school level, head circumference, intellectual ability and paternal schooling predicted mathematics SIMCE (R2 = .463). [→ Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019](#ivanovic-daniza-m-et-al-2019)
`q3 i?` In 2010 1st-grade high-school students, school achievement level, maternal schooling and intellectual ability predicted language SIMCE (R2 = .389), and school level, maternal schooling, sex (males) and intellectual ability predicted mathematics SIMCE (R2 = .500). [→ Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019 (2)](#ivanovic-daniza-m-et-al-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019

Ivanovic Daniza M. et al. (2019). A multifactorial approach of nutritional, intellectual, brain development, cardiovascular risk, socio-economic, demographic and educational variables affecting the scholastic achievement in Chilean students: An eight- year follow-up study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0212279

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Stepwise multiple regression in the 2010 5ESG sample (n = 682) identified the predictors of 2009 SIMCE language and mathematics scores; the model for mathematics entered school level, head circumference-for-age Z-score (> 0), intellectual ability and paternal schooling with "R 2 = .463; p < .00001".

> "for LSA, the level of SA of the educational establishments in the 2009 SIMCE tests (high), sex (females), paternal schooling (≥ 14y), head circumference-for-age Z-score (> 0) and intellectual ability (grades I and II) were the independent variables associated with LSA results (R2 = .354; p < .0001)"

### Ivanovic Daniza M. et al 2019 (2)

Ivanovic Daniza M. et al. (2019). A multifactorial approach of nutritional, intellectual, brain development, cardiovascular risk, socio-economic, demographic and educational variables affecting the scholastic achievement in Chilean students: An eight- year follow-up study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0212279

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Stepwise multiple regression in the 2010 1HSG sample (n = 671); for mathematics the model entered school level, maternal schooling, sex (males) and intellectual ability with R2 = .500; p < .00001.

> "the level of SA of the educational establishments in the 2009 SIMCE tests (high), maternal schooling (≥ 14y), and intellectual ability (grades I and II) were the independent variables more relevant associated with LSA in the 2009 SIMCE (R 2 = .3892; p < .00001)"
