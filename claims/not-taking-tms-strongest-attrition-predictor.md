## Evidence

### Kadmon Guni 2016

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression comparing TMS-takers and non-takers among best school leavers found non-takers at higher risk of prolongation and dropout (OR 0.324 and 0.451); most non-takers (82 of 97) belonged to the centrally admitted SLG-Best group.

> "A higher risk for prolonging the studies and for dropout was detected among the best school-leavers who had not taken the TMS as compared to those who had taken the TMS (OR 0.324 and 0.451, respectively; see Table 3 (Tab. 3), Point B)."

### Kadmon Guni 2016 (2)

Kadmon Guni, Kadmon Martina. (2016). Academic Performance of Students with the Highest and Mediocre School-leaving Grades: Does the Aptitude Test for Medical Studies (TMS) Balance Their Prognoses? GMS Journal for Medical Education 33(1):Doc7. [doi:10.3205/zma001006](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the pooled sample of both groups, logistic regression showed the TMS score and not having taken the TMS carried the same attrition risk potentials as in each group separately; the abstract reports attrition was most strongly associated with failing to take the TMS (OR=0.230-0.380).

> "The TMS scores (see Table 6 (Tab. 6), Point A) and not having taken the TMS (see Table 6 (Tab. 6), Point B) represented similar risk potentials for attrition as separately observed in each study group (compare to Table 3 (Tab. 3) and table 5 (Tab. 5))."
