## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant difference in mean scores between students who took the computer-assisted test and those who took the paper-based test (Mann-Whitney U, p = .111). [→ Öz 2018](#oz-2018)

## Evidence

### Öz 2018

Öz, H., & Özturan, T. (2018). Computer-based and paper-based testing: Does the test administration mode influence the reliability and validity of achievement tests? Journal of Language and Linguistic Studies, 14(1), 67-85. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1175793

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest-only experimental study: 97 pre-service ELT teachers randomly assigned to computer-based (n = 50) or paper-based (n = 47) versions of the same 60-item test. The Mann-Whitney U test found "no significant difference in mean scores"; equivalence was not tested. Descriptive means were 45.36 (CBT) and 42.98 (PBT).

> "The results revealed that there was no significant difference in mean scores of the participants who took the computer assisted test and those who took paper based test , Zu (954.500), p= .111> .05."
