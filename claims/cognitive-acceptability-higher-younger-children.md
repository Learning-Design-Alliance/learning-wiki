## Evidence

### Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E 2015

Bayley Julie E. and Brown Katherine E. (2015). Translating group programmes into online formats: establishing the acceptability of a parents’ sex and relationships communication serious game. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-015-2545-0

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA analysis in the acceptability evaluation compared game acceptability by gender, ethnicity and child's age. Child's age significantly affected cognitive acceptability, with post hoc univariate analysis showing significantly higher cognitive acceptability for parents of young children (partial η2 = .138).

> "MANOVA results showed a significant effect of child’s age on Cognitive Acceptability (Factor 1), (F (2, 38) = 3.764, p = .032; partial η2 = .165). Post hoc univariate analysis revealed that cognitive acceptability was significantly higher for parents of young children, (F(2, 43) = 3.436, p = .0141; partial η2 = .138)."
