## Evidence

### Calzone 2026

Calzone, K., Stokes, L., Peterson, C., Yee, L. M., Liewehr, D., and Badzek, L. (2026). Evaluation of the Integration of Genetics and Genomics Into Nursing Practice. Journal of Nursing Scholarship 58, no. 1: e70056. https://doi.org/10.1111/jnu.70056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confidence section of the 2023 GGNPS survey compared with 2010 data; the confidence response format was revised to a dichotomous confident versus not at all confident option. The article reports "only a small proportion of nurses were confident (N = 301, 33%)".

> "The current survey (Table 3) found that only a small proportion of nurses were confident (N = 301, 33%) in their ability to decide what family history information was needed to inform a patient's susceptibility to common diseases. This represents only a slight improvement from the 2010 assessment (N = 153, 27.5%)"

### Calzone 2026 (2)

Calzone, K., Stokes, L., Peterson, C., Yee, L. M., Liewehr, D., and Badzek, L. (2026). Evaluation of the Integration of Genetics and Genomics Into Nursing Practice. Journal of Nursing Scholarship 58, no. 1: e70056. https://doi.org/10.1111/jnu.70056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same 2023-versus-2010 GGNPS comparison showing directionally mixed confidence changes across items; the article also notes fewer nurses in 2010 (51%) than in 2023 (62%) reported lack of confidence about benefits information.

> "there was a modest lowering of confidence in giving patients information on the risk (2010‐N = 338, 61%; 2023‐N = 419, 46%) and limitations (2010‐N = 328, 60%; 2023‐N = 424, 46%) of genetic testing for common diseases"
