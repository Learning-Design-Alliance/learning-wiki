## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Applicants rated an SJT with a rating format more favourably than a pick-one format on applicant differentiation, study relatedness, chance to perform and ease of cheating, each with a small effect (η2 = .01). [→ De Leng Wendy E. 2018](#de-leng-wendy-e-2018)

## Evidence

### De Leng Wendy E. 2018

De Leng Wendy E., Stegers-Jager Karen M., Born Marise Ph., Themmen Axel P. N. (2018). Influence of response instructions and response format on applicant perceptions of a situational judgement test for medical school selection. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1390-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVAs among 372 medical school applicants randomly assigned to four SJT versions. Rating format scored higher on applicant differentiation (M = 4.30 vs 3.94), study relatedness (M = 3.73 vs 3.44), chance to perform (M = 3.81 vs 3.42) and lower ease of cheating (M = 4.94 vs 5.31), each F with η2 = .01. The article notes this was "in contrast to our hypothesis".

> "Overall, an SJT with a rating response format was rated more favourably than an SJT with a pick-one format on applicant differentiation, study-relatedness, chance to perform and ease of cheating."
