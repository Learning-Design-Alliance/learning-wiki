# Three-dimensional visual objects produced higher post-test drawing performance than two-dimensional visual objects, and both experimental groups outperformed the control group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Post-hoc pairwise comparisons showed the three-dimensional visual objects group scored highest, followed by the two-dimensional group, with the control group lowest; all pairwise mean differences were reported significant at 0.05 (TDVO-TWDVO 1.125; TDVO-control 1.4417; TWDVO-control 2.865). [→ Abass et al. 2014](#abass-et-al-2014)

## Evidence

### Abass et al. 2014

Bada Tayo Abass, Bello Isyakka, Ijisakin Yemi Olaolu & Fajuyigbe Michael Olusegun. (2014). Effects of Two and Three-Dimensional Visual Objects on the Acquisition of Drawing Skills among JSS1 Students in Osun State, Nigeria. World Journal of Education, 4(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v4n1p62

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc multiple comparisons (Table 3) of post-test drawing scores among the three groups (n = 40 each) in the same pretest-posttest experiment. The article reports mean differences of 1.125, 1.4417 and 2.865, "all significant at 0.05 level"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the performance of subjects in the three dimensional visual objects group is superior to that of the two dimensional visual objects group. However, subjects in the two experimental groups out-performed their colleagues in the control group."
