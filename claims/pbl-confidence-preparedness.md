# PBL students reported greater prescribing confidence and preparedness and completed more drug prescriptions during training, but confidence did not correlate with assessed skill

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in the PBL programme felt significantly more confident in their prescribing skills than students in the traditional programme (p < 0.001), except for verifying suitability of treatment. [→ Brinkman David J. 2021](#brinkman-david-j-2021)
`q2 i?` A larger proportion of PBL students felt adequately prepared for prescribing as a junior doctor (21% vs 9%) and more had completed drug prescriptions during training (23% vs 49% who wrote none). [→ Brinkman David J. 2021 (2)](#brinkman-david-j-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Brinkman David J. 2021

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire with the validated WHO 6-step self-rated confidence scale completed by the same two cohorts (n=36 traditional, n=54 PBL). Total confidence was 2.5 ± 0.5 vs 2.9 ± 0.5, "significantly more confident ... (p < 0.001)"; 'verify the suitability of your drug treatment' was the only step without higher PBL confidence. No effect size was printed.

> "Overall, students in the PBL programme felt significantly more confident in their prescribing skills than students in the traditional programme (p < 0.001; Table 5), especially regarding 'choose a (drug) treatment'."

### Brinkman David J. 2021 (2)

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Self-reported questionnaire data from the same cohorts: 21% of PBL vs 9% of traditional students felt adequately prepared, and 23% vs 49% respectively had completed at least one drug prescription during training for practice purposes. These are descriptive percentages without a printed effect size.

> "A larger proportion of students in the PBL programme felt adequately prepared for their prescribing responsibilities as a junior doctor compared with students in the traditional programme (21% vs 9%)."

### Brinkman David J. 2021 (3)

Brinkman David J., Monteiro Teresa, Monteiro Emilia C., Richir Milan C., van Agtmael Michiel A., Tichelaar Jelle. (2021). Switching from a traditional undergraduate programme in (clinical) pharmacology and therapeutics to a problem-based learning programme. European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-020-03027-3

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Spearman correlation analysis across both cohorts between self-rated confidence and case-scenario skill scores (r = 0.20 traditional, r = 0.09 PBL); number of prescriptions written was likewise weakly correlated (r = 0.21 and r = -0.01). The authors state this indicates students do not accurately assess their prescribing skills.

> "Prescribing confidence was not associated with the prescribing skills scores of students in either the traditional programme (r = 0.20) or the PBL programme (r = 0.09)."
