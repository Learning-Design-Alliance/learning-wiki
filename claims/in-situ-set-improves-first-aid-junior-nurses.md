## Evidence

### Shen Meiyu 2026

Shen Meiyu, Tan Xiaolin, Xianyu Yunyan, Chen Qiuxiang, Fang Yu, Zeng Chao, Yang Guirong, Pan Minghao, Cai Zhongxiang. (2026). Developing and evaluating an in situ, simulated emergency training course for nurses with low seniority. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08961-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized controlled study of 82 junior nurses, the between-group post-training difference in self-rated first aid ability was significant (z = -4.263, p < 0.05), with the intervention group scoring higher than the control group. First aid ability was measured with a 28-item self-report questionnaire scored on a 5-point Likert scale (totally inconsistent to completely consistent), not with a performance or skills test. The intervention group received in situ simulated emergency training 2-3 times a month in addition to the routine stratified training the control group received once a month, so the difference may reflect more training sessions as well as the simulation format.

> "After training, the scores on the four scales in the intervention group were higher than those in the control group; however, only the scores for CR (z = -2.198) and FA (z = -4.263) differed significantly (p < 0.05)."

### Shen Meiyu 2026 (2)

Shen Meiyu, Tan Xiaolin, Xianyu Yunyan, Chen Qiuxiang, Fang Yu, Zeng Chao, Yang Guirong, Pan Minghao, Cai Zhongxiang. (2026). Developing and evaluating an in situ, simulated emergency training course for nurses with low seniority. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08961-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the intervention group, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests showed self-rated first aid scores (a 28-item Likert self-report questionnaire, not a skills test) rose significantly from pre- to post-training (z = -4.931, p < 0.05), while the control group showed no significant change (z = -1.238, p > 0.05). This is a pre-post comparison within one group, and the intervention group's training period included in situ simulation sessions 2-3 times a month on top of once-monthly routine training, so the gain cannot be attributed to the simulation alone.

> "In the intervention group, after the training, the scores for CT (z= -2.218), CC (z= -2.326), CR (z= -3.593), and FA (z = -4.931) were significantly higher than before training (p < 0.05)."
