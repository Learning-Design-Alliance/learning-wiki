# Early implanted children performed significantly better on the word-learning task than late implanted children

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A K-means split of 20 CI children into early (7.6–13.6 months at switch-on) and late (16.0–21.4 months) groups showed a significant Stimulus Type × Implant Group interaction favoring the early group. [→ Derek M. Houston 2012](#derek-m-houston-2012)

## Evidence

### Derek M. Houston 2012

Derek M. Houston, Jessica Stewart, Aaron Moberly, George Hollich, and Richard T. Miyamoto. (2012). Word learning in deaf children with cochlear implants: effects of early auditory experience. Dev Sci. 2012 May ; 15(3): 448–461. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2012.01140.x

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA comparing early implanted (n = 12) and late implanted (n = 8) groups with similar degrees of hearing loss. There was a main effect of Stimulus Type (F(1, 18) = 6.37, p = .02, ηp2 = .26) and a Stimulus Type × Implant Group interaction (F(1, 18) = 10.39, p = .002, ηp2 = .43).
