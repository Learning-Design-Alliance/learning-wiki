## Evidence

### Araiza-Alba 2021

Araiza-Alba, P., Keane, T., Chen, W. S., & Kaufman, J. (2021). Immersive virtual reality as a tool to learn problem-solving skills. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.104121

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A randomized experiment with 120 children aged 7–9.9 years assigned to IVR, tablet, or board game conditions. The IVR group showed a higher completion rate: "the percentage of children who completed the problem-solving game was higher in the IVR condition (77.5%)".

> "the percentage of children who completed the problem-solving game was higher in the IVR condition (77.5%), compared with those in the tablet (32.5%) or board game (30%) conditions"
