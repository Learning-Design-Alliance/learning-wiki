# At age 3, improvement in knowledge of the environment is associated with improvement in all psychomotor dimensions, though some associations are negative

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Among 3-year-olds, improvement in KE has a statistically significant effect on all eight psychomotor dimensions, with positive associations for P (OR = 1.43), LC (OR = 2.59), HC (OR = 8.89), BSA (OR = 2.97), and BSoA (OR = 1.69), and negative associations for L (OR = 0.26), B (OR = 0.60), and AC (OR = 0.79). Logistic regression for 3-year-olds (Table 4) on the KE outcome; printed odds ratios include negative associations OR = 0.26, OR = 0.60, and OR = 0.79, and the largest positive association OR = 8.89 for hand coordination.

> "As for improvement in KE, it has a statistically significant effect on all dimensions of psychomotor skills. Thus, children who improve in L (OR = 0.26, p < 0.001), B (OR = 0.60, p < 0.001), and AC (OR = 0.79, p < 0.001) are less likely to improve in KE"
