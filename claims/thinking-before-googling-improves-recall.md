## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Undergraduate participants who generated answers to brainstorming questions before an online search recalled the searched information significantly better than those who searched immediately, after controlling for gender, prior knowledge, and pre-search motivation. [→ Ju Ae Kim 2026](#ju-ae-kim-2026)

## Evidence

### Ju Ae Kim 2026

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Randomized two-condition experiment with 104 undergraduates (52 per group) searching 15 modern pentathlon questions online for 10 minutes. In the MANCOVA-followed ANCOVA, recall favored thinking-before-googling (adjusted M = 11.13, SE = .31) over googling-only (adjusted M = 10.01, SE = .31), with a printed effect size of ηp² = .06 (F(1,98) = 6.46, p = .013).

> "the effect of condition was significant only on participants’ recall performance, F(1,98) = 6.46, p =.013, ηp 2 =.06. No significant differences emerged in the estimations of performance and study time or cognitive self-esteem between the two groups."
