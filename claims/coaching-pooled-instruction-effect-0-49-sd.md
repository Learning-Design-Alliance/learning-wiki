## Subclaims

`q4 i2` Across causal studies of teacher coaching, the pooled effect on teachers' instructional practice is 0.49 SD, with substantial variability across programs (random-effect SD 0.33). [→ Kraft 2018](#kraft-2018)

## Evidence

### Kraft 2018

Kraft, M.A., Blazar, D., Hogan, D. (2018). The effect of teacher coaching on instruction and achievement: A meta-analysis of the causal evidence. Review of Educational Research, 88(4), 547-588. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654318759268

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis (RVE) of 186 instructional effect sizes from 60 causal studies of coaching programs; pooled estimate 0.49 SD with between-program random-effect SD of 0.33 SD, indicating "substantial variability across programs".

> "Across all 43 studies that included a measure of instructional practice as an outcome, we find a pooled effect size of 0.49 standard deviations (SD). The associated standard deviation of the estimated random effect – a measure of the variation in effect sizes across programs – is 0.33 SD"

### Schachner 2024

Schachner, A., Yun, C., Melnick, H., & Barajas, J. (2024). Coaching at scale: A strategy for strengthening the early learning workforce. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/984.909

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The report's literature review attributes this to a recent meta-analysis of "60 experimental or quasi-experimental studies" in P-12 settings, reporting "strong positive effects of coaching on educators' instruction" and modest student-outcome effects. No effect sizes are printed.

> "A recent meta-analysis of 60 experimental or quasi-experimental studies found strong positive effects of coaching on educators’ instruction—with similar impacts of coaching shown for preschool, elementary, middle, and high school teachers—and modest effects of coaching on student outcomes."
