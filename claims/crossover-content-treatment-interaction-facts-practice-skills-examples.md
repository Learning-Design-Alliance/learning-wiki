# In a human experiment, verbatim facts are learned better from practice-only training while general skills are learned better from example-integrated training (cross-over content-treatment interaction)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants learning facts gained more from practice-only (EPPP) training whereas participants learning skills gained more from example-integrated (EPEP) training, a statistically reliable interaction. [→ Rachatasumrit 2023](#rachatasumrit-2023)

## Evidence

### Rachatasumrit 2023

Rachatasumrit, N., Koedinger, K. R., & Carvalho, P. F. (2023). Evidence and theory for why the best example-problem ratio to optimize learning gain depends on knowledge content. https://osf.io/29wpe [doi:10.31234/osf.io/eyxfw](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/eyxfw)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1: 95 Mechanical Turk participants randomly assigned to EPPP or EPEP training of facts or skills, pretest-posttest with trivia-filled retention interval. The interaction was statistically reliable, β = 0.41, t (124.53) = 2.09, p = .038, d = 0.38, in a mixed-effects model controlling for pretest.

> "for facts (on left in panel a) Practice-Only (blue bar) produces higher learning gains than does Study-Practice (orange bar) whereas for skills (on right in a) Study-Practice produces higher learning gain (orange) than does Practice-Only (blue)"
