Boetje, J., Sichterman, B., Van Ginkel, S., Smakman, M., & Versendaal, J. (2026). Design Principles for Developing Information Problem Solving Competence in Higher Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543251338057

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Descriptive analysis of the 69 included articles' outcome measures. The review reports that "the included studies primarily focused on assessing short-term knowledge and skills in searching and selecting information," underrepresenting other IPS constituent competencies.

> "the included studies primarily focused on assessing short-term knowledge and skills in searching and selecting information, while other competencies critical to IPS, such as defining the problem, processing, and presenting information, were less emphasized"

### Boetje 2026 (2)

Boetje, J., Sichterman, B., Van Ginkel, S., Smakman, M., & Versendaal, J. (2026). Design Principles for Developing Information Problem Solving Competence in Higher Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543251338057

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review's assessment-strategy analysis identifies "the so-called library bias" and a second bias concerning ecological validity, stemming from reliance on self-assessment tests and quick obsoletion of IPS tests, limiting generalizability to real-world settings.

> "The primary bias observed in IPS tests is the so-called library bias—that is, a focus on assessing traditional information search-and-find competencies"
