## Evidence

### Keller 2024

Keller, N. E., Salvi, C., Leiker, E. K., Gruber, M. J. & Dunsmoor, J. E. (2024). States of epistemic curiosity interfere with memory for incidental scholastic facts. npj Science of Learning 9:22. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00234-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Online between-subjects experiment (N = 239 across three task versions) testing memory for scholastic facts paired with high- vs. low-curiosity trivia. The omnibus ANOVA revealed "a main effect of curiosity category" and a task-version effect, "but no interaction (p = 0.788)"; the article prints generalized eta-squared values but the analysis is reported via test statistics.

> "revealed a main effect of task version (F(2, 236) = 4.80,p = 0.009,η2G = 0.036) and a main effect of curiosity category (F (1, 236) = 25.05, p < 0.001,η2G = 0.009), but no interaction (p = 0.788)."

### Keller 2024 (2)

Keller, N. E., Salvi, C., Leiker, E. K., Gruber, M. J. & Dunsmoor, J. E. (2024). States of epistemic curiosity interfere with memory for incidental scholastic facts. npj Science of Learning 9:22. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00234-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc paired t-tests within each of the three task versions of the online experiment. Memory was "significantly lower for scholastic facts in the high as compared to the low curiosity condition", e.g. Early Anticipation t(94) = −2.940, p = 0.004. The article reports a statistically significant decrease with no printed standardized effect size.

> "Post hoc paired t-tests revealed that within all versions of the task, memory was significantly lower for scholastic facts in the high as compared to the low curiosity condition (Early Anticipation: t(94) = −2.940, p = 0.004, 95% CI [ −0.060, −0.012];"
