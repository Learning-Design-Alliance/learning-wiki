`q2 i?` In some studies, students reported high satisfaction with VDTs even when post-test score differences did not reach statistical significance (e.g., p = 0.0979 in Anand et al.). [→ Telecan Teodora 2025](#telecan-teodora-2025)

## Evidence

### Telecan Teodora 2025

Telecan Teodora, Capraș Roxana-Denisa, Filip Gabriela Adriana, Bonea Maria, Crivii Carmen-Bianca. (2025). Dissection in the 21st century: virtual tables versus traditional methods and their influence on medical students' perception – a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07946-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In Anand et al.'s 3-hour neuroanatomy session study, the Anatomage group reported high satisfaction, but "the difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.0979)", suggesting perceived utility did not directly correspond to a measurable academic advantage.

> "while their post-test scores were slightly higher than those in the cadaver group (12.5 versus 11.73), the difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.0979)."
