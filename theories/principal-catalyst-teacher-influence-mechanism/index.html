Dual-support mechanism: deep principal leadership catalyzes collective instructional work while teacher influence makes it meaningful¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 5 studies (3 associational, 1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report proposes an explanatory account of how the three key conditions work together in small schools. Deep principal leadership "acts as a catalyst for initiating and sustaining developmental practices," organizing and monitoring collective improvement work such as peer observation models and grade-level expectation development. Teacher influence operates differently: it "helps create a context in which developmental practices are both meaningful and relevant to teachers' classroom responsibilities," so teachers actively shape rather than merely comply with improvement work. The account explains why schools with only one support did not achieve strong course performance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Both deep principal leadership and teacher influence must be present alongside developmental practices for the mechanism to be associated with strong outcomes

Constraints¶

  • Derived from qualitative interviews and focus groups in a ten-school sample; the report presents it as an interpretation of how the conditions connect, not a tested causal model

Target Learners¶

  • first-time freshmen in small urban high schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • improving freshman-year course performance and on-track rates through school-wide instructional improvement
