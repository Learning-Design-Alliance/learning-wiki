# Cognitive Flexibility Theory and its five instructional principles for complex, ill-structured knowledge domains

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Cognitive flexibility theory (CFT) is a constructivist theory of designing instruction for case-based learning, proposed by Spiro and co-workers to address acquisition and transfer of complex and ill-structured knowledge. The article applies five instructional principles: multiple knowledge representations; linking abstract concepts to multiple case examples; early introduction of domain complexity; stressing the web-like interrelated nature of knowledge; and recommending that learners assemble knowledge from previously learned sources for new situations. The study operationalized these principles through hypertext systems in an online Flash course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Application requires hypertext systems able to realize links between intra and inter pages to design complex online courses with hierarchical navigation
#### Constraints
- The article states implementation of CFT is not a simple issue to exploit, and the theory addresses complex and ill-structured knowledge domains rather than all content

### Target Learners
- first-level information technology diploma students at a high institute of technological studies

### Target Learning Objectives
- acquisition and transfer of complex, ill-structured domain knowledge (multimedia development skills)
