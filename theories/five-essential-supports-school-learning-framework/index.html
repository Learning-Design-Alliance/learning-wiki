Five essential supports framework for organizing school improvement self-analysis¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report organizes survey results according to five essential supports for student learning: School Leadership; Parent and Community Partnerships; Student-Centered Learning Climate; Professional Development and Collaboration; and Quality Instructional Program. Each support involves a number of best practices, and for most practices one or two scales from student or teacher responses summarize the state of the practice in a school. The framework was deliberately tailored so individualized school reports could serve as a companion to a district self-analysis guide.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Survey data from students and teachers sufficient to build one or two scales per best practice

Constraints¶

  • The report notes the Charting Reform surveys were not originally designed to relate to the Three-Tiered Process, and for seven of the practices there are no survey data

Target Learners¶

  • elementary and high school students in Chicago public schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • school improvement in the conditions supporting student learning

Claims¶

  • The Charting Reform surveys reached 266 elementary schools and 40 high schools in Chicago in Spring 1994 [+W]
  • Teacher views of Local School Council contribution differ sharply between top and bottom quartile schools [+W]
  • Students' reports of parental support for learning are far higher in top quartile schools, where two-thirds report moderate or very supportive parents [+W]
  • Principal leadership regard is nearly unanimous in top quartile schools but low for about 40 percent of teachers in bottom quartile schools [+W]
  • Most students, even in bottom quartile schools, report feeling mostly safe at school [+W]
  • Teacher influence over school decisions is minimal or limited for most teachers in bottom quartile schools but extensive or moderate for most in top quartile schools [+W]
