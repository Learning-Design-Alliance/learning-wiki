# Retrieval-practice account of why HOCS clicker questions benefit LOCS exam performance

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article explains its counterintuitive finding with a retrieval-practice mechanism: when students answer higher-order clicker questions, they must "practice recalling factual material in order to “solve” the problem", thereby encoding low-level information. This repeated retrieval of factual content during HOCS questioning strengthens recall of declarative knowledge on later exams, consistent with the Bloom taxonomy principle that higher-cognitive-level processes subsume lower-level processes. The account explains why HOCS clickers helped LOCS but not HOCS exam questions.
