Theories

Five defining characteristics of algorithms: finite, input, outputs, effective, definiteness¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The deck presents a framework, attributed to Shuchi Grover, defining algorithms as "precise step-by-step plans or procedures to meet an end goal or to solve a problem" and algorithmic thinking as the skill of developing them. It enumerates five characteristics: finiteness (must terminate after a finite number of steps), input (zero or more inputs), outputs (one or more outputs), effectiveness, and definiteness, where each step is "precisely defined" and carried out in a rigorous and unambiguous way.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An algorithm must terminate after a finite number of steps and each step must be precisely and unambiguously defined

  • The framework is presented for K-12 classroom use, citing Grover's Computer Science in K-12 handbook and Knuth's The Art of Computer Programming
