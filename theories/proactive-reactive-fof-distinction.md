# Proactive vs. reactive focus on form: two timing alternatives for grammar attention within communicative teaching

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents the proactive/reactive distinction (attributed to Doughty and Williams, 1998) as the organizing taxonomy for timing of focus on form within communicative language teaching. Proactive or preemptive FOF is preplanned instruction on forms chosen in advance, where "the teacher chooses a form in advance to present to students in order to help them complete a communicative task," with form choice driven by learners' communicative needs rather than an imposed syllabus. Reactive FOF is a responsive intervention reacting to learners' errors during communication, typically via corrective feedback. The study operationalizes both as treatments and compares their efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both approaches require a communicative context; reactive FOF requires teachers to notice pervasive errors and have techniques for drawing learners' attention to them
#### Constraints
- Proactive form choice is not centered on a set of language structures imposed by the syllabus but on learners' communicative needs, individual learner differences, developmental sequences, and L1 influences

### Target Learners
- adult EFL learners at beginner and upper-intermediate levels

### Target Learning Objectives
- grammatical accuracy and internalization of target grammatical structures
