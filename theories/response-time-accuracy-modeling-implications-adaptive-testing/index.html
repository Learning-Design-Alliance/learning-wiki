Incorporating response time into measurement models for adaptive testing must account for acceleration and idiosyncratic within-person time-accuracy variation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article advances an account of how response time behavior should enter measurement models in adaptive testing. It identifies two behaviors from a large adaptive assessment data set: response acceleration, "a reduction in response time for responses that occur later in the assessment," and within-person variation in the time-accuracy association. Because these behaviors are heterogeneous and idiosyncratic, the authors argue they carry "implications for models that incorporate response time and accuracy."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Models incorporating response time and accuracy must accommodate heterogeneous acceleration by estimated ability and inconsistent within-person time-accuracy associations, as the article states these findings have such implications.

Constraints¶

  • The evidence base is a single large data set from the adaptive NWEA MAP Growth Reading Assessment; the authors note their approach "may be useful in other studies of adaptive testing data," implying generalization beyond this setting is not established.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students taking adaptive interim reading assessments

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Accurate measurement of reading achievement in adaptive testing contexts
