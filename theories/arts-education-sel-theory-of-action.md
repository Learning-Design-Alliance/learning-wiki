# Theory of action: art practices carry social-emotional components that, cultivated over time, build social-emotional competencies

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report's central framework holds that each large-scale art education process is composed of many small-scale art practices, and that each practice simultaneously contains a social-emotional component. As the authors state, "Each art practice has a social-emotional component; it is simultaneously an arts activity AND a social-emotional activity." Repeated social-emotional components accumulate into long-term social-emotional competencies, just as repeated art practices build art competencies. The framework is offered to help educators design arts experiences that intentionally leverage social-emotional development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must be intentional in shaping the social-emotional contexts created through arts lessons, since effects occur regardless of instructor intent
#### Constraints
- The report notes effects can be negative as well as positive, and outcomes varied among participants and across settings

### Target Learners
- K–12 students in arts education settings

### Target Learning Objectives
- Social-emotional competencies such as self-management, interpersonal skills, and self-expression and identity
