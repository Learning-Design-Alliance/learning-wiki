# Three-segment school growth-status classification based on confidence intervals around school-level growth estimates

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study formulates a segmentation approach that classifies schools into three growth statuses using school-level growth estimates from a three-level hierarchical linear model. A confidence interval based on two standard errors is built for each school and for the average; a school is an "Overperformer when the lower bound of the school-level CI was larger than the upper bound of the average CI", an underperformer when the reverse holds, and average when the CIs overlap. This yielded 14%/13% overperformers, 73%/74% average, and 13%/13% underperformers in Grades 1/2.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-level growth estimates from a three-level hierarchical linear growth model fitted to the prior study's data
- Confidence intervals constructed from two standard errors for each school and the average
#### Constraints
- Classification reflects growth relative to the average, not absolute achievement; segment distributions were reported for Grades 1 and 2 only

### Target Learners
- Grade 1 and Grade 2 elementary students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Elementary mathematics learning growth
