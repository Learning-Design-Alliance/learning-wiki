# Dual nature of CRA learning: interdependent cognitive skills and dispositions

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide holds that learning in CRA involves both cognitive skills (understanding and applying knowledge, such as statistical procedures for identifying item bias) and dispositions (humility, empathy, ethical sensitivity, openness to multiple ways of knowing), which rely on metacognitive awareness. Cognitive objectives are organized by Bloom's six levels in a resource bank, while dispositional objectives adapt Bloom's affective domain into four categories: Awareness and Recognition, Reflection and Engagement, Advocacy and Prioritization, and Integration and Praxis. The guide stresses both are "essential and interdependent—and both must be named, nurtured, and assessed."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be intentional about cultivating dispositions, not only cognitive skills; dispositional objectives should allow multiple ways of expression
#### Constraints
- The guide acknowledges Bloom's framework has been critiqued for its rigid hierarchical structure and does not intend learning to follow a linear path

### Target Learners
- graduate students training to become measurement and assessment professionals

### Target Learning Objectives
- acquire cognitive skills for culturally responsive assessment
- cultivate dispositions of humility, empathy, and reflexivity
