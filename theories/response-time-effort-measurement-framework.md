# Response-time-based measurement of test-taking effort as a validity concern

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article situates item-level effort measurement within growing concern that unmotivated test taking threatens test performance and score validity. It frames response time as the basis for measuring test-taking effort, first through RTE for selected-response items and then through a general process extending to other item types and behaviors such as omitted or constructed responses. The framework aims to support detection of non-effortful test taking and individual score validation in computer-based assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Item response time data from computer-based test delivery.
- Item types and test-taking behaviors must be classifiable so that effort can be inferred per response.
#### Constraints
- RTE applies only to selected-response items that must be answered before advancing; the general process is presented as an illustration rather than fully validated in the available text.

### Target Learners
- test takers on computer-based assessments

### Target Learning Objectives
- valid interpretation of achievement scores
