# First principles of instruction: a cycle of activation, demonstration, application, and integration in the context of real-world tasks

## Description
The article builds on previously identified first principles of instruction, which "describe a cycle of instructional phases consisting of activation, demonstration, application, and integration all in the context of real world problems or tasks." These phases are best implemented around whole tasks, and the paper's task-centered strategy is presented as a systematic way to realize them, emphasizing demonstration (worked examples) and application at both the component and whole-task levels.
