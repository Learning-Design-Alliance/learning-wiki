Zone of Proximal Development: tasks a child cannot yet do alone but can do with help¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article defines the zone of proximal development as "The range of task that a child cannot yet perform alone but can perform with help and guidance from others." It distinguishes two ability levels: actual developmental level, the upper limit of tasks a child can accomplish alone, and level of potential development, the upper limit of tasks a child can accomplish with the assistance of a more competent individual. The ZPD changes over time as tasks are mastered, sets a limit on what a child is cognitively capable of learning, and excludes impossible tasks that cannot be done even with assistance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assistance from a more competent individual for tasks within the zone; tasks must not be impossible even with assistance

Constraints¶

  • Impossible tasks that a child cannot do even with assistance are not beneficial; the ZPD sets a limit on what a child is cognitively capable of learning

Target Learners¶

  • children
  • early childhood learners

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • accomplishing challenging tasks beyond current independent ability

Claims¶

  • Challenging tasks within the child's capability promote maximum cognitive growth, per the account presented [+W]
