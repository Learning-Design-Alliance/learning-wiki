Four critical parental supports for adolescents' educational success¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The brief presents a framework of four supports present when adolescents do better and stay in school longer: parents talking with their child about education and providing day-to-day monitoring of schoolwork; parents setting and holding high expectations; parents helping the child develop problem-solving and decision-making skills; and parents having knowledge of what their child is doing in school so that teachers and parents share a common understanding of the school's goals. Adolescents do best when parents play multiple roles as "teachers, supporters, advocates, and decision makers."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parents need information and resources from schools to understand what they can do to promote their child's success

Constraints¶

  • The extent to which parents play these roles often declines as students enter high school, requiring deliberate school outreach to sustain them

Target Learners¶

  • adolescents in high school

Target Learning Objectives¶
