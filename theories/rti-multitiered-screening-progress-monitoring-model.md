# Response-to-intervention multitiered support model with universal screening and progress monitoring as its assessment components

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The article describes the response-to-intervention (RTI) model as a multitiered framework for student support aimed at early identification of struggling students, typically with three tiers: Tier 3 is the most intense intervention for an estimated 5-10% of students, Tier 2 provides targeted less intensive intervention for 5-15%, and Tier 1 is core instruction for 80-90%. Within this model, "Universal screening identifies at-risk students in need of intervention, whereas progress monitoring tracks the learning progress of students who are already identified as at-risk for learning difficulty." The article uses this framework to motivate establishing technically sound screening benchmarks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A technically sound assessment system; the article states an RTI model cannot sustain itself if cut scores lack adequate classification accuracy
#### Constraints
- The article distinguishes universal screening cut scores from Read by Grade 3 cut scores, which are tied to state proficiency standards and correspond to much higher percentiles

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in reading and mathematics, particularly those at risk of severe learning difficulties

### Target Learning Objectives
- Early identification of students at risk for academic difficulty and appropriate tiered intervention placement
