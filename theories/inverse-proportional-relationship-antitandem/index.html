Inverse proportional relationships as quantities scaling in anti-tandem, y = r/x¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The paper extends the framework to inverse variation: 'Two measurable quantities in a scenario are said to be in an inverse proportional relationship if changing the measure of one quantity by a factor k causes the measure of the other to change by a factor 1/k.' It shows such data satisfies y = r/x, illustrated by volunteers stuffing envelopes, cake sharing, window washing, and the cats-and-rats teaser.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners must recognize which quantity varies inversely with which, by common sense about the scenario

Constraints¶

  • The paper treats inverse variation as a distinct relationship from direct proportionality, requiring anti-tandem rather than tandem scaling

Target Learners¶

  • middle grades and secondary mathematics students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • distinguish direct from inverse proportional relationships and reason with y = r/x
