Modelability: designing learning ecosystems to support generalizable predictive models via three principles¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Modelability is defined as "the extent to which a system is designed to support the development of models that are generalizable". The article argues a highly modelable system includes three features: valid and interpretable measurements, scalable and stable implementation, and a collaborative research-practice-technology ecosystem. Rather than improving algorithms post hoc, the approach improves the affordances of the world being modeled, addressing generalizability at its source.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Systems must generate valid and interpretable measurements, be implemented at scale with consistency, and be sustained through collaboration among researchers, practitioners, and developers.

Constraints¶

  • Algorithmic improvements alone can only enhance generalizability within the constraints of the data itself; highly context-dependent training data limits any model.

Target Learners¶

  • students in online and blended higher education and K-12 courses

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • predicting learning outcomes such as course grades to enable early intervention

Related Theories¶

  • CourseKata fully instrumented online textbook platform
