LVP model of impact of designing for learner variability in edtech¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report proposes a theory-of-change model linking implementation of a suite of research-based features on the ReadWorks platform to teacher awareness, student use of features, increased student engagement, and increased student performance. The model treats learner variability as the starting point: because students differ in factors like attention, working memory, vocabulary, and vision, a suite of optional supports lets each student select what they need in the moment rather than all students receiving the same support. The partnership investigated the validity of the model through a mix of qualitative and quantitative research methods at three levels: a national survey, district-level classroom use, and feature-level analysis.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires implementation of research-based features, teacher awareness of them, and student-facing availability so students can choose supports.

Constraints¶

  • The report notes it cannot make claims about the direction of observed relationships due to the observational nature of the study, and more research is needed to determine the true nature of these relationships.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students with varying strengths and challenges

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Reading comprehension
  • Engagement with assignments

Claims¶

  • In the district study, teachers assigned articles above students' STAR Lexile levels (about 375 points higher on average), and greater STAR growth related to completing more, harder, and more accurate ReadWorks assignments [+W]
  • Changing the audio default from opt-in to opt-out raised audio availability from 72% to 99% of assignments and student audio use from 68% to 84% [+W]
  • Feature use related to a slight increase in multiple choice accuracy, larger for lower-Lexile students and for easier articles [+W]
  • Feature use was widespread across student groups (92% of students used at least one feature; 75% of assignments included feature use), with no differences by STAR score, special education status, or income, but substantial variation across teachers [+W]
  • Using at least one feature raised students' multiple choice question attempt rate to 89 percent and eliminated the drop on hard assignments, with a significant feature-use × difficulty interaction [+W]
  • Teachers who tried ReadWorks features reported high intent to assign more and higher-level articles (89% and 82%) [+W]
  • Teachers reacted unanimously positively to new audio supports on the ReadWorks platform and offered them to all students, not only struggling readers [+W]
