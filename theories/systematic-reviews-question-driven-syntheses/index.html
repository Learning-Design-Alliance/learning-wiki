Systematic reviews as question-driven syntheses distinct from simple research summaries¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article defines systematic reviews as a distinct genre of research synthesis. It states that "It is important to define what we mean by a systematic review to distinguish it from simple summaries of research", and that "Systematic reviews are designed to answer a question and prevent the information overload that decision makers at all levels may face." The framework positions the systematic review as a purposeful, question-anchored alternative to ad hoc summaries, used to support evidence-based decision making at all levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The review must be designed to answer a defined question rather than simply summarize research.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Decision makers at all levels who use research evidence

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Interpreting and applying research evidence to practice and policy decisions

Claims¶

  • Decision makers face research summaries whose findings point in different directions, raising questions about study quality and comparability [+W]
  • Systematic reviews are designed to answer a question and prevent information overload for decision makers [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Weigh explicit considerations before deciding to fund or conduct a systematic review
  • Increase the supply of high quality systematic reviews so decision makers have more useful evidence summaries
