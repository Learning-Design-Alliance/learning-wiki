Reflective practice as a process for refining teaching craft through coached reflection on experience¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Reflective practice, introduced by Schon in 1987, is a critical process for refining one's artistry or craft in a discipline. As the digest defines it, "reflective practice involves thoughtfully considering one's own experiences in applying knowledge to practice while being coached by professionals in the discipline." It was recommended as a way for beginners to recognize consonance between their own practices and those of successful practitioners, and many teacher education programs were subsequently designed around it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Coaching by professionals in the discipline who help beginners connect their experiences to practice

Constraints¶

  • Later researchers cautioned that programs incorporating reflective practice focused on process while sacrificing important content in teacher education

Target Learners¶

  • Pre-service and in-service teachers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Refining teaching craft and artistry through reflection on experience

Claims¶

  • Reflective practice yields deeper understanding of teachers' own teaching style and greater classroom effectiveness [+W]
  • Peer reflective groups encourage student teachers to challenge existing theories and model collaborative professional development [+W]
