# The megastudy: a massive field experiment comparing many interventions in one population on one objectively measured outcome

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces the megastudy as a methodological innovation for applied behavioural science: "a massive field experiment in which the effects of many different interventions are compared in the same population on the same objectively measured outcome for the same duration." It was created "to address this limitation and accelerate the pace of discovery" arising from the lack of comparability of individual investigations that test different ideas in different samples with different outcomes and time intervals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Many independent scientist teams designing interventions that are deployed in the same population on the same objectively measured outcome for the same duration.
#### Constraints
- The article motivates the method by the lack of comparability of typical individual investigations, which test different intervention ideas in different samples using different outcomes over different time intervals.

### Target Learners
- Adult members of an American fitness chain (the demonstrated population)

### Target Learning Objectives
- Behaviour change, here encouraging sustained physical exercise habits
