Four types of practice: deliberate, purposeful, structured, and naive¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on Ericsson and Pool (2016) and Ericsson (2020), the article presents a taxonomy of four practice forms. The article states that "there are four main types of practice: deliberate, purposeful, structured, and naïve." Deliberate practice requires an instructor/coach who assesses performance, proposes objectives, and provides exercises affording immediate feedback, repetition, and enhancement; purposeful practice is goal-directed and teacher-guided but does not require a teacher's presence; structured practice comprises teacher-planned group activities not customized to individual levels; naive practice is extended engagement emphasizing time accumulation without specific goals. The taxonomy underpins the framework's distinction between classroom DP and out-of-class PP.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Deliberate practice requires the involvement of an instructor/coach who can assess existing performance, propose suitable objectives, and provide training exercises with immediate feedback and repetition

Constraints¶

  • Structured practice activities are not customized to individual competency levels and offer limited opportunities for targeted performance improvement; naive practice, such as passively listening to or watching target-language media, is suggested to do little to promote language competence in many L2 classrooms

Target Learners¶

  • Second language learners across proficiency levels

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Distinguishing quality of practice to design effective language learning protocols

Claims¶

  • Accumulated deliberate practice is associated with performance, explaining a domain-dependent share of its variance: about a quarter in games, 4% in education and under 1% in professions [+W]
  • Deliberate practice can expand short-term memory capacity beyond the conventional seven-item limit [+W]
  • Historical increases in peak performance contradict fixed innate upper limits [+W]
