Cummins' theoretical framework relating language proficiency to academic achievement via threshold and interdependence hypotheses¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Cummins proposes that a framework relating language proficiency to academic achievement is needed to set rational entry/exit criteria for bilingual programs. Two premises form its foundation: the threshold hypothesis (two levels of linguistic proficiency, below which bilingualism brings cognitive disadvantages and above which benefits) and the interdependence hypothesis (L1 and L2 academic proficiencies develop interdependently). The framework distinguishes surface fluency from cognitively and academically related aspects of proficiency, later labeled BICS and CALP, and holds that academic skills in L1 and L2 are interdependent through a common underlying proficiency. The article notes the framework 'has need for empirical confirmation'.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Empirical confirmation of the framework's predictions, which the author acknowledges is still needed

Constraints¶

  • The author avoids the acronyms BICS and CALP because the distinctions have been misinterpreted, although the underlying meaning remains the same

Target Learners¶

  • Bilingual and language minority students in elementary and secondary programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Development of academic language proficiency in L1 and L2
  • Rational entry/exit decisions for bilingual program placement

Claims¶

  • Available language proficiency tests vary enormously in what they purport to measure, reflecting lack of consensus on the construct [+W]
  • L1 and L2 academic proficiencies are developmentally interdependent, so instruction in either language can build underlying academic ability [+W]
  • Teacher ratings predicted reading achievement better than common language proficiency tests (41% vs 0-4% additional variance) [+W]
  • Assuming conversational fluency indicates English proficiency has led to bilingual children being misdiagnosed as learning disabled or retarded and to premature exit from support programs [+W]
  • L1 and L2 CALP are interdependent: strong promotion of L1 literacy in the early grades entails no adverse consequences for English [+W]
  • Critics argue Cummins' framework neglects the sociocultural context and CALP may index acculturation rather than cognitive ability [-W]
