# Criterion-referenced (mastery) testing as the advocated grading framework

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The essay's central framework: each student is "judged solely in relation to these pre-established criteria" — the course objectives — "and independently of the performance of any other student." Students who have not yet learned are merely in the process of learning; slower learners are helped, not stigmatized, and cooperation is safe because class rank is not at stake. A pass-fail cutoff remains arbitrary, but repeated opportunities to demonstrate mastery remove its sting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Criteria or behavioral objectives for the course must be established, which the author says is easy for logically sequenced subject matter but harder for courses aiming at creative products or novel solutions.
#### Constraints
- The pass-fail cutoff remains arbitrary, since adjacent scores around it are not significantly different; the author argues this is benign only because students get other opportunities to demonstrate achievement.

### Target Learners
- students at any educational level, elementary through college

### Target Learning Objectives
- mastery of pre-established course objectives
