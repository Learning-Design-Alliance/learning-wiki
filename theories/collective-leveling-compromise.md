# Collective leveling as a proposed compromise for pacing group instruction

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Collective leveling is Arlin's (1984) proposal, discussed and rejected in this article, that a predetermined level for the pace and rigor of group instruction should be set near the middle of students' individual learning rates. Arlin argues it "functions as anunconscious means to establish an equilibrium between theapparently conflicting ideals of equal time and equalachievement". The article's findings do not lend credence to the compromise: trained teachers instead reported goals of providing a greater range of enriched learning activities rather than establishing a manageable achievement level.
