# Two-segment customer taxonomy for the adult learning ed-tech market

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide organizes the adult learning market around two customer segments. The primary segment is learning providers — community colleges, K-12 districts, community-based organizations, workforce development centers, libraries, and correctional institutions — which are "well-established and offer an effective way to reach adult learners" and lend credibility to new products. The secondary segment is independent learners, a direct-to-learner market the guide calls "yet unproven" but showing indicators of potential. The guide uses this taxonomy to structure its analysis of objectives, pain points, and value propositions for each segment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Entrepreneurs must understand each segment's distinct objectives and pain points before defining value propositions, as the guide's customer objectives table shows segments differ in goals such as retention, job skills, and recidivism reduction
#### Constraints
- The guide states the direct-to-learner model is yet unproven and that not enough research exists to know what approaches would work with which independent learners

### Target Learners
- low-skilled adult learners in the United States

### Target Learning Objectives
- adult basic education, ESL, high school equivalency preparation, computer literacy, and job-based skills
