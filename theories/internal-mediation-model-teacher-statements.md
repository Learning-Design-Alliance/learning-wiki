# Internal mediation model of teacher statements, self-talk and self-concepts

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The article articulates an internal mediation model in which the influence of teachers' statements on children's academic self-concepts follows a pathway: the student receives specific performance feedback from the teacher, internalizes it as self-talk, and then generalizes the self-talk to form or modify the relevant academic self-concept. The article attributes the model to Craven, Marsh, and Debus (1991), noting its A-B-C steps correspond to the B-C-D steps of Blote's (1995) teacher-expectancy model. The study's SEM results, in which "positive self-talk mediated between perceived teacher praise and reading self-concept", are consistent with this mediation pathway.
