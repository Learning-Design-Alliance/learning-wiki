Feedback loops as purposeful, bidirectional, transformative interactions in education innovation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 design), q1 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report defines feedback loops, citing Baker, Weisgrau, and Bristal Philyaw (2022), as "an interaction between two or more parties that is purposeful, bidirectional, and interactive and transforms the knowledge, actions, or goals of the engaged parties." They are purposeful because they address problems of practice important to practitioners; bidirectional and transformative because information moves among all involved, creating new knowledge, actions, or goals; and iterative because interactions shape the loops themselves. The report positions feedback loops as a building block for more comprehensive partnership models.
