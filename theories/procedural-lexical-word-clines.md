# Three-cline characterization of procedural versus lexical words

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Robinson arranges words on three clines separating procedural from lexical words: indexicality (high, non-monotonic, schema-free versus low, monotonic, schema-specific), lexical content (few versus many fixed features), and distribution (wide, with no clear collocations, versus narrow, with clear collocations). Procedural words like 'do' sit at one pole and specific words like 'anaesthatize' at the other. The clines also distinguish grammatical from content words and correspond to semiotic, semantic and syntactic planes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Distinguishing the word classes relies on criteria of range and coverage across subject areas, since frequency counts of specific fields fail to separate general from specific words
#### Constraints
- The characterization is an analytical description of word properties, not an experimentally tested model

### Target Learners
- second language learners encountering general and technical vocabulary

### Target Learning Objectives
- understanding how general words carry and negotiate the meanings of specific technical words
