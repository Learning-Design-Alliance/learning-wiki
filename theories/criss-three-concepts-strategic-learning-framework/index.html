CRISS Strategic Learning framework: three concepts from cognitive psychology and brain research¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Project CRISS® is grounded in three primary concepts derived from cognitive psychology and brain research: students "monitoring their learning to assess when they have understood content," "integrating new information with prior knowledge," and "being actively involved in the learning process through discussing, writing, organizing information, and analyzing the structure of text to help improve comprehension." Teachers incorporate these concepts through comprehension strategies, and students learn when and how to apply the strategies that work best for them. The CRISS Strategic Learning Plan guides selection of content, setting of learning goals and objectives, assessment of student learning, and planning of instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers model strategies and provide time for guided practice so students can transfer strategies to independent learning situations

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • 3rd- through 12th-grade students in content-area classrooms

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Metacognitive monitoring of comprehension
  • integration of new information with prior knowledge
  • and active engagement through discussing, writing, and organizing information

Claims¶

  • A large randomized trial found no statistically significant effects of Project CRISS® on standardized grade 5 comprehension measures (James-Burdumy et al., 2009) [-W]
  • The WWC rates Project CRISS® as having potentially positive effects on comprehension for adolescent learners, with an average improvement index of +20 percentile points [+W]
  • Project CRISS® produced statistically significant greater free-recall gains than regular instruction in grades 4 and 6 (Horsfall & Santa, 1994) [+W]

Examples¶
