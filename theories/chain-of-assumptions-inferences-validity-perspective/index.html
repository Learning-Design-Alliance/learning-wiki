Chain of assumptions/inferences perspective within an argument-based approach to validity¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article works within an argument-based approach to validity, in which the chain of assumptions and inferences connecting test scores to interpretations and uses is made explicit. The author explores how intended test purpose and test score use are treated "under the chain of assumption/inferences perspective identified within an argument-based approach to validity," using this framework as the lens for the paper's reanalysis of score use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Test purpose and score use must be analyzed as links in an explicit chain of assumptions and inferences, as the argument-based validity tradition prescribes.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • classroom teachers and other intended test users of classroom assessments

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • valid interpretation and effective use of classroom assessment scores in mathematics and science

Claims¶

  • An argument-based approach to test score effectiveness can be illustrated by contrasting effectiveness arguments for interim assessments based on a conventional test blueprint versus one augmented with learning progressions [+W]
  • For classroom assessments of complex constructs such as learning progressions in math and science, test score use is more effectively conceptualized as part of a potential solution to a problem, or 'job-to-be-done' [+W]
  • The lack of guidelines for test score use fails to reflect the ultimate purpose of a test score: helping solve an important problem faced by intended test users [+W]
  • The article argues for shifting from the definition of validity to the concept of effectiveness for classroom assessment score use [+W]
  • Approaches to test score use and test purpose lack well-developed methodological guidelines and established sources of evidence, unlike score interpretation [+W]
  • Interpreting differences in school-aggregate test scores as real differences in school performance raises accuracy concerns even under routine schooling conditions, which the pandemic may exacerbate [+W]
