TFT partnership logic model linking administrator, teacher, and student outcomes across three time horizons¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The partnership's theory of change organizes activities and outcomes into short-term, medium-term, and long-term columns for three actor groups. Short-term outcomes include teachers gaining "pedagogical knowledge and strategies related to fraction computation and rate and ratio"; medium-term outcomes include administrators aligning "district or school practices and PD offerings with recommendations"; the long-term student outcome is solving fraction computation and ratio and rate problems.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Partnership activities must run: implement, refine, and assess usability and feasibility of the TFT, co-develop it, and study short-, medium-, and long-term outcomes

Constraints¶

  • Outcomes are projected in a logic model, not yet measured results

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 6 math students
  • grade 6 math teachers
  • math leaders
  • school and district administrators

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Fraction computation
  • ratio and rate problems
  • instructional alignment with practice guide recommendations

Claims¶

  • The partnership's long-term goal is that students can solve fraction computation and ratio and rate problems [+W]
  • The partnership targets improved teacher instruction in eliciting student thinking, using representations, and focusing on math reasoning [+W]

Examples¶

  • Teaching Fractions Toolkit (TFT) providing grade 6 teacher PD modules, reflection tool, and formative assessment probes
  • Provide administrator and math leader supports to reinforce the fraction trajectory across grades PK–8
  • Co-develop the toolkit with districts and refine it iteratively across cohorts
  • Develop dissemination resources to support acceptability and feasibility of the toolkit approach
