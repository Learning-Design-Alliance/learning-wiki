# Validity, reliability, and fairness of AI outputs as a practical evaluation framework grounded in educational measurement

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a practical framework for evaluating AI-generated outputs in edtech, grounded in educational measurement concepts. Validity asks whether the AI output accurately does what it is designed to do, including face validity and consequential validity; reliability asks whether the AI produces consistent results across repeated uses; fairness asks whether outputs are equally valid across relevant student subgroups. The authors state that "Standards established for educational measurement can serve as a practical framework for AI model evaluation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human-defined standards for what the AI should achieve must anchor the evaluation
#### Constraints
- For open-ended or personalized outputs there is often no clear ground truth; fairness remains under-evaluated due to limited access to demographic data and a lack of agreed-upon measurement standards

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher education students using AI-enabled edtech, especially learners from underserved populations

### Target Learning Objectives
- Accuracy, consistency, and equitable treatment of AI-generated feedback, scoring, and personalization outputs
