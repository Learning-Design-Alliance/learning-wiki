The Compound Polynomial (CP) growth model¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The CP model, developed by Thum and Hauser (2015), is a flexible functional form for describing growth trends with marked seasonal patterns. It combines "a polynomial growth in performance (scores) that occurs within a segment" with "a second polynomial growth model for one or more within-segment growth components," so that the model "includes parameters capturing between- and within-year growth." In this article it is applied in a school evaluation context to estimate school contributions to student growth from fall and spring MAP Growth data across grades two through six, with three levels (time points within students within schools).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires vertically scaled test scores with equal-interval properties and multiple test administrations within the year (e.g., fall and spring) so within- and between-year growth can be estimated on a common scale

Constraints¶

  • The authors state results can be sensitive to how the CP is parameterized, such as the grade at which time is centered
  • The authors state the CP is highly parameterized and involves considerable smoothing, making it likely not optimal when only single-year, discrete estimates of school contributions are needed

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students in grades 2-6

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • math and reading achievement growth
