## Description
Whitehead (1929) called knowledge "inert" when its use stays confined to the setting where it was taught. Renkl, Mandl and Gruber (1996) review why knowledge that a learner seems to have is not used for problem solving, and group the explanations in three:

1. **Metaprocess explanations.** The knowledge is there but access to it fails. In the metacognitive variant the learner lacks conditional knowledge, knowing when and why to use a fact or strategy; in the motivational variant low interest or intrinsic motivation keeps deep-level strategies from being used.
2. **Structure deficit explanations.** The knowledge is not held in a form that allows it to be applied: conceptual understanding is missing, declarative knowledge has not been compiled into procedures (Anderson's ACT\*), knowledge for talking and knowledge for acting are held separately, or knowledge acquired in different contexts is stored in compartments that lack connections.
3. **Situatedness explanations.** Knowledge is fundamentally bound to the context in which it was acquired, so the traditional notions of knowledge and transfer are themselves questioned.

The remedies they discuss are the situated-learning models of the time, cognitive apprenticeship, anchored instruction and random-access instruction, which share principles: learning starts from an interesting problem, knowledge is acquired while it is applied to that problem, and the problem is authentic, complex and ill-defined. They note that one need not adopt a situated view of cognition to expect these models to help.

## Implications

### Context
- School and university learning whose test is use outside the instructional setting: realistic word problems, professional problem solving, everyday life.

### Target Learners
- School pupils and university students; the evidence spans sixth-graders' mathematics and college students' study strategies.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Using learned knowledge to solve problems in new contexts (transfer).
