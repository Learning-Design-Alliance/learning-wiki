Sociocultural theory as a framework for gifted education¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The essay proposes applying Vygotsky's sociocultural theory to gifted education, arguing the field lacks a widely accepted theoretical framework. Learning is framed socially: "The sociocultural perspective suggests that learning is a process of appropriating 'tools for thinking'" made available by social agents acting as guides. The theory is used to critique identification practices and to inform teacher preparation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators must treat social interaction with others as necessary for individual development, not merely as casual socializing

Constraints¶

  • The essay acknowledges that social and cultural forces influence but do not completely explain development

Target Learners¶

  • gifted and talented students in K-12 schooling

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • intellectual development through social interaction and appropriation of cultural tools for thinking

Claims¶

  • A narrative review, citing Ford (2003), attributes the underrepresentation of culturally and linguistically diverse students in gifted education to identification measures biased toward another cultural background [+W]
  • Traditional gifted identification tests cannot fully determine learning potential and omit classroom-learning components [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Vygotsky's sociocultural theory: development is historically situated, culturally determined, and proceeds through internalization of social interaction
  • Sociocultural Theory
  • Zone of Proximal Development as an assessment and instruction tool
  • Zone of Proximal Development: the gap between independent and assisted problem solving

Examples¶

  • Use dynamic assessment to identify gifted learners' potential
  • Apply scaffolding and social group-investigation models in gifted classrooms
  • Prepare novice teachers of the gifted in the sociocultural context of the gifted classroom
  • Use multiple criteria and non-traditional measures to identify diverse gifted learners
