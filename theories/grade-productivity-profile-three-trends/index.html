Grade productivity profile framework: input, output, and learning-gain trends for each school grade¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The guide introduces a value-added framework in which each school grade's productivity profile presents "three inter-related pieces of information for each school grade." Input status is the previous year's matched test scores, output is end-of-year scores for the same stable students, and the gain is the improvement between them. Profiles are built year by year from 1987 to 1996, smoothed with best-fitting trend lines, and used to read school improvement over time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires matching student scores across two consecutive years in the same school, so grades with few matched students (e.g., second and third grades where early-grade testing is sparse) are less informative

Constraints¶

  • Students who move into or out of a school during the academic year do not count in that year's productivity profile

Target Learners¶

  • Chicago public elementary school students in grades two through eight

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Reading and mathematics achievement growth as measured by the Iowa Tests of Basic Skills

Claims¶

  • Productivity profiles in adjacent grades are negatively related, so judging a school from selected grades alone can be very misleading [+W]
  • Judging school improvement requires reading learning gain trends and output trends simultaneously, since either trend monitored alone can mislead [+W]
  • Profile trends labeled up or down are determined by statistical criteria, so those labels denote statistically significant trends [+W]
  • CPS elementary school productivity improvements stalled after 1997, with no evidence of significant productivity growth since then [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Value-added academic productivity indicator estimating each school's contribution to student learning from longitudinal test scores

Examples¶

  • Student-level value-added regression model with teacher dosage variables and student background controls
