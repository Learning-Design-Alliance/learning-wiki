# The Learning Salon five-phase engagement model for district-provider equity collaboration

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Learning Salon model is a structured engagement framework bringing district leaders and BIPOC solution providers together around districts' problems of practice rooted in inequity. Its five phases—Salon Prep, Expert Panel and Problem of Practice Framing, Co-Design Part 1 (Analysis), Solution Strategy Mapping, and Co-Design Part 2 (Exploration), plus Feedback—move from root-cause diagnosis of a district's named problem toward collaborative solution exploration. The report states the model "created an ecosystem" to "Prioritize deep understanding and mapping school districts' problems of practice" and "Invite districts and solution providers to collaborate on solutions to the problems of practice." It was piloted in in-person (4-hour) and virtual (2-hour prep plus 3-hour) variants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Districts must articulate a well-defined problem of practice and commit time and personnel; providers complete pre-work analysis of the district's problem statement
#### Constraints
- Solution Strategy Mapping is exclusive to the in-person model; the first salon's real-time adjustments limited in-session co-design

### Target Learners
- District leaders
- BIPOC education solution providers

### Target Learning Objectives
- Addressing equity challenges facing historically and systematically excluded students through district-provider co-design
