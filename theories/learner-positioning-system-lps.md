# Learner Positioning System (LPS): a GPS-like system positioning each learner with a learner profile, learning map, and resource bank

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper envisions Learner Positioning Systems, analogous to a geographic GPS, that help learners position themselves in their learning. "An effective LPS would include information about the learner; a map of learning topics and progressions; and a bank of programs and resources tied to the learning map and the student's unique characteristics." Students would self-identify strengths, preferences, and challenges, select learning trajectories, and find resources, communities, and programs; the LPS would also help teachers and parents support learners. It is a proposed design concept, not an evaluated artifact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Information about the learner, including quantitative measures of progress, content knowledge, skills, dispositions, learning preferences, and interests
- A map of learning topics and progressions
- A bank of programs and resources tied to the learning map and the student's unique characteristics
#### Constraints
- The LPS is presented as a vision for future development, not an existing or tested system

### Target Learners
- K-12 students with diverse learning needs, including the estimated nearly 50 percent of students with some form of learning diversity

### Target Learning Objectives
- Self-understanding of personal learning characteristics to guide one's own learning
- Selecting appropriate learning trajectories and resources toward learning goals
