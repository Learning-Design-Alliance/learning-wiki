# Contextual Systems Model (CSM): child/family and school/schooling as interrelated systems

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Pianta and Walsh's (1996) Contextual Systems Model, presented in the review as an improvement over earlier models, is based on Ford and Lerner's Developmental System Theory. "The CSM involves two interrelated systems, the Child/Family system and the School/Schooling system", with time as an underlying relation affecting both. The review uses it as the framework for organizing recent studies of minority underachievement in mathematics and argues it reframes at-risk children as an education-wide problem rather than a particular group's problem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attention to relationships between child and family and schooling and the other individuals and institutions involved in schooling, including better communication between the two systems
#### Constraints
- The review notes the model is not related to any subject in particular, and that hazards such as poverty and peer rejection exert a regulatory influence on children's development as an organized system

### Target Learners
- children at risk and, by the review's extension, minority students generally

### Target Learning Objectives
- understanding development and underachievement in school contexts, including mathematics
