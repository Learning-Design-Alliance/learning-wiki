# Reading fluency consists of three components: accuracy, automaticity, and prosody

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review defines reading fluency as the ability to read aloud a text accurately and with natural speed, built from three components. Accuracy is precise word recognition, automaticity is recognizing and decoding words easily, and prosody is reading smoothly with expression and intonation. The article states that "fluency is thought to consist of 3 main components of accuracy, automaticity and prosody", and that describing these elements is essential to understanding how fluency should be addressed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fluent readers must be able to identify words accurately and spontaneously, which requires learning alphabets, letters and sounds, including sight words
#### Constraints
- No valid method has been created to measure students' prosody, although reliable measures exist for accuracy and automaticity

### Target Learners
- EFL and L2 learners developing reading fluency

### Target Learning Objectives
- Oral reading fluency: word recognition accuracy, automaticity, and prosody
