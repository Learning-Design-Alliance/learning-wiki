# Five-category feature taxonomy for classifying ethnic bias in educational texts

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes candidate bias signals into a taxonomy of "five categories: psycholinguistic markers, linguistic abstraction, language valence, ethnic group mentions, and contextual attributes". Psycholinguistic markers (e.g., aggressiveness, emotional stability, self-reference) reflect the author's psychological state; abstraction metrics capture specificity versus generality in descriptions; valence quantifies emotional tone; mentions operationalize ethnic group references via a keyword list of over 1,000 terms; contextual attributes encode discipline and material type. Features were computed for both sentences and surrounding excerpts to make classification more context aware.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Part-of-speech tagging, psycholinguistic ratio computation, and sentiment (VADER) scoring pipelines over sentences and excerpts
- OER metadata for contextual attributes and a keyword list for ethnic group mentions
#### Constraints
- Group references alone do not necessarily indicate bias and are more informative alongside attributes such as valence

### Target Learners
- Higher education students
- Online learners

### Target Learning Objectives
- Interpretable identification of ethnic bias in text-based learning materials
