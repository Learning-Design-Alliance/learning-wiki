# Authentic Intellectual Work framework: construction of knowledge through disciplined inquiry producing products with value beyond school

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The framework defines authentic intellectual work as involving "original application of knowledge and skills, rather than just routine use of facts and procedures." Its three criteria are construction of knowledge (organizing, interpreting, evaluating, or synthesizing prior knowledge to solve novel problems), disciplined inquiry (use of a prior knowledge base, in-depth understanding, and elaborated communication), and production of discourse, products, or performances with utilitarian, aesthetic, or personal "value beyond school." The study adapted this framework, developed by the Center on Organization and Restructuring of Schools, into scoring standards for classroom assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All three criteria are important; an assignment meeting only some criteria falls short of authentic intellectual work as the article's examples show
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied to writing and mathematics assignments in grades three, six, and eight of Chicago elementary schools

### Target Learners
- elementary students in grades 3, 6, and 8 in urban public schools

### Target Learning Objectives
- complex intellectual activity including interpretation, analysis, synthesis, and elaborated communication, alongside basic skills
