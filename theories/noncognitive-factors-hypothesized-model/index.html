Hypothesized model of noncognitive factors acting through behaviors within classroom and socio-cultural context¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review proposes that mindsets give rise to perseverance, which manifests through academic behaviors, which shape academic performance — with academic behaviors as the proximal channel: "academic behaviors are most proximal to one's performance in school," and "virtually all other noncognitive factors work through academic behaviors to affect performance." The model is nested in School and Classroom Context and a larger Socio-Cultural Context including student background characteristics. Arrows are one-way only for simplicity; the authors anticipate reciprocal relationships and that "the actual relationships among these factors are no doubt messier and more complex than indicated in the illustration."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Empirical testing of the relative strengths of paths and the importance of each category controlling for the others, which the authors say is needed

Constraints¶

  • The model is explicitly a simplified hypothesized framework; much existing evidence is correlational rather than causal

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescent students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Course performance and grades

Claims¶

  • Positive academic mindsets increase perseverance and academic behaviors, yielding higher grades; negative mindsets produce a self-defeating cycle [+W]
  • Academic behaviors have the most immediate effect on course grades; directly targeting grit is not an evidenced lever [+W]
  • Best leverage points for improving performance lie in teacher strategies for classroom context, mindsets, and learning strategies [+W]
