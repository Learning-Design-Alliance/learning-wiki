MAP Growth theory of action within a comprehensive assessment system¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report articulates a theory of action positioning MAP Growth as the interim component of a comprehensive assessment system alongside summative and formative assessments. Its premise is that "when MAP Growth is situated in a comprehensive assessment system and used for its intended purposes (to yield information about student learning and enable educators to make data-informed decisions about curriculum and instruction), all students learn." Intended uses are classified as instructional, predictive, or evaluative, with aggregation matched to the level of decision-making.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The theory assumes MAP Growth is used for its intended purposes and combined with other assessment forms and data sources

Constraints¶

  • The report states MAP Growth is neither a summative nor a formative assessment itself, though it can predict summative performance and support formative practice through professional learning

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Data-informed decisions about curriculum and instruction

Claims¶

  • Adaptive testing yields better measurement precision than a fixed-form test of the same length [+W]
  • Linking studies predict proficiency on state summative assessments from MAP Growth scores [+W]
  • MAP Growth reliability coefficients average about 0.95, with lower values in Science and early grades and stronger test-retest reliability over shorter intervals [+W]
