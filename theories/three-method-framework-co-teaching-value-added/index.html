Three-method framework for accounting for co-teaching in value-added models¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes the problem of modeling value added "when the same student is taught the same subject by multiple teachers" into three named methods: the Partial Credit Method, the Teacher Team Method, and the Full Roster Method. The Partial Credit Method apportions responsibility by fraction of year with each teacher; the other two "presume that co-teachers share joint responsibility for the achievement of their shared students." The framework was developed to help states and districts overcome a potential limitation in the use of value added in multiple-measures teacher evaluation systems.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires teacher-student roster links, ideally checked and confirmed by teachers themselves.
  • Applies when a student is taught the same subject by more than one teacher, so teacher effects must be separated across co-teachers.

Constraints¶

  • Developed for value-added teacher evaluation in subjects where students are co-taught; the Partial Credit branch is limited by practical problems.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in districts using value-added teacher evaluation
  • K-12 students taught the same subject by more than one teacher

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Fair and accurate attribution of student achievement growth to teachers under co-teaching
  • Accurate estimation of individual teacher effects on student achievement in value-added accountability systems
