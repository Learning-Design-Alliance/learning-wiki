Information-based approach to setting rapid-guessing thresholds¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 design, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes "a new information-based approach to setting thresholds" for identifying rapid guessing on achievement tests. The approach is presented as an alternative that "does not share the limitations of other methods," specifically those based on rapid-guessing response accuracy or visual inspection of response-time distributions. It is operationalized as a pair of information-based threshold methods evaluated in an empirical comparison study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Threshold methods must be reliable and aligned with test taker behavior for rapid-guessing identification to be effective

Constraints¶

  • Presented as an alternative to accuracy-based and visual-inspection methods, which the article characterizes as having limitations

Target Learners¶

  • test takers of low-stakes achievement tests

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • valid measurement of achievement when test engagement varies

Claims¶

  • Information Based Thresholds More Reliable Than Accuracy Visual Methods [+M]
  • Response-time threshold-setting methods for detecting noneffortful item responses were compared using reading scores from over 728,923 US students in 2,056 schools [+M]
  • Rapid guessing can be identified from item-level response time, and its proper interpretation depends on contextual requirements [+M]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Evaluators should re-estimate IRT scores treating rapid guesses as missing and compare with unadjusted results
