TPACK (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge)¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 26 claims (13 for, 13 mixed) · 14 studies (5 associational, 3 theoretical, 2 causal, 2 design, 1 review, 1 qualitative), q1–q3 · 4 of 14 report an effect size · 26 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TPACK (Koehler & Mishra, 2009) extends Shulman's concept of Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK) — the specialized knowledge of how to teach a particular subject — by adding technology as a third interacting knowledge domain. Teaching with technology is treated as an ill-structured problem: there is no single best way to integrate a given technology into a curriculum, because content, pedagogy, and technology constrain and reshape one another differently in every classroom context.

The framework names three core knowledge domains — Content Knowledge (CK), Pedagogical Knowledge (PK), and Technology Knowledge (TK) — and the knowledge that emerges at their intersections: Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK) (how to teach a specific subject), Technological Content Knowledge (TCK) (how technology and content shape and constrain each other — e.g., how carbon-14 dating changed archaeology), Technological Pedagogical Knowledge (TPK) (how teaching and learning change when a given technology is used in a given way, independent of subject matter), and TPACK itself — the emergent understanding of how to teach a specific subject, using specific pedagogical approaches, with specific technologies, in a specific classroom context. Digital technologies complicate this more than older tools because they are "protean" (usable in many ways), unstable (rapidly changing), and opaque (their inner workings are hidden), unlike a pencil or a chalkboard (Koehler & Mishra, 2009).

TPACK is not additive — a teacher cannot be TPACK-competent by separately mastering content, pedagogy, and technology. What matters is the ability to flexibly navigate the interactions between all three as they play out in a specific, dynamic classroom context; a change in any one factor has to be compensated for by changes in the other two.

Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A specific content area, pedagogical approach, and technology considered together, rather than technology adopted as a generic "add-on"
  • Designer or teacher fluency in reasoning about the interactions among the three domains, not just competence in each domain separately
  • Teachers' knowledge for teaching with technologies must integrate pedagogical knowledge with content and technological knowledge, not subject matter alone

Constraints¶

  • Technology Knowledge is inherently unstable and dates quickly, so TPACK is not a one-time competency but requires ongoing re-integration as tools change
  • Provides a diagnostic lens for why technology integration succeeds or fails, but does not by itself prescribe which technology to choose — see Technology Integration Levels (SAMR / RAT / PIC-RAT) for frameworks that evaluate the effect of a specific choice
  • The discussion notes TPACK appears best at organizing qualitative observations of what teachers learned into categories, and it is less obvious it gives teachers coherence and direction in their long-term growth

Target Learners¶

  • Teachers and instructional designers making decisions about which technology to use for a specific content area and pedagogical goal
  • in-service mathematics teachers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Not a learner-facing objective — a designer/teacher competency: producing technology-integrated instruction that is well-matched to both the content and the pedagogical goal, rather than technology used for its own sake
  • integrating digital technologies as mathematics learning tools
  • transforming teacher knowledge for teaching with technologies

Theory¶

Supporting¶

  • Builds directly on Shulman's (1986, 1987) construct of Pedagogical Content Knowledge, extending it to include technology as a third interacting domain

The evidence by question¶

  • Does a TPACK-based course or programme build teachers' capacity to design technology-integrated instruction, and does the gain last? A 13-week TPACK-based course significantly improves pre-service preschool teachers' instructional design competence compared with traditional instruction [+M]
  • Which teacher knowledge predicts integrating AI into teaching: technology knowledge alone, or knowledge integrated with pedagogy and content, together with beliefs? Review reports that teachers' beliefs, attitudes and TPACK—not AI knowledge alone—mediate AI integration [+M]
  • Does teachers' technology-integration knowledge rise with teaching experience, grade or age, or differ by gender? Teaching experience does not significantly enhance MTES AI-TPACK proficiency, though experienced MTES scored slightly higher on every variable [~M]
  • Does a technology's benefit come from the technology or from the pedagogy it is used within? The educational value of GAI is contingent on pedagogical integration, not on the technology itself [+M]

Related Theories¶

  • TPACK framework (Technological, Pedagogical and Content Knowledge) for teacher technology integration
  • Align technology use with specific learning goals rather than adopting technology for its own sake
  • Five-stage TPACK development trajectory from recognizing to advancing

Related Principles¶

  • Technology Integration Levels (SAMR / RAT / PIC-RAT) — a complementary framework for judging the effect a specific technology choice is having, once a TPACK-informed choice has been made

Examples¶

  • Choosing simulation software over a static diagram because it lets students manipulate variables a way the content and the pedagogical goal (inquiry-based understanding of a process) both call for
  • A whiteboard used differently in a teacher-led lecture (front of room, teacher-controlled) versus a brainstorming session (open to all, focal point for negotiation) — same technology, different TPK depending on pedagogical intent
  • Build educator AI literacy and pedagogical competence alongside student critical engagement with AI-generated content
  • AI Certificate Pedagogical Transformation Effectiveness Evaluation Indicator System (20 indicators, S1–S20)
  • Justify GenAI integration through a when-how-why framework: proven contexts, specific implementation methods, and pedagogical reasoning
  • Four-course online TPACK professional development program (SED 520, 521, 522, 594)
