# Cultural Difference paradigm: underachievement explained by mismatch between school and students' cultures

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review traces this paradigm to Cole and Bruner (1971) building on Labov's finding that what was treated as deficit should be considered difference. It "corresponded to a shift of belief about the source of the problem, from the student and their homes to the school", recasting children as different rather than broken and asking schools and teachers to be more culturally responsive. The review judges it an improvement that nonetheless "assumed a simplistic and static conception of culture" and addressed only one source of the problem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers who see students as culturally different rather than as having an intellectual disease, and who provide environments in which children show skills they already possess before transferring them to new situations
#### Constraints
- The review states it assumes a simplistic and static conception of culture, does not recognize that competence is not easily moved from one situation to the next, and speaks only of the school as the source of the problem

### Target Learners
- children from non-dominant cultures in US schools

### Target Learning Objectives
- mathematics achievement; culturally responsive teaching
