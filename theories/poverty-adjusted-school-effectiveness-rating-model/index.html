Value-added school effectiveness rating model based on poverty-adjusted regression residuals¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A model that rates each school's effectiveness by regressing average school test scores on school poverty level and standardizing the residuals. As the article describes it, "the school effectiveness ratings start by finding regression equations relating average school test scores to the poverty level of each school," and schools are rated by standardized residuals averaged over all tests given in a year. Ratings are relative, following roughly a cumulative normal distribution with mean near zero and standard deviation about .75, and incorporate all systemwide tests in grades three through five. The article uses the model both to identify high-performing schools and as a tool for ex post facto program evaluation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The program under study must be introduced during a period for which ratings are available
  • Enough schools must adopt the program for statistical significance, but not so many that no comparison group remains

Constraints¶

  • Ratings are relative and say nothing about absolute changes in performance
  • A decline in rating could reflect poorer performance by the school or improved performance by the average school

Target Learners¶

  • elementary schools and their students, particularly low-income populations

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • school-level student achievement on standardized tests

Claims¶

  • Target Teach Schools Significant Rating Increase [+M]
  • Direct Instruction Gains Depend On Implementation Support [+M]
  • Sage Schools No Significant Rating Change [+M]
  • Simulation estimates that regression-to-the-mean selection bias inflates apparent Target Teach improvement by about .03 per year, leaving a smaller but still significant gain [~W]

Examples¶
