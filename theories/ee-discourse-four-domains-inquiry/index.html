Four domains of inquiry in environmental education discourse formed by intersecting ontological and axiological dimensions¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The first conceptual map organizes environmental education-related discourse along two intersecting dimensions: a reality dimension (materialist versus immanence) and a valuation dimension (anthropocentric versus ecocentric ways of seeing human/nature relations). The discourse also reveals two aspects of concern, risk and relationship. Their intersection 'forms four interconnected, interrelated domains of inquiry: ecology, deep ecology, scientific humanism, and what the first author terms theology (a theology of human/nature relations).' Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Texts whose truth and value advocacies can be named and located relative to the two dimensions and two aspects

Constraints¶

  • The authors note texts within a domain may share ways of knowing 'but may not necessarily agree on desired outcomes'
  • The map is a conceptual, not phenomenographic, work

Target Learners¶

  • comparative educators
  • environmental education practitioners and researchers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • orienting educators to the diversity of available perspectives within environmental education discourse

Claims¶

  • Social cartography makes value and power relations in a policy discourse explicit and opens the heuristic circle to readers [+W]
  • Reader interactions with the maps produced divergent interpretations, illustrating inclusion of readers in the hermeneutic circle [+W]
  • A map risks becoming a new metanarrative unless it is continually remapped and readers actively construct their own maps [+W]
