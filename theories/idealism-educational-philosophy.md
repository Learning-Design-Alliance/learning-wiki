# Idealism (Educational Philosophy)

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
For Idealists, ideas are the only true reality; conscious reasoning is the only way to locate what is true, beautiful, and just. Plato founded Idealism and outlined its tenets in *The Republic*. For Plato there are two worlds: a spiritual or mental world where universal ideas and truth are permanent, reachable only through conscious reasoning, and a world of appearances and imperfection experienced through the senses. Plato dramatizes this duality in the Allegory of the Cave — people chained facing a wall mistake shadows cast by a fire for reality; only by breaking free and reaching the world outside does one discover the true source of everything previously known, the realm of pure fact and form. The everyday world is only an imperfect projection of that realm of ideas.

Roughly two centuries later, René Descartes shifted Platonic Idealism toward mind-body dualism with "*Cogito, ergo sum*" (I think, therefore I am) — for Descartes the only proof of his own existence is his thinking, and only rational deduction, not unreliable sense perception, could reach truth. Where Plato posited two separate worlds, Descartes grounded Idealism in a dualism privileging the thinking mind over the physical body and external world — an influence that shaped educational philosophy well into the 20th century.

For Idealists, teaching focuses on moral excellence that benefits society. Students focus on subjects of the mind — literature, history, philosophy — and demonstrate understanding through participation in lectures and Socratic dialogues meant to engage introspection and bring universal forms and concepts to consciousness.

## Implications

### Context
- An idealist classroom is organized around lecture and Socratic dialogue rather than hands-on or applied tasks, since the goal is conscious reasoning toward universal truths rather than skill in manipulating the physical world

### Target Learners
- Learners in curricula weighted toward the humanities (literature, history, philosophy) rather than applied or vocational subjects

### Target Learning Objectives
- Moral excellence and insight into universal, unchanging truths, reached through introspection and reasoned dialogue rather than direct experience
