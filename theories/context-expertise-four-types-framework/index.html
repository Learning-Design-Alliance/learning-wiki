Context expertise framework: four types of proximate knowledge holders in education R&D¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the Tamarack Institute's work on context experts (Attygalle, 2017), the article defines context expertise as "first-hand understanding of what it means to live in a community, know the student and community needs, and experience the impact of inequity." The framework identifies four types of context expertise relevant to education R&D: the expertise of students, the expertise of teachers, the expertise of families, and the expertise of community-based organizations. Inclusive Innovation honors content and context expertise on an equal plane, prioritizing context expertise in researching root causes and developing solutions. Individuals with context expertise are described as more proximate to the factors and conditions creating barriers, with deep, specialized, direct lived experience and skills that outsiders do not have the ability to observe.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Individuals with context expertise must be positioned as collaborators and co-researchers rather than subjects of research
  • Context expertise must be prioritized equally with content expertise in R&D processes

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • students and communities historically excluded from education R&D processes

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • authentic problem definition
  • equitable solution design
  • root cause analysis of educational inequities

  • Over 90 percent of Bristol teachers who participated in student-led mental health PD reported being more conscious of the experiences of students of color in school [+W]
