AMOVA is a post hoc linear stochastic modelling procedure for validating psychometric instruments¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

AMOVA (Accumulative Manifold Validation Analysis) is a specialized statistical methodology for testing the internal and external validity of uniquely designed psychometric instruments. The article describes it as "an arithmetic form of natural mean optimization that is parallel to the discipline of linear stochastic modelling", in which weighted item scores are aggregated into within-cluster and between-group means. It operates post hoc on instrument items grouped into categorical clusters (manifolds), producing a single accumulative efficacy score judged against a Taxonomy of Process Education cutoff.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instrument items must be assignable to categorical clusters (manifolds) determined at research design time
  • Item scores must be weightable on the Taxonomy of Process Education Self-Growth scale

Constraints¶

  • The methodology is presented for researcher-designed psychometric instruments; its applicability beyond the sample data is stated by the author to need further substantiation

Target Learners¶

  • researchers and faculty designing and validating psychometric instruments

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • instrument validity and reliability testing
  • psychometric evaluation of researcher-designed measures

Claims¶

  • AMOVA uses a null-hypothesis rejection cutoff at a TPE score of 2.45, which rounds via the nearest-integer function to 3.00 [+W]
  • In the worked sample analysis of faculty professional development data, AMOVA yielded a final value of 3.00, a 'Proficient' level, leading to rejection of the null hypothesis [+W]
