The Kantian Effect: a metaphor and paradigm linking intertextuality and interdisciplinarity through four dialogic principles¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Kantian Effect is the article's proposed metaphor for the scholarship of integration and a paradigm for reimagining interdisciplinarity. It characterizes "the constructivism and intertextuality evident across the disciplines" that Kant separates architectonically and Bakhtin reconnects dialogically. The article articulates four guiding principles: language and dialogue create unity out of differences; words, texts, genres, and disciplines integrate through dialogic processes, making intertextuality and interdisciplinarity figurative equivalents; dialogism is a continuum on which intertextuality and interdisciplinarity are complementary nodes; and exigence, context, intertext, and hypertext determine the proper application of interdisciplinarity.

Claims¶

  • Interdisciplinary studies are hindered by proliferating definitions and competing instrumental versus conceptual approaches [+W]
  • Kant's faculty-division model of higher education left a legacy of disciplinary fragmentation that persists today [+W]
  • Newell's complex systems theory of interdisciplinarity fails to explain integration, as its critics and Newell himself acknowledge [+W]
  • No special pedagogy or unique strategy set exists for teaching interdisciplinarity; a variety of pedagogies is required [+W]
  • Interdisciplinarity is almost impossible to practice without the integrative power of texts, which are foundational to the disciplines [+W]
  • Effective learning environments depend on the interaction of four overlapping frames rather than their compartmentalization [+W]
  • Hypertextuality extends intertextuality into the digital world, foreshadowing connectivism [+W]
  • Dialogue underpins networked learning: thinking in networks means thinking through collaborative dialogue [+W]
