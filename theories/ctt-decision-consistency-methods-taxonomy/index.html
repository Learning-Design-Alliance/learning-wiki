Three classical test theory decision consistency estimation methods differing in assumptions and complexity¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes single-administration decision consistency estimation around three CTT methods: Livingston and Lewis (LL-DC), which assumes a beta-binomial distribution and uses effective test length; Peng-Subkoviak (PS-DC), a normal approximation extending Huynh's procedure; and Wolkowitz (W-DC), which uses 95% confidence intervals about observed scores. The article states "All three methods require total scores, reliability, and the cut score as input," with LL-DC additionally requiring minimum and maximum possible scores. Complexity of understanding ranges from high (LL-DC) to low (W-DC).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Total scores, reliability, and the cut score for all three methods; LL-DC additionally requires the minimum and maximum possible scores on the exam

Constraints¶

  • LL-DC assumes a beta-binomial distribution; PS-DC assumes bivariate normality and may be less accurate with non-normal data; W-DC makes a practical but non-theoretical assumption that a 95% confidence interval is best

Target Learners¶

  • measurement professionals
  • classroom educators and other non-measurement professionals
  • credentialing program staff

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • estimating and interpreting decision consistency indices for pass/fail exams
  • selecting an appropriate DC estimation method for a given dataset

Claims¶

  • Dc Methods Similar Normal Distributions [+M]
  • Dc Methods Skewed Data W Dc Ll Dc Preferred [+M]
  • Cohen's kappa exhibits a paradox in which high observed agreement yields low kappa, making it unsuitable for pass/fail decision consistency [-W]
  • The greatest differences among the three DC estimates occur when the cut score is set at the mode of a skewed score distribution [+M]
  • DC estimates tend to be lowest when the cut score is set at the peak of the score distribution, where inconsistent decisions are most likely [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Classical Test Theory (CTT) and its stated limitations

Examples¶

  • Target a decision consistency estimate of at least 0.85 for high stakes exams while interpreting lower values in light of cut score location, reliability, and population heterogeneity
  • Select a DC estimation method based on score distribution shape, the user's measurement background, and the intended audience
