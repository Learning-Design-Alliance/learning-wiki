# Informational (error-estimation) account of self-controlled feedback benefits

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The article contrasts two explanations for why self-controlled knowledge of results (KR) aids motor learning. The motivational explanation says self-control "satisfies the basic psychological needs of autonomy and competence"; the information processing perspective says benefits are driven by "performance-dependent KR strategies". The authors conclude that error estimation, available when the KR decision follows the movement, is the more critical mechanism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The article states the learner must be able to base the KR decision on the performance just completed: self-controlled learning benefits "depend on the option of making the KR decision after completing one’s motor response".
#### Constraints
- The study collected no autonomy or motivation measure regarding choice over the KR schedule, so the motivational side was assumed rather than measured.
- The authors state that an immediate retention test would be required to assess offline learning, so the consolidation explanation is offered as initial support only.

### Target Learners
- Young adult volunteers learning a novel motor skill (university sample in this study)

### Target Learning Objectives
- Retention and transfer of a motor skill
- Accurate error detection and correction (estimating one’s own movement outcome)
