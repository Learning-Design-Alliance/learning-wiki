# Negotiation of meaning sequences model (Varonis & Gass, revised by Smith) with trigger and resolution parts

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's analytic framework codes chat interaction into negotiation of meaning sequences consisting of "two parts: trigger and resolution". The trigger <T> is "the utterance or portion of an utterance on the part of the speaker that results in some indication of non-understanding on the part of the listener"; the resolution comprises indicator <I>, response <R>, reaction <RR+>/<RR->, confirmation <C> and reconfirmation <RC>. Five trigger types were found in this study: lexical, content, syntactic, task complexity, and discourse. Sequences of T-I-R or longer were counted; T-I-only sequences were excluded.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A complete transcript of learner interaction, here produced because dyads communicated only via the chat tool
#### Constraints
- Sequences consisting of only a trigger and indicator were not included in the data analysis

### Target Learners
- non-native speaker dyads in communicative second language tasks

### Target Learning Objectives
- quantifying negotiation of meaning as an index of input comprehension work in second language acquisition
