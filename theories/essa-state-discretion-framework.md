# ESSA as a federal policy framework granting states more discretion over standards, assessment, school improvement, and educator effectiveness

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report describes the Every Student Succeeds Act as the current authorization of the federal Elementary and Secondary Education Act. ESSA "continues to call for states to set academic content standards, assess student achievement, identify and support low-performing schools, and improve educator effectiveness," but "departs from prior law, however, in giving states more discretion about how they accomplish these objectives." The report uses this framework to organize its survey examination of Title I and Title II-A implementation in 2017–18.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- States remain responsible for setting academic content standards, assessing achievement, identifying and supporting low-performing schools, and improving educator effectiveness under ESSA.
#### Constraints
- The report covers only the 2017–18 transition period and two large ESEA programs (Title I and Title II-A), as a foundation for ongoing assessment.

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in Title I and Title II-A supported schools and districts

### Target Learning Objectives
- Academic content standards attainment, student achievement, school improvement, and educator effectiveness
