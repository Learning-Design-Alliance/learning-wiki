# Distributed leadership model for school principals

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 5 studies (3 qualitative, 1 causal, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The playbook describes distributed leadership as an approach Chicago Public Schools encourages, in which "principals, assistant principals, teachers, and other school staff share responsibilities to support students' experiences & achievement." The model "leverages the expertise, connections, and contributions of multiple people, rather than relying on the principal alone." The playbook links it to practices such as delegating meaningful leadership roles to teachers, ensuring common planning and collaboration time, and building cultures of collective responsibility and trust.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Principals must delegate meaningful leadership roles such as department chairs, instructional coaches, or team leaders, and ensure common planning and collaboration time during the school day.
#### Constraints
- The playbook presents this as a CPS-encouraged approach within Chicago's governance context, where principals report to both a Network Chief and an LSC.

### Target Learners
- school principals and leadership teams
- teachers in shared leadership roles

### Target Learning Objectives
- building school-wide culture and collective responsibility for student learning
