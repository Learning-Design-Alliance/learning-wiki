# Symbolic interactionism: individuals and society as inseparable, held together by changing shared symbols

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The essay locates Goffman in the symbolic interactionist tradition of Mead, Blumer, James, Cooley, Dewey and Thomas, a tradition favoring field research over laboratory studies and armchair writing. At its center, "individuals and society are inseparable and interdependent," and society exists through shared symbols. Because those symbols are "changing and unstable," society is not fixed and permanent, and the symbolic interactionist studies how individuals maintain shared symbols when they come together.
