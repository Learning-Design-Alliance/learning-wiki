Two-dimensional advocacy framework: four sites of advocacy crossed with tactical versus strategic action¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's conceptual framework combines Athanases and deOliveira's (2008) categorization of advocacy sites — classroom, school, families and community, and larger sociopolitical structures, depicted as nested circles radiating outward — with Peters and Reid's (2009) distinction between tactical and strategic advocacy. The article states that "advocacy-in-action is both tactical and strategic," addressing immediate day-to-day teaching as well as larger social justice issues. The framework was used deductively to code participants' reported actions by site and impact.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires coding advocacy actions by both the site where they occur and whether they address immediate or systemic issues

Constraints¶

  • The article notes advocacy without understanding of larger social justice issues may reinforce deficit perspectives (Haneda & Alexander, 2015)

Target Learners¶

  • novice ESL teachers
  • aspiring ESL teachers in graduate teacher education programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding and enacting advocacy for emergent bilinguals across classroom, school, family/community, and sociopolitical sites

Claims¶

  • Advocacy Actions Mostly Tactical One Strategic [+M]
  • Novice Esl Advocacy School Level Most Common [+M]
  • Novice ESL teachers reported no sociopolitical-dimension advocacy, focusing instead on immediate student needs [+W]
  • School hierarchy and lack of seniority made advocacy challenging for novice ESL teachers, who relied on relationship building [+W]
