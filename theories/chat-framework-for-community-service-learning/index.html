Cultural-historical activity theory (CHAT) as a framework for analyzing community service-learning¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

CHAT is described as a cross-disciplinary framework for studying how humans purposefully transform natural and social reality as a culturally and historically situated, socially mediated process. The paper uses it to analyze CSL in higher education, tracing three generations: Vygotsky's object-oriented action mediated by cultural tools, Leont'ev's collective activity, and Engeström's interacting activity systems. Activity systems direct attention to division of labour, tools, rules, and outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Analysis must attend to activity systems: who carries out activities, the tools at their disposal, the cultural norms and rules governing performance, and desired outcomes

Constraints¶

  • CHAT research varies widely in the extent to which it engages with Marxist ideas, and paradigmatic tensions persist between more adaptive and transformative orientations

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students in community service-learning programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding learning as socially and culturally mediated activity
  • connecting academic coursework to community practice

Claims¶

  • CHAT references are noticeably absent from commonly cited CSL literature despite shared commitments with Dewey and Freire [+W]
  • Contradictions within and across activity systems are vital forces for change and provide a rationale for CSL [+W]
  • CHAT is an interventionist research method aimed at transformation, but tensions persist between adaptive and transformative orientations [+W]
  • Knowledge development as social collaboration: ZPD and collective activity systems support CSL learning networks [+W]
  • CHAT's rejection of the theory/practice divide frames CSL as boundary crossing that develops connective skills [+W]
