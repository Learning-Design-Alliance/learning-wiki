# Support-and-pressure principal leadership framework for productive school improvement

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central framework holds that principals of improving Chicago elementary schools lead by combining support and pressure on the adults who work with children. The authors state that such principals "skillfully use a combination of both support and pressure to enable and vitalize the efforts of adults who work directly with children." The framework organizes four leadership-style characteristics (inclusive/facilitative orientation, institutional focus on student learning, efficient management, and support-and-pressure) and three strategies (quick hits, long-term focus on the instructional core, strategic orientation) that together drive three substantive foci: strengthening parent/community ties, developing teachers' knowledge and skill with school-based professional community, and building trust, all aimed at improved student learning.
