Black box analysis of educational processes as the missing link between means and ends in post-secondary education management systems¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper's central framework is black box analysis: the examination of the internal character and content of educational processes that lie between documented inputs (instructional formats) and outcomes. The author argues the WICHE/NCHEMS structure has formal and substantive elements in place and outcomes under construction, but the outcomes segment is developing in a disengaged manner that ignores internal processes. He states "the general problem for the whole of the WICHE/NCHEMS structure is the absence of documented causal links between means and ends in explicitly viable ways grounded in theory and empirical science," and illustrates the framework through the instruction/learning interface.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research into black boxes so as to eliminate them one by one, requiring sophisticated research into the intersects of the Information Exchange Procedures modules

Constraints¶

  • The author notes he is not prepared with a full general systems theory analysis of the total NCHEMS structure

Target Learners¶

  • post-secondary education students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • documenting causal links between instructional means and educational outcomes for institutional accountability

Claims¶

  • The author calls for early WICHE/NCHEMS research into recognized black boxes including institutional environment, institutional utility structure, and accreditation self-study understandings [+W]
  • Data Element 1107 (Method of Instruction) shows three deficiencies: higher-education bias, non-descriptive morphology, and no stated relation between instructional methods and learning outcomes [+W]
