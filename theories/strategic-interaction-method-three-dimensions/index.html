DiPietro's Strategic-Interaction Method gives equal significance to form and function and analyzes conversation along formal, transactional, and interactional dimensions¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The Strategic-Interaction (S-I) Method, designed by Robert J. DiPietro, is a pedagogical model in which all matters to be taught are cast in the framework of conversations, giving equal significance to both form and function of language. It recognizes three dimensions of conversational discourse open to pedagogical elaboration: the formal dimension (referential meaning open to grammatical analysis), the transactional dimension (language used to motivate actions in one's favor through strategies, protocols, and counterstrategies), and the interactional dimension (how conversations reflect execution of roles). Classroom management divides time into on-stage phases, where students perform and are open to evaluation, and off-stage phases, where they learn and acquire skills.
