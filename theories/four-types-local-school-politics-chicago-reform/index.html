Four types of local school politics in Chicago elementary schools under the 1988 reform¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on a case-study synthesis, the report identifies four general types of local school politics shaped by the interplay of three sites of power: the Local School Council, the principal, and the faculty. These are consolidated principal power, adversarial politics, maintenance politics, and strong democracy. Strong democracy occurs when "there is dissatisfaction with current operations, sustained debate about school change, and shared interests emerge across the three sites of power to promote school improvement." The typology organizes the report's analysis of governance and improvement.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The typology rests on the reform's devolution of authority to three local sites of power: the LSC, the principal, and the faculty

Constraints¶

  • Types were derived from case-study synthesis of Chicago elementary schools during the first four years of reform and apply to schools with IGAP scores below national norms

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary school students in the Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • School-wide organizational conditions that support instructional improvement and student learning
