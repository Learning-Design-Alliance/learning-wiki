Canale and Swain's integrative framework of communicative competence: grammatical, sociolinguistic, and discourse components¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The packet presents Canale and Swain's (1980) framework as an integrative theory in which communicative competence is "a synthesis of knowledge of basic grammatical principles, knowledge of how language is used in social contexts to perform communicative functions, and knowledge of how utterances and communicative functions can be combined according to the principles of discourse". It is depicted in a figure showing sentence grammar, sociolinguistic elements (topic, role of participants, setting, norms of interaction, register), and is used as the organizing framework against which the packet's two teaching models are compared. The framework also spans speaking, reading, and writing rather than a subset of skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction must address grammatical, sociolinguistic, and discourse knowledge together rather than form alone
  • Instruction adopting the framework requires teachers to develop students' grammatical competence as well as sociolinguistic and strategic competencies

Constraints¶

  • The packet notes the framework focuses on speaking, reading, and writing rather than a subset of these skill areas

Target Learners¶

  • bilingual education teachers and teacher trainees
  • bilingual education teachers in training
  • L1 and L2 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • communicative competence in L1 and L2
  • communicative competence across grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic components

Related Theories¶

  • Multidimensional oral proficiency assessment framework grounded in communicative competence theory
  • Krashen's five hypotheses of second language acquisition as principles for bilingual program design
  • DiPietro's Strategic-Interaction Method: equal weight to form and function across three dimensions of conversational discourse
  • Canale and Swain's four dimensions of communicative competence as a lexical exercise framework
  • DiPietro's Strategic-Interaction Method gives equal significance to form and function and analyzes conversation along formal, transactional, and interactional dimensions

Examples¶

  • Adopting a communicative approach requires teachers to take a dual role facilitating both natural acquisition and learning
  • Teacher training packets operationalize communicative competence theory through workshop activities, pre/posttests, and guided discussion rather than expert knowledge alone
