Vygotsky's distinction between lower and higher mental functions¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article enumerates Vygotsky's first central concept as a distinction between two kinds of cognitive processes. Lower mental functions are biologically built-in ways of learning and responding that many species exhibit, with discovering what foods to eat given as an example. Higher mental functions are described as "dilate, focused cognitive process that enhance learning, memory, and logical reasoning" and are exhibited only by human beings, with society and culture described as critical to their development.
