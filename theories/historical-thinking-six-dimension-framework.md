# Six-dimension framework of historical thinking skills for high school world history

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report organizes historical thinking into six aligned dimensions: historical argumentation (making, supporting, or assessing a historical claim in relation to evidence), historical causation, historical comparison, historical contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing. The same six categories are applied in parallel to activities (A1–A6) and student work (S1–S6), and the categories are aligned with widely accepted national frameworks and standards. Each dimension is scored on a four-level scale from absence to rigorous presence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dimensions are relevant to high school world history teaching and learning, established through alignment with national frameworks and careful review by teachers and researchers.
#### Constraints
- It is rare for a single assignment to encompass all dimensions, so it is neither expected nor realistic for every activity to score high on all dimensions.

### Target Learners
- high school world history students

### Target Learning Objectives
- historical thinking skills
