# Unidimensional Rasch model scaling of MAP scores on the RIT scale

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 2 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
All MAP tests assume a single latent achievement factor and are scaled on the unidimensional Rasch model with the RIT scale, linearly transformed from logits (RIT = logit x 10 + 200). The article's stated rationale is that "Rasch model has unique properties over other models", including sufficiency, separability, specific objectivity, and latent additivity, and that the model preserves the interpretability of the construct map and item order across the person-location range.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The measurement model must preserve the order of items throughout the range of person locations consistent with the construct map's interpretational requirements
#### Constraints
- The unidimensionality premise treats complex traits like reading and math as coherent wholes; goal-area structure exists for domain sampling rather than to measure different traits

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in grades 3 to 9

### Target Learning Objectives
- Interpretable achievement scores for growth measurement in Reading and Mathematics
