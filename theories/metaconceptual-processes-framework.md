# Metaconceptual processes framework: metacognitive knowledge and processes specific to concept learning, classified as knowledge, awareness, monitoring, and evaluation

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts the metaconceptual framework to focus metacognition on concept learning during reading: "the term metaconceptual has been used to focus on metacognitive processes that are specific to concept learning". Metaconceptual knowledge and processes activated during conceptual change "have been classified as metaconceptual knowledge, awareness, monitoring, and evaluation". The study uses this framework to design strategy instruction aimed at activating metaconceptual processes, and its data analysis coded strategies and products by whether their purpose was cognitive or metacognitive. The findings section maps six metacognitive processes performed while reading, including drawing attention, monitoring ideas, evaluating, and facilitation of memorization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strategy and product coding requires examining participants' stated purposes for using each strategy or product to decide whether it is cognitive or metacognitive
#### Constraints
- The framework addresses metacognitive processes specific to concept learning, not the full range of metacognition

### Target Learners
- preservice science teachers

### Target Learning Objectives
- conceptual understanding of science concepts
- conceptual change and elimination of misconceptions
