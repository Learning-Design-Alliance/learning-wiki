# AI Literacy Framework of three interconnected Modes of Engagement: Understand, Evaluate, and Use

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report defines AI literacy as the knowledge and skills that enable people to critically understand, evaluate, and use AI systems and tools. Its framework organizes this into three Modes of Engagement—Understand, Evaluate, and Use—that the authors state 'happen concurrently and together support robust engagement in AI literacy.' The framework is underpinned by Core Values (Human Judgment and Centering Justice), six AI Literacy Practices, and three Types of Use, giving educational leaders a structure to adapt AI literacy programs to their specific audiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leaders must adapt the framework's components to their unique contexts and audiences, such as learners, teachers, or caregivers
#### Constraints
- The framework is a definitional/design contribution informed by prior frameworks and practitioner input, not an empirically tested model in this report

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners
- educators
- school leaders
- community members

### Target Learning Objectives
- critically understand AI systems
- evaluate AI tools for bias and accuracy
- use AI safely and effectively
