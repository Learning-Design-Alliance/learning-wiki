# Skill acquisition theory account of written CF effects: declarative knowledge proceduralized into implicit knowledge through repeated practice

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article explains its findings within skill acquisition theory (citing DeKeyser and Ellis), which distinguishes explicit declarative knowledge of facts and rules from implicit procedural knowledge. It states that "explicit declarative knowledge is proceduralized and automatized into implicit knowledge by practicing it, namely activating it repeatedly for meaningful purposes or uses". The study uses this framework to interpret why CF groups that gained explicit knowledge also gained implicit knowledge after a post-CF writing task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practice opportunities that repeatedly activate declarative knowledge for meaningful purposes are needed for proceduralization.
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is disagreement in the literature on how implicit knowledge can be obtained, and future research should consider other perspectives.

### Target Learners
- Low-intermediate university EFL learners

### Target Learning Objectives
- Development of explicit and implicit L2 grammatical knowledge
