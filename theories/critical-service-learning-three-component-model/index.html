Critical service-learning model with three components: social change orientation, power redistribution, and authentic relationships¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Critical service-learning is a model that adopts a social justice framework, as opposed to a more "apolitical helper" model of service-learning, and demands an analysis of power structures and social change (Mitchell, 2008). The article identifies three components: "(a) providing a social change orientation; (b) working to redistribute power; and (c) developing authentic relationships." Authentic relationships challenge the self-other binary, emphasizing interdependence and reciprocity. This study operationalizes the authentic relationships component by examining professor, community partner, and peer mentor relationships.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Adopts a social justice framework rather than an apolitical helper model
  • Demands analysis of power structures and social change
  • Authentic relationships sustained by good communication and on-going dialogue between community partner and faculty or campus

Constraints¶

  • Service-learning has not fully realized its original civic purpose without moving toward justice-oriented pedagogy
  • The article notes Mitchell does not clearly outline the extent of the community partner's role

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate students in service-learning courses
  • higher education students
  • faculty
  • community partners
