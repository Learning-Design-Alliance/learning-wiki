# Three-approach taxonomy of school-family engagement: traditional parent involvement, school-parent partnership, and family- and community-centered engagement

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The brief categorizes school-family engagement research into three approaches distinguished by the school's relationship with families, intentions and goals, decision-making structure, and communication with parents. In traditional parent involvement, communication "is limited and one-way"; in school-parent partnership, communication is two-way on selected topics with schools consulting parents; in family- and community-centered engagement, schools share decision-making responsibility with parents and goals are decided collectively. The brief offers this taxonomy with reflection questions to help educators situate their current practice and develop a vision for family engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must identify their intentions, goals, and structures for engagement, since communication is most impactful when grounded in clearly identified intentions and goals
#### Constraints
- Drawn from prior research rather than tested in this study; the brief notes each approach carries strengths and outcomes depend on the context and needs of the school community, and many schools likely employ a combination of the three

### Target Learners
- K-12 students and their families and caregivers

### Target Learning Objectives
- Strengthening school-family communication and collaboration to support student learning and well-being
