# Five cornerstones of an effective classroom technology coaching program

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (10 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report proposes that five aspects of the DLP account for its success: partnership, active learning, personalization, sustained support, and content flexibility. Partnership means a voluntary, non-evaluative, school-based collaboration among administrators, coaches, and teachers in which "teachers and coaches co-constructed lessons." Active learning structures coaching around learning by doing on each teacher's own classroom challenge; personalization fits support to teacher, school, and district context; sustained support spans weeks and months; and content flexibility lets coaches support subjects outside their own expertise by treating the teacher as the content expert.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Voluntary, non-evaluative coaching with confidentiality so teachers can experiment without fear of failure
- Sustained support over weeks and months rather than one-off professional development events
#### Constraints
- The report states coaching is most effective when aligned with district and school goals organically and without superseding teachers' ability to select their own challenges

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers in participating schools
- technology coaches
- school principals

### Target Learning Objectives
- impactful use of technology to support teaching and student 21st-century skills
