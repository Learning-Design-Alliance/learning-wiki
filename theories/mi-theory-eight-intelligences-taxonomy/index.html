Gardner's Multiple Intelligences theory: eight named intelligences with a possible ninth¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (1 mixed, 6 against) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review briefly outlines Gardner's MI theory before presenting critics. Gardner defines intelligence as "a biopsychological potential to process information that can be activated in a cultural setting to solve problems or create products that are of value in a culture." The theory posits at least seven basic intelligences, later an eighth and a possible ninth: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, body-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and naturalist, with spiritual or existential intelligence mentioned but not officially included.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The review states Gardner's theory rests on a modular picture of the brain in which mental activities are parceled out into various regions more autonomous from one another than previously thought

Constraints¶

  • The review notes Gardner has not officially included the possible ninth spiritual or existential intelligence in his theory

Target Learners¶

  • all students in K-12 classrooms

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • recognizing and developing diverse human intellectual potentials across linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, body-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and naturalist domains

Claims¶

  • A large psychometric data review supports a hierarchical model of intelligence headed by g, which MI theory does not fit [-M]
  • Gardner never laid down a detailed plan for applying MI theory in schools, and classroom implementations operate independently of him [~M]
  • Children scoring highly on one intelligence tend to score highly on others, consistent with general intelligence [-M]
  • A century of psychometric data consistently shows performances on intellectual tests are correlated, which MI must account for [-M]
  • Learning Styles Matching Does Not Improve Learning [-M]
  • MI theory lacks empirical theory-testing research supporting its intelligences as autonomous faculties [-M]
  • The Theory of Multiple Intelligences is weak in stressing science and social sciences as fields of talent, and existing subject categories could nurture pupils' talents [-W]
