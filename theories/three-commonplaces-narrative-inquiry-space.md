# Three commonplaces—temporality, sociality, and place—specify the dimensions of a narrative inquiry space

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents the three-dimensional narrative inquiry space as the organizing framework for making meaning of experience. Situated in narrative accounts are three commonplaces—temporality, sociality, and place—which, quoting Connelly and Clandinin (2006), "specify dimensions of an inquiry space" (p. 479). Temporality treats life as ongoing and people, places, and events as always in transition; sociality requires describing both personal conditions (feelings, hopes, desires, aesthetic reactions, moral dispositions) and social conditions (existential conditions, environment, surrounding factors and forces); place requires attending to the specificity of the location where events take place. Understanding a narrative inquiry requires simultaneous exploration of all three commonplaces.
