# Turnaround schools as a distinct category of school improvement

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The guide defines turnaround schools by two criteria: they "began as chronically poor performers" with generally 20 percent or more of students failing state proficiency standards over two or more consecutive years, and they "showed substantial gains in student achievement in a short time (no more than three years)". Substantial gains are exemplified by reducing the proportion failing proficiency by at least 10 percentage points or improving standardized test performance by about 0.25 standard deviations. Turnaround differs from general school improvement in speed and in the likelihood of staff replacement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must meet both the chronic low-performance criterion and the rapid substantial-gain criterion to count as turnaround schools
#### Constraints
- The guide excludes comprehensive school reform models and beating-the-odds schools that were never low performing

### Target Learners
