# RIT vertical equal-interval scale grounded in item response theory

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The RIT scale is the report's central measurement framework: an equal-interval vertical scale spanning grades K–12, derived from item response theory and the Rasch model. The report explains that "The RIT scale has two key characteristics. It is a vertical scale with equal-interval units," and that "the equal-interval characteristic of the RIT scale allows the measurement of growth over two or more time points (i.e., learning) by calculating the difference in scores." IRT places item difficulty and person ability on the same continuous scale, enabling adaptive testing and score comparability across item sets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Items must be calibrated to the RIT scale, and scoring uses maximum likelihood estimation with fencing for extreme response patterns
#### Constraints
- The scale spans grades K–12 in Math and Reading but grades 2–12 in Language Usage and Science

### Target Learners
- K–12 students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Comparing achievement across grades and terms and measuring growth as score change
