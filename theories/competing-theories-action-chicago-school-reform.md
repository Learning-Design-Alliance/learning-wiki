# Competing theories of action for school change in Chicago: business-oriented, teaching-and-learning, community-dialogue/equity, and parental-control views

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report organizes Chicago school-reform actors' views of school change into a typology of competing theories of action. The Challenge's own theory emphasized school-site autonomy, restructuring of time, size, and isolation, and networked school collaboration. By contrast, "The city's political and district leaders typically expressed a view of school change in business-oriented terms," higher-education leaders focused on the primacy of teaching and learning, and community actors split between broad equity dialogue and parental/community control of schools. The report uses this typology to assess the Challenge's legitimacy and potential influence.
