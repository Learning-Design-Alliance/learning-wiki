# Whole-child learner variability theory of change

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Learner Variability Project's theory of change holds that synthesizing learning sciences research into factors critical to learner outcomes, connected across the whole child, generates a mental model for educators. As the article states, "Our theory of change proposes that by synthesizing the learning sciences research into factors critical to learner outcomes (see Figure 1) and showing how they are connected across the whole child, we can help generate a mental model for educators." Figure 4 depicts three steps: providing accessible tools and professional learning, shifting educator mindsets to recognize and leverage each student's strengths, and creating safe, supportive, personalized learning environments that enable powerful learning experiences. Mindset shifts include expanding what variability encompasses, correcting misconceptions such as learning styles, connecting factors, and trying new strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need access to synthesized, research-based factors and strategies, plus time and professional support, to build the mental model
#### Constraints
- The article notes every strategy may not work as expected across students and contexts

### Target Learners
- Pre-K through grade 12 students in general education, special education, English learner, and low-income populations

### Target Learning Objectives
- Educator understanding of learner variability and application of evidence-based strategies across cognitive, social-emotional, content, and background factors
