# Transparency theory: a holistic, tacit-skill account of learning and language acquisition

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Transparency theory, articulated by Brown, Warner, and Williams (1985), is a view of learning and skill acquisition growing out of the psychological tradition emphasizing the functional and holistic aspects of human perception and action. It "emphasizes the tacit skill aspects of language acquisition and the functioning of language as a tool of social interaction" alongside traditional explicit instruction in grammar and vocabulary. Drawing on Gibson's ecological optics, Polanyi's account of tacit versus explicit and focal versus subsidiary knowledge, contemporary psychophysics, and second language acquisition research, it treats language as a skill best acquired as a tool in the service of some other purpose, with learners focusing through words as instruments of attention to their meaning. The authors propose it as a theoretical foundation for holistic, skill-based approaches to language acquisition and for interactive video design.
