Seven Stages of Concern About an Innovation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Stages of Concern (SoC) concept, generalized from Fuller's work on teacher concerns and part of the Concerns-Based Adoption Model, describes seven hypothesized stages through which individuals' concerns about an innovation move: awareness, informational, personal, management, consequence, collaboration, and refocusing. The article defines each stage, e.g., management means "Attention is 'focused on the processes and tasks of using the innovation and the best use of information and resources." The stages range through Fuller's Self, Task, and Impact orientations and are measured by the SoC Questionnaire.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The concept assumes individuals involved in change exhibit concerns that can be located on the seven stages at a given point in time, with one or two stages generally predominating.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school teachers implementing a new curriculum

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding and supporting teachers' concerns during curriculum implementation

Claims¶

  • Individual teachers' concern profiles vary widely and can diverge from group trends during implementation [+W]
  • Management concerns never predominated for any group in the SCIS implementation, contrary to other innovation studies [+W]
  • Anticipating a workshop versus having prior workshop experience differentiates teachers' concern profiles [+W]
  • Teachers' concerns follow a developmental trend from lower to higher stages during curriculum implementation [+W]
  • The two-week SCIS workshop shifted participants' concerns from lower-stage nonuser patterns toward higher-stage user patterns [+W]

Examples¶

  • Stages of Concern Questionnaire (SoCQ)
  • Stages of Concern Questionnaire (SoCQ): a 35-item diagnostic instrument
  • Use individual and group Stages of Concern data to tailor inservice and training decisions
  • Use three methods to assess concerns: questionnaire, open-ended statements, and informal conversation
