Relational trust as an organizational property grounded in discernment of intentions within mutual obligations¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper develops relational trust as a collective property of a social institution, building on Coleman's trustworthiness in social networks but rejecting a purely rational-choice account. Trust takes three forms — organic, contractual, and relational — of which relational trust, founded on beliefs and explicit expectations regarding obligations, is most relevant to schools. Its defining feature is discernment: "relational trust entaiIs a dynamic interplay of actual behavior and a discernment of intentions in the context of the obligations that are shared by various parties." Fulfilling obligations requires doing the right thing for the right reasons.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sustained social interactions among parties through which expectations can be validated and intentions discerned

Constraints¶

  • Relational trust can only be informally and infrequently monitored, and abrogations are not easily subject to legal redress; individuals typically withdraw trust when expectations are violated

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary school communities in urban districts

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • School improvement and organizational conditions supporting student learning

  • Relational Trust Hierarchical Respect Trust Caring [+M]
  • Relational trust varies substantially between schools, supporting its treatment as an organizational property [+W]
  • Facilitative, inclusive principal leadership and effective principal supervision are associated with more positive trust relations [+W]
