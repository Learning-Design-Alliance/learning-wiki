A four-type hierarchy of evidence for judging educational technology effectiveness, ordered from weakest to strongest¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The guide presents an organizing framework of four evidence types that educators encounter when evaluating product effectiveness claims. The types are explicitly ranked: "The types of evidence in this guide are ordered from weakest to strongest, and each evidence description is accompanied by examples of information sources containing that type of evidence." The hierarchy serves as the guide's structure for helping educators judge how strongly each evidence type can back an effectiveness claim.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators need to identify which of the four evidence types a given information source contains before judging the strength of its support

Constraints¶

  • The guide addresses evidence about technology effectiveness specifically, in the context of making technology purchasing decisions

Target Learners¶

  • educators making technology adoption decisions

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • evaluating the strength of evidence about effectiveness of educational options

Claims¶

  • The quality of evidence about technology effectiveness varies widely across information sources [+W]
  • Different evidence types differ in how strongly they can support claims about effectiveness [+W]
