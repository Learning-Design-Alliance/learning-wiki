# Interpersonal theory: Agency and Communion dimensions and the principle of complementarity

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study, `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article uses interpersonal theory (Kiesler, 1996) to map interpersonal behavior and styles on two orthogonal dimensions: Agency, manifesting in strivings for power or control, and Communion, manifesting in strivings for friendliness and affiliation. Its complementarity principle states that "dominance induces submission, and vice versa" on Agency, while on Communion "hostility begets hostility and friendliness begets friendliness". Interactions largely following this principle are claimed to relate to relationship quality, though the article notes interactions often do not follow it, especially in hierarchical relationships where professionals may deliberately refrain from complementarity.
