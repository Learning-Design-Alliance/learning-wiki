Authentic intellectual work framework: construction of knowledge, elaborated written communication, and connection to students' lives¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authentic intellectual work framework, originally developed by the National Research Center on School Organization and Restructuring and adopted by the Chicago Annenberg Research Project, defines ambitious instruction through demands that students apply knowledge to real-world problems, organize and synthesize information, ground solutions in disciplinary concepts, and communicate conclusions effectively. In this study, teacher raters "rated each assignment against the three standards of intellectual challenge: construction of knowledge, written communication, and connection to students’ lives." Rubrics for these standards were applied by teams of Chicago teachers, with scores combined into an overall measure of intellectual quality.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Scoring requires trained raters applying rubrics for each of the three standards, with rescoring of a random subsample to adjust for rater severity

Constraints¶

  • The report notes this is not the only conceptual framework with associated scoring rubrics that could be applied to assess the intellectual challenge of classroom assignments

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students in grades three, six, and eight

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • construction of knowledge through interpreting, analyzing, and evaluating information
  • elaborated written communication
  • connecting academic work to problems beyond school

Claims¶

  • Challenging Assignments Score Higher Than Typical [+M]
  • The intellectual quality of classroom assignments in Annenberg Challenge schools improved between 1997 and 1999, though some 1999 averages were lower than 1998 [+W]
  • A 1998 baseline study found typical Chicago assignments were mostly rudimentary basic-skills exercises, but most students completed the more demanding tasks they were given [+W]
  • More than 80 percent of sixth and eighth grade mathematics assignments in 1999 provided only minimal or no intellectual challenge [+W]
  • Writing assignments in 1999 showed more intellectual challenge than mathematics, with 48 to 64 percent reaching moderate or extensive challenge depending on grade [+W]
  • Students whose assignments are more authentic produce more authentic intellectual work in both writing and mathematics [+W]
  • Students showed more authentic intellectual work in writing than in mathematics [+W]
