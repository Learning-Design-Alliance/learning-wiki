# Behaviorism frames reading fluency instruction as sub-skill training with response and feedback

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review presents behaviorism as a theory in which information is passed from a more knowledgeable teacher to a less knowledgeable learner, and reading tasks are separated into smaller components. It names "three main Behaviorism Theories: 1) Classical Conditioning, 2) Connectionism and 3) Operant Conditioning", with reading components grouped into five categories such as visual and auditory discrimination, each containing sub-skills that require a response and feedback. The article also records the main criticism: learners are treated as passive information receivers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each sub-skill requires a response and feedback; teachers should place feasible goals to assess students using behaviorism
#### Constraints
- The theory is severely criticized for treating learners as passive receivers and for its account of how information is processed in the mind

### Target Learners
- Students developing reading skills, including EFL learners

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading sub-skills: phonics, sight words, blending, word recognition
