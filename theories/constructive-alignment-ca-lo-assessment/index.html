Constructive alignment as the theoretical foundation linking LO definitions, assessment strategies, and continuous improvement¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article adopts Biggs's (1996) concept of constructive alignment, which "emphasizes the systematic integration of intended LOs, teaching and learning activities, and assessment tasks." It provides the theoretical foundation for why consistent LO definitions and appropriate assessment strategies are essential: they ensure that what is taught, how it is taught, and how it is assessed are mutually reinforcing. In the study, instructors' engagement with CA visualizations prompted reflection on whether assessments captured intended outcomes, leading to course design adjustments consistent with this alignment principle.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A robust, shared conceptualization of what constitutes a learning outcome
  • Reliable measurement methods and aligned assessment strategies

Constraints¶

  • The field of LA lacks a clear, consistent definition of learning outcomes, with many studies using the term without solid theoretical grounding

Target Learners¶

  • higher education faculty and program administrators

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • alignment of teaching, learning activities, and assessment with intended learning outcomes
