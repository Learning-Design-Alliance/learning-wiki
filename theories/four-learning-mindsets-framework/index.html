Four learning mindsets framework: belonging, success expectancy, growth mindset, and task value¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The synthesis names four learning mindsets that are particularly important for students' academic behaviors, persistence, and performance, expressed from the student's point of view: "I belong in this learning community," "I can succeed at this," "My ability and competence grow with my effort (also known as a 'growth mindset')," and "This work has value for me." How true these statements feel within a classroom determines how likely students are to focus attention on learning and persevere with challenging tasks; feeling any of them untrue makes disengagement more likely.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must create classroom conditions and experiences, not persuasion alone, for the mindsets to feel true to students.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students, with attention to adolescents developing social identities

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Motivation, persistence on challenging academic tasks, and positive academic behaviors

  • Mindsets Vary Across Classrooms With Teacher Practices [+M]
  • Positive academic mindsets increase perseverance and academic behaviors, yielding higher grades; negative mindsets produce a self-defeating cycle [+W]
