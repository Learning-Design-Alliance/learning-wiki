# Deferred acceptance assignment with strategy-proof ranking

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
GoCPS assigns students via a deferred acceptance process: each student is temporarily placed in their top-ranked program, programs order eligible applicants by priority group and then lottery number or application score, seats are filled down the ordering, and unassigned students defer to their next-ranked program iteratively. The paper attributes two desirable properties to this process: it is "a stable assignment because no student loses a seat at a (preferred) program to a student who is ordered below them," and "the best option for students is to rank programs in their true preference ordering," removing any incentive for strategic ranking behavior. The same mechanism underlies medical resident hospital matching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must have defined seat counts, priority groups, and selection rules (lottery or points) that the computerized process can apply
- Applicants submit ranked preference lists that the iterative process can process
#### Constraints
- The strategy-proofness property relies on students being allowed to order all available programs; GoCPS caps rankings at 20, which the authors argue is not problematic because most students rank fewer

### Target Learners
- Incoming ninth-grade students participating in school choice

### Target Learning Objectives
- Fair and stable assignment of students to requested programs
- Reducing strategic complexity in families' application decisions
