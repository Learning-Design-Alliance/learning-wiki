# State Digital Equity and Opportunity Framework: five domains across education, workforce, and housing sectors

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (4 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The State Digital Equity and Opportunity Framework establishes the central role states must play across education, housing, and workforce sectors to establish digital equity. It imports three domains from the K-12 framework (Leadership for Digital Transformation, Consistent Access to Devices and Connectivity, and Digital Competency) and adds two new domains: Digital Ecosystem and Technology for Accessibility and Inclusion. For each domain and sector it provides suggested objectives, requirements for contractors and agencies, metrics for measuring success, and funding sources, targeting governors' offices, state broadband development offices, education agencies, and workforce development boards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- States must adopt policies and take action including inclusive planning and assessment mechanisms, standards for technology integration and digital competencies, and budgets for connectivity and devices.
#### Constraints
- The objectives are not intended as one-size-fits-all solutions but guideposts to hyper-focus states on rural communities, historically and systemically excluded communities, those experiencing poverty, and people who require accessible technologies.

### Target Learners
- Rural, unserved, and underserved communities
- Residents of public housing
- Workers needing digital upskilling

### Target Learning Objectives
- Digital equity
- Economic mobility
- Full participation in the digital age
