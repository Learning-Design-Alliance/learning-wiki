# Dispositional mastery framework: Ability, Inclination, and Sensitivity

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article reframes mastery of cross-cutting Portrait Skill Sets and Practices as dispositional, drawing on Project Zero's account that "Dispositions include three elements: Ability, Inclination, and Sensitivity." Ability concerns whether a learner can apply a skill; Inclination whether they are comfortable and motivated to engage it; Sensitivity whether they can recognize when it applies in unique contexts. The article argues that ability proven in a single context is insufficient, because a learner who does not choose to use a Practice or fails to see its relevance gains essentially nothing, and calls for learners to be coached in Practices "repeatedly in diverse contexts and varied use cases."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning experiences designed for learner variability, cultural context, and personal interests across repeated, varied contexts
#### Constraints
- The authors state most existing assessment models consider only ability and would mark a skill mastered once ability is proven in a single context

### Target Learners
- K-12 students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Cross-cutting Portrait Skill Sets such as evaluating information, communicating, and self-awareness
