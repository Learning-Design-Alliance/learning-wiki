## Description
Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) explains learning as a cyclical process in which learners set goals, choose strategies, monitor progress, and reflect on outcomes in order to improve later performance. Rather than treating learning as passive reception, SRL emphasizes that effective learners actively manage attention, effort, strategy use, and feedback across time.

Barry Zimmerman's work is one of the most influential formulations of SRL. In that model, learners move through forethought, performance, and self-reflection phases. Goal setting, strategy selection, self-monitoring, and self-evaluation are therefore not add-ons to learning; they are central mechanisms that shape whether learning efforts become effective.

A second, complementary formulation comes from clinical and addiction-behavior research: building on Kanfer's foundational work (Kanfer, 1970a, 1970b), Miller and Brown (1991; Brown, 1998) proposed a **seven-step model** in which self-regulation can falter at any of seven points: (1) receiving relevant information, (2) evaluating it against a norm, (3) triggering the decision to change, (4) searching for options, (5) formulating a plan, (6) implementing the plan, and (7) assessing the plan's effectiveness — which recycles back to steps 1-2. Unlike Zimmerman's three-phase cycle, this model is explicitly diagnostic: a scored instrument, the [Self-Regulation Questionnaire (SRQ)](../elements/self-regulation-questionnaire.md) (Brown, Miller, & Lawendowski, 1999), lets a practitioner identify which specific step in the cycle is failing for a given person rather than treating self-regulation as a single undifferentiated capacity.

## Implications

### Context
- Strong fit where learners must plan, monitor, and revise over extended tasks
- Especially relevant in independent study, writing, problem solving, exam preparation, and online learning
- Often requires instructional supports such as explicit goals, feedback, prompts, and reflection routines before learners can regulate effectively on their own

### Target Learners
- Learners developing independence and metacognitive control
- Adults and adolescents managing long-term goals, deadlines, and self-study demands
- Novices who need support learning how to learn, not just what to learn

### Target Learning Objectives
- Goal setting and strategic planning
- Monitoring understanding and performance during work
- Adjusting strategy in response to feedback or error
- Building persistence and more accurate self-evaluation

## Related Theories

- [Self-Determination Theory](self-determination-theory.md) — motivation and autonomy shape whether learners engage in self-regulatory behaviors
- [Cognitive Load Theory](cognitive-load-theory.md) — working-memory demands can constrain learners' ability to monitor and regulate effectively, especially when they are novices
- [Executive Function Development](executive-function-development.md) — self-regulation depends substantially on executive-function capacities (inhibitory control, working memory) that develop across childhood and adolescence
