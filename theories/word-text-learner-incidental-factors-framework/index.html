Three-factor framework of word, text, and learner factors affecting incidental L2 vocabulary acquisition¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 10 claims (9 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 3 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review organizes the factors enhancing incidental L2 vocabulary acquisition into three aspects: the word itself, the reading text, and the learners. Word factors cover "the predictability of word meanings, the certain word types such as high-frequency words, the certain word class such as no uns, a certain number of exposures of the word and the glossing of the word". Text factors are context quality and richness, topic familiarity and rhetorical organization; learner factors are proficiency, motivation, involvement and L1 lexicalization, with synonym generation disadvantageous.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Applies to incidental vocabulary acquisition through reading in L2 English contexts as reviewed

Constraints¶

  • Synonym generation is identified as disadvantageous to vocabulary acquisition

Target Learners¶

  • L2 English learners across proficiency levels

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Incidental acquisition of L2 vocabulary meaning, form, and form-meaning connections through reading

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Combine implicit and explicit word instruction, ensure repeated exposures, and use interesting topics and delayed-feedback review to support vocabulary learning
  • Develop learners' personal sets of effective vocabulary strategies rather than teaching arbitrary strategy lists
