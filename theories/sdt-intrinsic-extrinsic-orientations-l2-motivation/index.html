Self-determination theory framework of intrinsic and extrinsic motivational orientations for L2 learning¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 9 studies (3 review, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 theoretical, 1 causal, 1 associational), q1–q4 · 1 of 9 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study adopts Noels and associates' intrinsic/extrinsic motivation framework derived from self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan) as its "informative framework". It treats motivational constructs as orientations: intrinsic orientations relate to inherent interest, with three types (Intrinsic-Knowledge, Intrinsic-Accomplishment, Intrinsic-Stimulation), and extrinsic motivation is categorized into External, Introjected and Identified Regulation, the last being the most self-determined form.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Measurement of learners' motivational orientations requires an instrument such as the Language Learning Orientation Scale, translated and back-translated to prevent semantic loss.

Constraints¶

  • In EFL settings learners have only the classroom environment to use the target language, which the article notes caused problems for integrative motivation constructs.

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners in foreign-language (classroom-only) settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Understanding and fostering motivated engagement in L2 learning

Claims¶

  • Turkish Efl Learners Generally Extrinsically Motivated [+M]
  • Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation, engagement, and persistence in learning. [+M]
  • Rewarding an already-intrinsically-motivating activity can reduce future engagement with it [+M]
  • Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [+S]
  • The article argues extrinsic rewards such as grades can reduce intrinsic motivation and should be phased out [+W]
