Five domains of an outcomes-based contract for edtech¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 11 studies (11 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 11 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The COBC's "Standards of Excellence for Outcomes Based Contracting™: Edtech Instructional Interventions" organizes OBC design around five domains: clearly defined population and program, clearly defined outcomes and metrics, contingent outcomes payment, mutual accountability, and continuous improvement. Table 2 contrasts each domain with traditional contracting, e.g., "At least 40% of the contract value is predicated on agreed-upon outcomes" versus payment based solely on services or licenses. The domains help build a rate card aligning contract elements to outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Contracts must define target populations, total budgets, OBC price per student, and non-OBC price per student

Constraints¶

  • If the district does not meet a student's dosage requirements, the district remains obligated to pay the full amount after agreed-upon remedies have been attempted

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in participating districts, including emergent bilingual learners and students performing below average

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improving student outcomes in targeted content areas such as early literacy and English language arts through edtech interventions

Claims¶

  • Obc Contract Development Collaborative Data Driven Decisions [+M]
  • Obc Clear Roles Shared Responsibility [+M]
  • Lack of pricing transparency in the edtech market made OBC pricing decisions challenging, with pricing varying up to 40% between districts. [+W]
  • OBCs drove a systemic shift for district and school teams to monitor and leverage data for continuous improvement. [+W]
  • OBCs drove increased edtech implementation rates, boosted by provider implementation support, despite challenges including buy-in, staff workload, unclear terms, leadership turnover, and time constraints. [+W]
  • The OBC model enabled districts to better establish instructional coherence, support increased student engagement, and assess associated outcomes. [+W]
  • Providers gained super users of their products and learnings for product design and effective implementation support through OBC engagement. [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Outcome-based contracting (OBC) edtech rate cards tying payment to measured learner outcomes
  • Structure OBC procurement around intentionality questions on learner goals, populations, outcomes, and stakeholders
  • Support future edtech OBC cohorts with readiness assessments, early planning, needs-assessment framing, and cross-cohort collaboration
