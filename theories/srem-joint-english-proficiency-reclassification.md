# Shared random effects model jointly estimating English proficiency development and time to reclassification

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops a shared random effects model (SREM) to estimate English proficiency development and time to reclassification simultaneously. It works by treating student-specific random effects as latent covariates in the time to reclassification model, so that growth in academic English proficiency informs predictions of when an English learner will reclassify to fluent English proficient status. The model was applied to data from a large Arizona school district.
