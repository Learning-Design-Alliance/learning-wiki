# OECD AI System Classification Framework as an analytical lens for comparing AI regulations

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The OECD framework is a multifaceted classification approach for characterizing AI systems across technical, ethical, and societal dimensions. It encompasses five dimensions: "People and planet," "Economic context," "Data and input," "AI model," and "Task and output." The article uses it as a foundational tool to classify and compare 11 AI regulatory documents, providing a structured basis for assessing each regulation's core focus and priorities and for considering implications for learning analytics. It also proposes lifecycle elements from planning and design through operating and monitoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regulatory documents must be available in English or officially translated versions for classification using the framework's language
#### Constraints
- Some dimensions apply more in early development phases (in the lab) than once AI systems are deployed (in the field)
- The framework was not designed as a governance tool itself

### Target Learners
- Learning analytics researchers
- AI policy researchers
- Educational institution policymakers

### Target Learning Objectives
- Understanding how AI regulatory frameworks can be systematically compared and classified to inform responsible AI-driven learning analytics design
