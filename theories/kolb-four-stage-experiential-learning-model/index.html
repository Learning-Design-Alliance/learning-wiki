Kolb's four-stage experiential learning model and its four learning style categories¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review describes Kolb's experiential learning model, in which "learning theoretically is undertaken on the basis of a four-stage cycle": Concrete Experience, Reflective Observation, Abstract Conceptualization and Active Experimentation. Crossing two dimensions (concrete experience versus abstract conceptualization; active experimentation versus reflective observation) yields four learning style categories — Convergence, Divergence, Assimilation and Accommodation — each with two dominant abilities. Kolb's Learning Styles Inventory contained nine items of four words each, ranked by the subject.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners are assumed to need four different abilities corresponding to the four stages of the cycle

Constraints¶

  • The review reports Kolb questioned whether learners develop all four abilities, finding most continually choose a set of learning abilities for specific situations

Target Learners¶

  • college and professional students assessed in Kolb-based research

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • characterizing individual preferences across the stages of the learning cycle

Claims¶

  • Kolb's inventory successfully differentiated group learning styles by students' subject majors [+W]
  • Kolb's inventory showed no association with medical career choices, and its personality-type predictions were contradicted [-W]
  • Learning styles measured by Kolb's inventory showed no significant change over one year of varied instruction [-W]
