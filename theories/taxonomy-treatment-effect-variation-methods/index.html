Three-part taxonomy of methods for studying variation in treatment effects: pre-intervention subgroup analyses, post-intervention subgroup analyses, and impact estimation under varying effects¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report organizes the quantitative methods literature into three research topics: subgroup (moderator) analyses using participants' characteristics "measured before the intervention is implemented"; subgroup analyses based on "participants' experiences, mediators, and outcomes measured after program implementation"; and impact estimation when treatment effects vary. This taxonomy structures the report's technical guidance for education impact evaluations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to study participant data on characteristics, experiences, mediators, or outcomes, and an impact evaluation design suitable for subgroup or varying-effects estimation

Constraints¶

  • The focus is on randomized controlled trials, though the report states the methods are also applicable to quasi-experimental designs

Target Learners¶

  • students in educational interventions evaluated in impact studies

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • instructional practices and student learning outcomes in education impact evaluations

Claims¶

  • Quantitative methods exist for assessing how impacts of educational interventions on instructional practices and student learning differ across students, educators, and schools [+W]
  • Subgroup analyses in education impact evaluations can also be based on participants' experiences, mediators, and outcomes measured after program implementation [+W]
  • Subgroup (moderator) analyses in education impact evaluations can be based on participant characteristics measured before the intervention is implemented [+W]
  • Methods for estimating impacts when treatment effects vary apply to randomized controlled trials and are also applicable to quasi-experimental designs [+W]
