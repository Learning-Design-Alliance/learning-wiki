Colorado Growth Model as a teacher-effectiveness measure based on student growth percentiles¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Colorado Growth Model (CGM) assigns each student a growth percentile by comparing the student's achievement to that of other students with similar past test scores; the median or average growth percentile of a teacher's students serves as the teacher-effectiveness measure. The article notes the "CGM does not account for other student background characteristics and excludes other features included in many value-added models." Sixteen states currently use or plan to use it as a component of teacher performance evaluations.

  • Excludes other features included in many value-added models used by states and school districts
