# Four academic mindsets framework

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review defines academic mindsets as psycho-social attitudes or beliefs about oneself in relation to academic work, naming four: "I belong in this academic community," "My ability and competence grow with my effort," "I can succeed at this," and "This work has value for me." The framework draws on goal theory, social learning theory, attribution theory, expectancy-value theory, self-efficacy, and locus of control, and includes learned helplessness and stereotype threat as ways positive mindsets are undermined by negative contextual conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom contexts that support belonging, growth beliefs, expectancies of success, and perceived value of the work
#### Constraints
- Mindsets can be undermined by negative contextual conditions such as stereotype threat and learned helplessness

### Target Learners
- Adolescent students in middle and high school

### Target Learning Objectives
- Academic perseverance, academic behaviors, and course grades
