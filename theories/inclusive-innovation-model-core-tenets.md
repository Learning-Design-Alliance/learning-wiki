# Inclusive Innovation model with Core Tenets of co-leadership with those most proximate to the challenge

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Inclusive Innovation is an education R&D model that brings students, families, and community members together with educators, researchers, and solution providers to address challenges affecting historically and systematically excluded communities. It depicts a readiness stage and five main phases, the last being Sustain & Scale. It is predicated on Core Tenets holding that "those who are most proximate to the challenge should be in co-leadership in researching the problem, deﬁning the outcomes, and designing or determining the solution." Participants most often cited sustaining three tenets: student voice and leadership, honoring context expertise and multiple perspectives, and continuity of equity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- District-community teams must include students, families, and community members with lived experience of the challenge alongside educators and researchers
#### Constraints
- The pilots studied allowed understanding of small-scale expansions but not scaling fully into new contexts, as teams did not set out to scale within the data collection period

### Target Learners
- Students from historically and systematically excluded communities

### Target Learning Objectives
- Community-defined education challenges such as adolescent writing, racism and student mental health, and social justice discourse
