# A general statistical model for interim achievement data that jointly represents year-to-year growth and within-year seasonal gains and losses

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces a general statistical model motivated by the seasonal character of interim achievement data. It is "designed to combine features from traditional polynomial models that estimate year-to-year growth but ignore within-year gains and losses with those from piecewise models, which directly estimate within-year gains/losses but do not include terms for year-to-year growth." The model is intended for monitoring progress of students, classrooms, and schools within and across school years.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal interim assessment data with repeated measures across school years, so that both within-year and year-to-year terms can be estimated
#### Constraints
- Traditional polynomial models ignore within-year gains and losses, while piecewise models lack terms for year-to-year growth; the model exists to address both limitations

### Target Learners
- K-12 students taking interim assessments

### Target Learning Objectives
- Monitoring academic achievement growth within and across school years
