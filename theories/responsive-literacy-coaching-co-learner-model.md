# Responsive literacy coaching as a co-learner model grounded in trust and inquiry

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article articulates a framework of eight principles for responsive literacy coaching in which the coach embraces Cambourne's co-learner model rather than positioning herself as the expert. Care and trust are central: "As relationships evolve, coaches seek to understand teachers' ways of knowing" and coaching involves "inquiring, learning together, rethinking, wondering, and exploring multiple instructional possibilities." The coach's primary responsibility is working with teachers, not evaluation, and collaboration produces communities of inquiry in which teaching becomes more deliberate and purposeful.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A coach's primary responsibility must be working with teachers; the coach adopts a co-learner stance rather than an expert or evaluator role
#### Constraints
- Scripted-program environments attempt to simplify the complexities of teaching, which the model resists

### Target Learners
- practicing classroom teachers in elementary, middle, and upper elementary settings

### Target Learning Objectives
- professional growth in literacy instruction
- teacher professionalism and reflective practice
