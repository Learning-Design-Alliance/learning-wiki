# Kolb's experiential learning cycle and four learning styles

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts Kolb's model in which "knowledge is created through the transformation of experience" via a four-stage cycle of concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualization, and active experimentation. Plotting learners on two continua (perception and processing) yields four styles: accommodator, assimilator, converger, and diverger. The study uses this taxonomy to classify participants and to explain why the guided VR mode, which combines virtual exploration with conventional text and images, suits all four learning characteristics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners' styles are measured with the Kolb Learning Style Inventory, whose combined scores place them on the learning styles grid
#### Constraints
- The study collapsed Kolb's four styles into two groups (assimilator and accommodator) using a diagonal split of the grid

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Accommodating individual differences in learning style across instructional treatments
