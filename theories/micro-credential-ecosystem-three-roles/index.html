The micro-credential ecosystem of issuers, earners, and recognizers¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report situates earned micro-credentials within "a larger micro-credential ecosystem" that "requires three unique and distinct roles: issuers, earners, and recognizers." Issuers are organizations such as non-profits and universities supported by Digital Promise to develop micro-credential content; earners are educators who develop and submit evidence of learning; recognizers — districts, states, and others — give credentials value and accept them as evidence of learning in an educator's career.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recognizers must define the various ways micro-credentials have value in an educator's professional career for the ecosystem to function

Constraints¶

  • Issuers operate as organizations supported by Digital Promise to develop micro-credential content

Target Learners¶

  • educators earning credentials
  • district and state decision-makers
  • issuing organizations

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • recognition of demonstrated educator competencies in professional careers

Claims¶

  • As of mid-2017 the Digital Promise micro-credential ecosystem had drawn over 5,000 submissions from educators in approximately 550 districts, with about 55 percent of portfolio submitters awarded a micro-credential [+W]
  • Districts and universities increasingly attach concrete incentives to earning Digital Promise micro-credentials [+W]
  • Nine states offered CEUs for Digital Promise micro-credentials by 2017, but no coherent framework existed for valuing micro-credentials [~W]
