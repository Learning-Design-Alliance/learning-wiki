Differentiated knowledge measures framework: static/dynamic, modality, and quality categories¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that multimedia learning effects should be evaluated with knowledge measures differentiated along three categories. As printed: "We defined three knowledge categories: static/dynamic (process or facts knowledge), modality (verbal or visual knowledge), and quality (passive or active knowledge)." Static/dynamic distinguishes facts (objects, events, symbols) from processes (flows of interdependent causalities); modality derives from dual coding theory's separate verbal and visual channels; quality contrasts active (production/reconstruction) with passive (recognition) knowledge. The framework let the study show that media effects differ by knowledge category rather than being uniform.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning outcome tests must include measures reflecting both static and dynamic knowledge characteristics when the topic contains both

Constraints¶

  • The active/passive distinction is described as heuristic and explorative, not tied to hypothesis testing in this study

Target Learners¶

  • Adult learners in experimental multimedia-learning research

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Assessing knowledge acquisition of facts and processes across verbal, visual, active and passive knowledge forms

Claims¶

  • Media Effects Vary By Knowledge Category [+M]
  • No media effect for process knowledge: animation did not improve learning of dynamic content [+W]
  • Pre-experimental knowledge and education level relate to specific knowledge categories [+W]
