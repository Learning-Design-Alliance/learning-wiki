# The OpenSciEd storyline instructional model: coherence from the students' perspective via four teaching routines

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 9 studies (8 review, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 9 report an effect size

## Description
The storyline instructional model centers on coherence from the students' perspective, emphasizing students' epistemic agency, with the instructional sequence driven by student and peer questions about a phenomenon rather than a premeditated sequence determined by disciplinary experts. The review reports that "the paper describes four teaching routines to promote coherence from the students' perspective: anchoring phenomenon, navigation (i.e., figuring out next steps), putting pieces together (i.e., assembling science ideas and practices), and problematizing (i.e., uncovering gaps or problems in current understanding)". OpenSciEd's distinctive principles include being coherent to students, being phenomena-driven, promoting iterative refinement and consensus-building, and embodying the K-12 Framework vision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to facilitate discussions across initial ideas, building understandings, and consensus discussion types
- Enactment with integrity to the principles of coherence, phenomena-driven instruction, iterative refinement, and consensus-building
#### Constraints
- The review notes a tension that storyline units follow a premeditated sequence of knowledge building that may not be what organically arises from students, and describes pseudoagency as a risk when teachers ask for ideas but fail to honor them

### Target Learners
- middle school science students

### Target Learning Objectives
- three-dimensional NGSS science learning
- epistemic agency in scientific sensemaking
