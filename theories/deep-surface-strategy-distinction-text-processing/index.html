Deep versus surface learning strategies as determinants of scientific text understanding¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organises cognitive learning strategies by the depth of processing they induce. Deep strategies such as "elaboration and organisation" build and integrate information, ensuring coherence and structure and facilitating deeper understanding, and are associated with high-quality outcomes. Surface strategies such as "Repetition and rote learning" focus attention on surface aspects of material rather than meaning. The article also distinguishes the quantity versus quality of strategy application, arguing quality of application relative to purpose is more important for learning outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Understanding of scientific content depends on the type and quality of strategies used to process it

Constraints¶

  • The analysis focuses only on the cognitive components of SRL, not metacognitive, motivational or cooperative dimensions

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students studying scientific texts

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • deep understanding and retention of complex content

Claims¶

  • Cognitive prompts activating deep learning strategies produce a marginally significant immediate exam advantage over prompts activating shallow strategies in a real psychology course [+W]
  • The immediate advantage of deep-processing prompts does not persist at a 5-month follow-up, with a Bayesian test confirming the null [~W]
  • Prompting strategies for activating deep learning alone are insufficient for preserving a long-term learning advantage [~W]
  • Question prompts improve learning [+M]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Ad hoc instructional strategy integrating deep- or shallow-processing cognitive prompts into a psychology coursebook chapter
  • Complement deep-processing cognitive prompts with practice testing, distributed practice, and metacognitive or motivational prompts to support long-term retention
