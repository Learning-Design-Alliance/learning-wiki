# Direct versus Mediated Effects of Retrieval Practice

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 unmarked) · 3 studies, `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A distinction the chapter uses to organize retrieval research. Mediated effects arise when a test guides later study, informs teachers, or motivates effort; in contrast, "Any gains in learning from practicing retrieval, without restudy or feedback, represent direct effects of retrieval processes on learning". The chapter credits the mediated-effects idea to Roediger and Karpicke (2006a).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessing direct effects requires that learners practice retrieval without restudying or receiving feedback afterward.
#### Constraints
- The chapter states that feedback introduces mediated effects and prohibits assessing the direct mnemonic effects of retrieval, even though feedback is beneficial in educational settings.

### Target Learners
- Researchers designing retrieval practice experiments
- Educators using tests and quizzes

### Target Learning Objectives
- Distinguish learning caused by the act of retrieval from learning caused by feedback, restudy, or motivation
