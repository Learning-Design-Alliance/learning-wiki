Untestable-assumptions account for bias in nonexperimental estimation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper frames nonexperimental methods such as regression modeling and statistical matching as unbiased only under assumptions that "are usually not testable." This framing explains why bias in nonexperimental estimators must be assessed indirectly, for example by comparing estimates against experimental benchmarks, and motivates the paper's reassessment of when such methods perform adequately.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Underlying assumptions of the nonexperimental method must hold for estimates to be unbiased

Constraints¶

  • The assumptions are usually not testable directly, per the paper's abstract
