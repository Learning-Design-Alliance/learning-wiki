Three-criterion framework for evaluating test mode comparability¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report organizes its comparability evaluation around three criteria drawn from the score-comparability literature: "there are three general categories of criteria adopted in evaluating comparability: (1) content and construct validity, (2) psychometric properties and reliability, and (3) statistical assumption/test administration condition." Content validity is checked by adherence to blueprint content specifications; the psychometric criterion covers score distributions, reliability, adaptivity, and item exposure; the administration criterion extends to students' test-taking experience via manifest variables such as test duration and length. The framework structures both the simulation and empirical studies of Project Altair.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Same test blueprints and item pools across modes; items not recalibrated after switching engines, so checks focus on RIT score distributions with equal means and standard deviations

Constraints¶

  • Most summarized scenarios in the prior literature (adaptive vs. paper-pencil comparisons) were not directly applicable to this engine-to-engine comparison

Target Learners¶

  • K–8 students taking adaptive interim assessments

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Valid and reliable measurement of achievement comparable across test delivery engines

Claims¶

  • Map Growth Cbe Colo Growth Effect Sizes Small [+M]
  • Cbe Colo Reliability Comparable Cbe Higher Precision [+M]
  • The Altair (engine) main effect is not significant for winter RIT scores in either content area, but an Altair-by-sex interaction is significant in Reading only [+W]
  • CBE shows better engine adaptivity than COLO, especially for extremely low or high achievement students [+W]
  • Item exposure rates are comparable across engines, with most items below 10%, and the CBE Reading item-use rate is lower than COLO's [+W]
  • Test lengths are comparable across engines, but CBE test durations are higher than COLO's in both terms and content areas, more prominently in Reading [+W]
