# Goal-Action-Outcome frame: explanations of many historical events have a generic structure of goal, action, and outcome

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The analysis of Why-Action explanation questions rests on a generic structure attributed to Armbruster and Anderson (1982): "explanations of many historical events have a generic structure consisting of three causally related categories of information" — Goal, Action, and Outcome. The Goal is the desired state sought by an individual or group, the Action is the overt behavior undertaken to accomplish the Goal, and the Outcome is the consequence of the Action. In questions of this type, the Action and/or Outcome is the effect given in the question and the Goal is the cause required in the answer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Answering Why-Action questions requires connecting a stated action to the goal that motivated it, which may require a causal inference because the goal is not always stated explicitly in the text
#### Constraints
- The frame applies to explanations whose causes are goals, motives, purposes, or intentions of human actors; other explanation subcategories handle conditions and processes

### Target Learners
- middle school students answering history textbook questions

### Target Learning Objectives
- constructing cause-oriented answers to history explanation questions
