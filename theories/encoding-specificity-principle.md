# Encoding Specificity Principle

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Tulving and Thomson (1973) state the principle in its broadest form as: only that can be retrieved that has been stored, and how it can be retrieved depends on how it was stored. Its testable form is that the memory trace of an event, and hence what makes an effective retrieval cue, is set by the specific encoding operations performed when the event was studied. A cue works if the information it carries was part of the trace, not because it is generally associated with the item.

The evidence that forced the principle was recognition failure of recallable words. Participants studied target words paired with weak cues. Later they generated strong associates of the targets, often producing the targets themselves, and then failed to recognise many of those targets as studied words, although they could recall them when given the original weak cues. Generation-recognition theories, which hold that recall is generating candidates and then recognising them, cannot explain recall succeeding where recognition fails. The failure persisted under forced-choice recognition with confidence ratings, so it is not response bias, and pairing targets with weak cues at study did not impair generating them from strong associates.

For instruction, the principle explains why knowledge learned with one set of cues can be unavailable in another setting, which is one root of [inert knowledge](inert-knowledge.md) and of context effects in [retrieval practice](transfer-appropriate-processing-account-of-retrieval-practice.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Cued recall and recognition of word lists, where the study cue and the test cue can be varied independently.

### Target Learners
- Adults in laboratory memory studies.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Recall and recognition of studied material; choosing the cues and contexts learners meet at study so that they match the ones they will meet at use.
