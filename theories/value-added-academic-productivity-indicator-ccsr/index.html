Value-added academic productivity indicator estimating each school's contribution to student learning from longitudinal test scores¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Consortium's academic productivity research uses a value-added approach in which, "Using Iowa Tests of Basic Skills (ITBS) data, the productivity indicator estimates each school's contribution, or value-added, to the learning of students enrolled at that school." A more refined statistical model developed with Steve Raudenbush assumes each student has an academic growth trajectory shaped by latent growth parameters and exposure to instruction in a given class and school, permitting estimation of student growth curves and time trends for classroom and school effects. The agenda proposes extending this to classroom-level analysis and comparing it against NCLB adequate-yearly-progress benchmarks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires students' longitudinal test-score data with repeated measures of students over time within different classrooms and schools
  • Extending classroom-level analysis to the whole system depends on a CPS information management system change allowing linking of teacher and student records

Constraints¶

  • A school could be judged as not making adequate yearly progress under NCLB yet show improvement on the productivity measure, and the reverse is also possible

Target Learners¶

  • Chicago Public Schools elementary and high school students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improving student learning gains in reading and mathematics; school accountability and improvement

Claims¶

  • Phase One Weak Gains Disadvantaged Schools [+M]
  • CPS elementary school productivity improvements stalled after 1997, with no evidence of significant productivity growth since then [+W]
  • ITBS test form progression systematically influences eighth-grade math gain scores, with Form L-to-M years showing the largest gains and Form K-to-L years the smallest [~W]
  • CPS elementary ITBS scores improved across the 1990s in both reading and math for all age groups, but trend gains flattened after 1998 [~W]
  • All six CPS age cohorts saw a slight 2001 decline in math scores of less than one month, which a Rasch-score re-analysis suggests reflects an actual drop in learning rather than test-form effects [~W]
