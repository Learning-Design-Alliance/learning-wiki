# Value-added identification of highest-performing teachers as the top 20 percent within district teacher pools

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study defines teacher performance using value-added estimates from a student-level growth model with teacher fixed effects. "Highest -performing teachers" are "those whose value -added estimates in English language arts (ELA), math, or both put them in the top 20 percent of all eligible teachers in their teacher pool within their district", based on two or more years of data. Elementary teachers are ranked on the average of separately standardized ELA and math value-added estimates, and estimates are empirical Bayes-shrunk. The framework is applied to three teacher pools: elementary, middle school math, and middle school ELA.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must have taught in a tested grade and subject for at least two years and be teaching in the most recent academic year
- At least two years of student growth data per teacher to estimate value added
#### Constraints
- Districts C and D used vendor-provided (SAS EVAAS) rankings rather than the study's own model
- Restricting to teachers with complete multi-year data reduces the teacher sample and potentially underestimates differences in teacher quality by school poverty level

### Target Learners
- K-8 students in ten study districts

### Target Learning Objectives
- Identifying and equitably distributing the district's most effective teachers in ELA and mathematics
