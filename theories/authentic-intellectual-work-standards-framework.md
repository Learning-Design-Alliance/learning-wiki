# Authentic intellectual work framework: construction of knowledge, elaborated written communication, and connection to student lives

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The manual's research strand rests on an intellectual foundation drawn from Fred Newmann and Gary Wehlage's work on authentic achievement. It defines a target of student learning that "includes and goes beyond the acquisition of basic knowledge and skills" toward analyzing and solving real-world problems and communicating ideas in elaborated ways. Rubrics operationalize this as three standards per subject: "construction of knowledge," "elaborated written communication," and "connection to student lives," each scored 1 to 3 or 1 to 4, with slightly different definitions for assignments versus student work and by grade and subject.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scoring requires subject-specific rubrics whose standards are, by design, applied differently by grade and subject
- Scoring requires trained raters applying rubrics for each of the three standards, with rescoring of a random subsample to adjust for rater severity
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied to written assignments and written student work in mathematics and language arts/English in a purposive sample of Chicago Annenberg schools
- The report notes this is not the only conceptual framework with associated scoring rubrics that could be applied to assess the intellectual challenge of classroom assignments

### Target Learners
- Grade 3, 6, 8, 9, and 10 students in Chicago public elementary and high schools
- elementary school students in grades three, six, and eight

### Target Learning Objectives
- Deep subject-matter understanding, authentic intellectual work, and elaborated communication of ideas
- construction of knowledge through interpreting, analyzing, and evaluating information
- elaborated written communication
- connecting academic work to problems beyond school

# Authentic intellectual work framework: construction of knowledge, elaborated written communication, and connection to students' lives

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The authentic intellectual work framework, originally developed by the National Research Center on School Organization and Restructuring and adopted by the Chicago Annenberg Research Project, defines ambitious instruction through demands that students apply knowledge to real-world problems, organize and synthesize information, ground solutions in disciplinary concepts, and communicate conclusions effectively. In this study, teacher raters "rated each assignment against the three standards of intellectual challenge: construction of knowledge, written communication, and connection to students’ lives." Rubrics for these standards were applied by teams of Chicago teachers, with scores combined into an overall measure of intellectual quality.
