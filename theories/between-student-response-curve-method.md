# Between-student testing and the response curve as a method for revealing the dynamics of student understanding

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The between-student method, first used in physics by Sayre and Heckler, gives short conceptual quizzes to different sub-groups of the population in successive weeks, avoiding test-retest effects. Plotting average performance over weeks yields a "response curve" sensitive to the current topic of instruction and to exams or homework. The conventional pre/post test corresponds to only the first and last points on the curve and "can miss much of the dynamic evolution of understanding."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires significantly larger student populations and overhead on grouping
- Groups must be approximately normally distributed with the same variance, which the authors confirmed
#### Constraints
- Data were collapsed across quarters by week, appropriate only because the syllabus is unchanged
- The Mechanics and E&M task formulations are not completely isomorphic, so Mechanics and E&M responses are not directly compared

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics students at a university

### Target Learning Objectives
- Newton's Third Law understanding in mechanics and electricity and magnetism
