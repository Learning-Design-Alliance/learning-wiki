# Responsive, resilient school communities built from interlocking student-centered efforts

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report's organizing account is that no single initiative makes a school responsive or resilient; rather, "it is the interlocking of multiple different efforts intended to center and respond to the experiences, perspectives, and needs of students that create a holistic, shared approach to making schools more responsive to their needs." This framework positions schools as protective spaces where coordinated adults, strong climate, and community partnerships jointly buffer the impacts of adverse experiences outside school, and frames this as shared work that cannot be accomplished in isolation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple coordinated efforts rather than a single initiative
- Substantial resources and sustained, hard work
- Partnerships with community-based organizations to extend reach
#### Constraints
- Educators cannot be solely responsible for addressing or preventing violence across the city; public investment in communities is also needed

### Target Learners
- Students in urban public schools serving communities affected by gun violence

### Target Learning Objectives
- Mitigating negative impacts of adversity on academic and behavioral outcomes and supporting holistic wellness
