# Expectancy-Value Framework for Teachers' Motivation to Use AI Applications

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
A theoretical application of Expectancy-Value Theory to why teachers do or do not adopt AI applications in education. The paper holds that "teachers' motivation to use AI technologies in the classroom is shaped by their beliefs about how successfully they can use these technologies (expectancy)" and by their perceptions of the technologies' significance (value), with value split into attainment, utility, intrinsic/interest value, and cost.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The value component is treated as four subcomponents: "attainment value, utility value, intrinsic/interest value, and cost", each examined for its impact on teachers' motivation to use AI applications.
#### Constraints
- The paper is a theoretical examination and reports no data; it proposes that future research examine the specific factors influencing teachers' expectancy and value components, and that "longitudinal studies can provide valuable insights into how teachers' motivations to use AI technologies evolve over time".

### Target Learners
- In-service teachers considering the use of AI applications in their classrooms

### Target Learning Objectives
- Adoption and effective classroom use of AI applications by teachers
- Design of educational policies and teacher training programs for AI integration
