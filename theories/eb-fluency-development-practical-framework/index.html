A practical framework for emergent bilinguals' reading fluency development¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper distills the eleven reviewed studies and the classical Kuhn studies into a sequenced framework for EB fluency instruction: "Begin with accessible, meaningful texts; provide teacher or adult fluent modeling; provide scaffolded practice (e.g., modeling and feedback); have students reread the same text in supported formats; give immediate corrective and motivational feedback". It then connects fluency work to vocabulary, comprehension, and where appropriate bilingual or primary-language support, and closes with choosing appropriately challenging grade-level texts. The framework positions fluency as one strand of broader literacy instruction rather than a standalone skill.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Fluency practice must be explicit, structured, and delivered with texts matched to students' reading levels and instructional goals

Constraints¶

  • Prosody is the missing component: there is a paucity of instructional guidance for EBs on explicit instruction in phrasing, expression, and rhythm

Target Learners¶

  • Emergent bilinguals / English learners in the primary grades

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Reading fluency (accuracy, rate, prosody)
  • Reading comprehension
  • Vocabulary and oral language support

Claims¶

  • Repeated Reading Modeling Feedback Outperform Control Eb Fluency [+M]
  • Readers Theater Alone No Unique Eb Gains Evidence [~M]
  • An AI-supported reading progress tool produced large effect sizes for speed and accuracy but not significant prosody gains for third-grade EFL learners [+W]
  • The HELPS one-to-one fluency program significantly outperformed control on GORT Fluency and GORT-Comprehension for grade 2 Spanish-speaking ELLs [+W]
  • Two years of multicomponent fluency intervention produced greater word-reading and fluency growth than one year or control, with no comprehension differences [+W]
  • Treatment effects were not moderated by emergent bilingual status, pretest levels, or tutoring attendance [+W]
