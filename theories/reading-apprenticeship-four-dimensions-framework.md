# Reading Apprenticeship® four-dimensions framework for integrating literacy into subject-area teaching

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Reading Apprenticeship® calls for teachers to integrate four dimensions of classroom life into subject-area teaching through conversations about the thinking processes students and teachers use as they read. The dimensions are Social (a safe, collaborative environment drawing on students' interest in social interaction), Personal (students' reading identities, purposes, and metacognitive awareness), Cognitive (comprehension and problem-solving strategies for academic texts), and Knowledge-building (knowledge of word construction, vocabulary, text structure, genre, language, and content). The framework underpins both the teacher professional development and the student Academic Literacy curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must assume the role of expert reader, modeling and guiding students' text-based problem solving in content-area classes
#### Constraints
- The framework is implemented through small-group and full-classroom conversations about, and students' reflections on, the thinking process being used while reading

### Target Learners
- middle school, high school, and community college students, including struggling readers, English language learners, special education students, and honors students

### Target Learning Objectives
- engagement, fluency, and comprehension of content-area materials and texts
