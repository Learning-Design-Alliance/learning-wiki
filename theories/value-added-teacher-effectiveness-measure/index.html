Value added as a measure of teacher effectiveness¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The brief defines value added as a way of quantifying teaching quality: "'Value added' is a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains." It frames emerging research on disadvantaged students' access to effective teaching as based on these value-added measures, while noting that researchers have presented such evidence in varied ways, making it hard for practitioners to interpret.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires measures of students' learning gains from which a teacher's contribution can be estimated.

Constraints¶

  • The brief states that because individual researchers have varied in their presentation of this evidence, it is challenging for practitioners to draw lessons from the data.

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 4-8 in the studied districts and states

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Equitable access to effective teaching measured through student learning gains

Claims¶

  • Disadvantaged Students Less Effective Teaching [+M]
  • Access to effective teaching for disadvantaged students varied across districts, from no statistically significant difference to a disparity equivalent to 13 weeks of learning [+W]
  • The average teaching-effectiveness disparity for disadvantaged students equals about four weeks of learning in reading and two weeks in math [+W]
  • Teachers in districts with large fractions of disadvantaged students receive lower value-added ratings when student background characteristics are omitted [+W]
  • Disadvantaged students in 29 study districts receive poorer-quality instruction, on average, compared with other students [+W]
