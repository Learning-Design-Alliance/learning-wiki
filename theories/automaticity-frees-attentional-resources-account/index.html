Automaticity-frees-attention account of transcription skills in early literacy¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

This account holds that achieving automaticity in low-level transcription skills frees attentional resources for higher-level processes. For writing, researchers hypothesize that "achieving letter writing automaticity frees attentional resources for higher level nonautomatic processes such as ideation during the writing process," while slow letter production would interfere with composition. The article extends this logic to spelling: automatized letter writing may free cognitive and phonological resources so children can attend to integrating print knowledge, grapheme-phoneme correspondence, meaning, and orthographic knowledge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners must be simultaneously developing multiple skills such as phonological awareness, letter-sound correspondences, and orthographic knowledge for the resource-freed benefit to matter most

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as a theoretical hypothesis; the spelling relation it motivates was only marginally significant (p = .06) in this study
