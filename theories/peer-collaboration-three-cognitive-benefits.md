# Crook's three cognitive benefits of peer collaboration: articulation, conflict, and co-construction

## Description
The article presents Crook's (1994) account that "peer collaboration offers three cognitive benefits: articulation, conflict, and co-construction." Articulation: students must make intuitive and emerging ideas explicit and public, and the pressure to communicate well helps them gain conceptual clarity. Conflict: when students disagree they must justify and defend positions, forcing reflection, based on the Piagetian perspective of socio-cognitive conflict. Co-construction: students build shared knowledge by complementing and building on each other's ideas, based on the Vygotskian perspective that understanding is first rehearsed between people before it is developed within the learner.
