# Two-stage model of native language acquisition: natural oral-aural stage followed by nonnatural literacy stage

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a stage model of native language acquisition in which the first stage is natural oral-aural development, "better known as speaking and listening," gained in the home before school, and the second stage is the nonnatural process of literacy, reading and writing, which is a cultural skill taught in school. The model holds that the stages "always follow in the proper sequence," so native speakers must be competent in the natural stage before progressing to the nonnatural one. The article applies this model to foreign language learning, arguing schools must supply the first stage.
