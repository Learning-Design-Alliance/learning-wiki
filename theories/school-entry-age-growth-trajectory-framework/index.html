School entry age as a determinant of early-grade academic growth trajectories¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames school entry age as a factor shaping academic growth through second grade, rather than only initial achievement. Its guiding question — "Does entering school older give students an edge?" — and its answer that "an early advantage may fade in later grades" treat entry age effects as trajectory-level, examined with a multi-state regression discontinuity design so that relative-age advantages can be traced as students progress through early grades.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal growth measures across early grades and variation in school entry age to support a regression discontinuity analysis

Constraints¶

  • Applies to academic growth through second grade in a multi-state U.S. context; the brief states the advantage "may fade" rather than estimating a definitive fade-out magnitude
