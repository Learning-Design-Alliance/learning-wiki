Historical thinking defined as skills for analyzing and applying historical content knowledge to interpret the past, distinct from content knowledge itself¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report defines historical thinking as the disciplinary skills students use to interpret the past, distinguishing them from historical content knowledge (facts about periods, themes, regions, people, events). It states: "we define historical thinking as the skills that students employ when they analyze and applyhistorical content knowledge to interpret the past". The report traces how cognitive models vary across English, German, American, and Canadian research strands — English second-order concepts, German historical consciousness, American heuristics such as Wineburg's sourcing and contextualization, and Canadian six-concept frameworks — while noting considerable convergence in overlapping categories across models.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Alignment with recognized frameworks and standards such as the C3 Framework, Common Core, and NAEP

Constraints¶

  • Models differ in emphasis; American models tend to emphasize historical reading skills while German and Canadian models may include a greater moral dimension

Target Learners¶

  • U.S. high school world history students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Interpreting the past through disciplinary practices such as making historical arguments and establishing cause-and-effect relationships

Claims¶

  • Dimensions of historical thinking are conceptually decoupled: sophistication in one second-order concept does not imply sophistication in another [+W]
  • Before developing historical thinking skills, students approach the past with common sense, including presentism — seeing past people as sharing contemporary values [+W]
  • Six historical thinking dimensions — historical argumentation, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing — were selected as rubric assessment targets based on convergence across U.S. national frameworks and the WHP course guide [+W]
  • British empirical research concluded that students do not naturally progress in historical understanding with age but progress because of targeted instruction designed to support it [+W]
  • Empirical progression research is uneven across historical thinking dimensions: most research covers causal reasoning and sourcing, while contextualization and comparison remain thin [+W]
