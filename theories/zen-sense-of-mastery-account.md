# Zen-derived sense-of-mastery account of mastery learning's accumulated benefits

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper develops an account, drawn from Zen training traditions, in which a learner intensely focuses on a very small domain, masters it, and then uses "this sense of mastery as a guide to the tackling of new tasks." The traditional Japanese arts student began "by the tedious mastery of a very small domain," then, having achieved mastery once, knows what mastery feels like and can accomplish it again. The paper parallels this with Japanese students working on just one or two mathematics problems and with Mastery Learning's emphasis on mastery at one stage before moving to the next.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must achieve mastery in an initial small domain so the felt sense of mastery can guide subsequent tasks
#### Constraints
- The paper states that whether Mastery Learning students develop a generalizable sense of mastery is an aspect that has yet to be investigated

### Target Learners
- elementary school mathematics students

### Target Learning Objectives
- deep mastery of mathematics problems
- transfer of a sense of excellence to new tasks
