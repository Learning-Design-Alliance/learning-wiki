# Cummins' theoretical framework relating language proficiency to academic achievement via threshold and interdependence hypotheses

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Cummins proposes that a framework relating language proficiency to academic achievement is needed to set rational entry/exit criteria for bilingual programs. Two premises form its foundation: the threshold hypothesis (two levels of linguistic proficiency, below which bilingualism brings cognitive disadvantages and above which benefits) and the interdependence hypothesis (L1 and L2 academic proficiencies develop interdependently). The framework distinguishes surface fluency from cognitively and academically related aspects of proficiency, later labeled BICS and CALP, and holds that academic skills in L1 and L2 are interdependent through a common underlying proficiency. The article notes the framework 'has need for empirical confirmation'.
