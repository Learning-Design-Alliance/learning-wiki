# Starting-point difference account of the Catholic-public school achievement gap

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames the Catholic school achievement advantage as substantially a starting-point phenomenon: Catholic school students score higher on average in math and reading, but they also enter each school year at a higher level. Within-year growth is faster for public school students, who partially close the gap, and the growth differences depend on grade and subject. This reframes the cross-sectional score gap as one that changes in size during the school year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires longitudinal MAP Growth scores at the start and end of school years to separate entering achievement from within-year growth
#### Constraints
- Growth differences are specific to grade span and subject: earlier grades show less Catholic-school growth in both subjects, while middle school math growth is comparable across sectors

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten through eighth grade students in Catholic and public schools

### Target Learning Objectives
- Understanding sector differences in math and reading achievement growth
