Extensive reading framework: ten tenets and the virtuous circle of the good reader¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article presents extensive reading as an approach in which learners "read quickly large amounts of easy and varied self-selected material to build fluency and consolidate language knowledge", with reading as its own reward. It reproduces Day & Bamford's ten tenets (read as much as possible; materials within linguistic competence; learner selection; faster rather than slower reading; teacher as model reader, among others). It also explains the mechanism via Nuttall's vicious circle of the weak reader and its reversal: easy material produces understanding, enjoyment, speed, and a "virtuous circle of the good reader".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Materials must be at or slightly below the learner's proficiency level so reading can be understood and enjoyed without a dictionary or instructor support
  • A wide variety of self-selected materials must be available, and teachers orient students to the goals and model reading themselves

Constraints¶

  • The approach runs counter to intuitions of students and instructors who expect difficult texts to be necessary for improvement

Target Learners¶

  • foreign language learners from beginner through advanced levels

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • L2 reading fluency and proficiency
  • positive attitudes toward reading
  • lifelong reading habits

Claims¶

  • Extensive reading yields affective benefits: positive attitudes, motivation, confidence, and reduced anxiety, with difficult texts raising anxiety [+W]
  • Extensive reading improves reading comprehension and reading fluency [+W]
  • Extensive reading improves grammatical knowledge and writing style [+W]
  • Extensive reading improves vocabulary acquisition and spelling through repeated meaningful exposure [+W]
  • Meta-analyses consistently validate extensive reading benefits across languages and populations, with program length as a moderator [+W]
  • Reading for pleasure is associated with preserved cognitive functioning, delayed dementia symptoms, and greater general knowledge [+W]
