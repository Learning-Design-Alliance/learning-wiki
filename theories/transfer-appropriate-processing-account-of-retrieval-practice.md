# Transfer-Appropriate Processing Account of Retrieval Practice

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 13 claims (7 for, 3 mixed, 3 against) · 9 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 3 causal, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q4` · 4 of 9 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

## Description
Transfer-appropriate processing holds that performance is best when initial learning processing matches the processing required on a later final assessment. The chapter says it "provides a useful heuristic for explaining why practicing retrieval during learning should bolster long-term retention", illustrated by the need to practice a skill such as playing the violin.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners practice retrieving and using knowledge because the final assessment will require retrieving and using it.
#### Constraints
- The chapter states it does not offer a mechanism that explains how or why retrieval improves learning.
- Its strict prediction, that an exact match between initial and final conditions yields the best performance, has not been supported: initial recall tests tend to beat initial recognition tests regardless of final format.

### Target Learners
- Learners in educational settings
- Researchers comparing theories of retrieval-based learning

### Target Learning Objectives
- Explain why retrieval practice bolsters long-term retention
