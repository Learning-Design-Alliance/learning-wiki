Mental models framework: children's conceptual understandings as coherent, theory-like structures constrained by presuppositions¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article adopts Vosniadou and colleagues' account in which "children represent scientific concepts in the form of theory-like structures that are internally coherent and empirically accurate," structures "constrained by the presuppositions and intuitions known as 'mental models.'" The study uses this framework to classify kindergartners' causal explanations of the day and night cycle into initial (naïve), synthetic, and scientific model categories via model identification methodology. Synthetic models are described as children's attempts to integrate scientific information with their naïve understandings.
