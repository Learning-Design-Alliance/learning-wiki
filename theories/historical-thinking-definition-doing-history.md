# Historical thinking defined as skills for analyzing and applying historical content knowledge to interpret the past, distinct from content knowledge itself

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report defines historical thinking as the disciplinary skills students use to interpret the past, distinguishing them from historical content knowledge (facts about periods, themes, regions, people, events). It states: "we define historical thinking as the skills that students employ when they analyze and applyhistorical content knowledge to interpret the past". The report traces how cognitive models vary across English, German, American, and Canadian research strands — English second-order concepts, German historical consciousness, American heuristics such as Wineburg's sourcing and contextualization, and Canadian six-concept frameworks — while noting considerable convergence in overlapping categories across models.
