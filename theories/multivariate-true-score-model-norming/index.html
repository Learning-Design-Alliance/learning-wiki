Multivariate true score model for building achievement and growth norms¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report uses a model-based approach in which observed scores are decomposed into true scores and error scores, with the standard error of measurement introduced so that imprecision of observed scores is accounted for. The article states it uses "a multivariate true score model that accounts for the known imprecision of scores" to generate both achievement norms per term and between-term growth norms. True scores are assumed to have a multivariate normal sampling distribution, and the joint distribution of predicted fall, winter, and spring scores provides the basis for the norms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Score imprecision must be quantified via the standard error of measurement of each score and entered into the model
  • The joint normality assumption of score components must be reasonable for the validity of inferences

Constraints¶

  • Inferences rely on the reasonableness of the joint normality assumption of score components

Target Learners¶

  • Students in Grades 6–12 taking course-specific MAP Growth tests

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Measuring student achievement status and between-term academic growth in course-specific mathematics and science content

Claims¶

  • Normality assumptions of the true score model appeared reasonable for the course-specific tests [+W]
  • Q-Q plots, CDF curves, and residual checks support the joint normality assumption underlying the multivariate true score model for the course-specific tests. [+W]
  • The multivariate normality assumption of the true score model was judged reasonable for all domains and grades [+W]
  • The multivariate normality assumption of the norming model was judged met for all domains and grades, despite some tail deviations in Q-Q plots [+W]
  • True-score-model correlations between term scores exceed 0.90 for almost all course-specific tests [+W]
