Swain's output hypothesis with three functions of output in second-language acquisition¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's theoretical framework presents Swain's output hypothesis, which assigns production of language a significant role in second-language acquisition alongside comprehensible input. It identifies three functions: a hypothesis-testing function, a metalinguistic function, and the noticing/triggering function, in which "learners may notice a gap between what they want to say and what they can say". The study tests the third function in EFL writing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Production of the target language under circumstances that push learners to generate output

Constraints¶

  • The noticing function operates "under some circumstances", per the quoted Swain passage

Target Learners¶

  • Second-language learners, here an adult EFL learner

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Second-language writing, lexis and grammar

Claims¶

  • Output Noticing Predominantly Lexical [+M]
  • Rewriting Solves More Lexical Gaps [+M]
  • Comprehensible input alone is insufficient for acquisition of high levels of grammatical proficiency [+W]
  • Notetaking as a feedback tool facilitates resolution of lexical problems but leaves grammatical problems unresolved, suggesting reformulation as an alternative [+W]
  • Comparing original texts to model paragraphs prompted noticing that was again focused on lexical features [+W]
