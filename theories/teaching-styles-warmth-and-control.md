# Teaching Styles as Warmth and Control

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
For many years, warmth and control were treated as opposite ends of a single continuum — a teacher who chose to be warm could not also be in control, and vice versa. Soar and Soar (1983) proposed instead that warmth and control are two independent dimensions that can occur in any combination and to any degree, each measured by its own set of classroom behaviors. **Control** runs from low (student spontaneity, risk-taking, student-initiated responses) to high (teacher talk, task orientation, teacher authority). **Warmth** runs from low (frequent reference to formal rules and procedures, punishment, criticism, scolding, reprimanding) to high (praise and rewards, use of student ideas, responsiveness to student requests). Crossing the two dimensions yields four teaching-style profiles:

- **Authoritarian** (high control, low warmth) — a cold, controlling style that may humiliate or criticize students to control their behavior, producing a businesslike, task-oriented classroom with few teacher-student interchanges the teacher didn't initiate. Motivation to do high-level work is driven more by fear of punishment or embarrassment than by expectation of reward.
- **Authoritative** (high control, high warmth) — classroom rules are mutually determined and a consistently applied system of praise and rewards motivates good behavior, so warmth and control coexist. Motivation for good behavior comes from that well-defined reward system, rather than from rules and punishment as in the authoritarian style. Soar and Soar (1983) note a specific risk here: excessive reliance on rewards can create an almost suffocating climate in which only teacher-identified behaviors are eligible for reward, leaving students little room to pursue behavior or activity independently.
- **Permissive** (low control, high warmth) — the teacher praises and rewards frequently while giving students near-complete freedom to set the limits of their own behavior, which can produce confusion at the extreme. In a milder form, praise and rewards flow freely but student spontaneity and risk-taking are bounded to certain times or content areas, with the teacher acting as moderator or co-discussant rather than controller; warmth is conveyed mostly nonverbally, through rules that encourage individual initiative.
- **Neglectful** (low control, low warmth) — a cold, permissive combination in which the teacher spends much of the time scolding and criticizing but has few rules actually constraining the behavior being criticized. This extreme sometimes appears when a substitute teacher takes over unannounced: unfamiliar with established rules and unable to fall back on them, and with a role limited to keeping order rather than creating rules, the substitute ends up "holding the line" mainly through criticizing, reprimanding, and punishing.

An effective classroom management plan blends warmth and control in a way that avoids the overly rigid, dictatorial form of control found in the authoritarian style — using instead a mutually agreed-upon set of rules paired with a well-defined, consistently applied system of praise and rewards, striking a genuine balance between the two dimensions rather than treating them as a single trade-off.

## Implications

### Context
- Applies to the ongoing, everyday climate a teacher establishes through rule-setting, response to misbehavior, and use of praise and reward — not to any single isolated classroom event
- The four profiles describe corners of a continuous space; real teachers fall at varying points and may shift position across contexts (e.g., a normally authoritative teacher managing a lesson very strictly during a fire drill)

### Target Learners
- All students are affected by the ambient teaching style, though see [Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms](../principles/culturally-responsive-classroom-norms.md) [~M] for how the same style can land differently depending on a student's cultural background (e.g. an interdependent-self student may experience a permissive, low-structure climate as unsettling rather than liberating)

### Target Learning Objectives
- Sustained work engagement and reduced disruptive behavior through a climate students want to remain part of, rather than one they comply with only under threat

## Claims
- [Rewarding an already-intrinsically-motivating activity can reduce future engagement with it](../claims/overjustification-effect-reduces-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M] — the authoritative style's own documented risk (a reward system so tightly managed it crowds out independent behavior) is a specific case of this broader effect

## Related Theories
