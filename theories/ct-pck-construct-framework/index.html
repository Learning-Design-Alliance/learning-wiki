CT–PCK construct framework: five core computational thinking practices integrated into middle school math via three instructional strategies¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The survey's organizing framework defines CT–PCK as teachers' knowledge of content and of instructional strategies that make content accessible, operationalized through five core CT practices drawn from a literature review: "pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, debugging, and algorithm development." Items were mapped to middle school math topics from Common Core grades 6–8 and to three instructional strategies for CT integration adapted from Rich et al. (2020): framing the lesson, prompting, and inviting reflection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Item content must align with CT practices from established CT frameworks, state math standards topics, and instructional strategies for CT integration

Constraints¶

  • Psychometric analyses supported the practices as a single overall construct rather than five separable dimensions in the 16-item version; a multidimensional measure remains future work

Target Learners¶

  • Middle school math teachers (grades 6–8)

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Pedagogical content knowledge for integrating computational thinking into math instruction

Claims¶

  • Ct Pck 16 Item Single Construct [+M]
  • Ct Pck Content Face Validity [+M]
  • The overall CT–PCK Survey functioned similarly across teacher characteristic groups, though some items showed raw differential item functioning above 0.64 logits [~W]
  • Teachers who were White, female, or had five or more years of experience scored about 14 percentage points higher; emergency-certified teachers scored 14 points lower [~W]
  • The 16-item CT–PCK Survey shows internal consistency reliability around .71–.72 and person separation reliability of .69 [+W]
  • Teachers scored across a wide range on the CT–PCK Survey, averaging about 61 percent correct, with 92 percent scoring above chance [+W]
