Evidence reviews as projects that identify, assess, and summarize existing research to support evidence-based program decisions¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames evidence reviews as "projects that identify, assess, and summarize existing research in a given area" that play an important role in supporting the quality of program evaluations and how findings are used. Their goals and standards determine conclusions about whether an evaluation is of high quality or a program is effective, and they "encourage the use of sound scientific principles to identify, select, and implement evidence-based programs."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Reviews require stated goals and standards that determine their conclusions about evaluation quality and program effectiveness.

Constraints¶

  • Conclusions depend on each review's own goals and standards, which vary across reviews.
