# Equations as Language

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
Physics instruction often treats an equation as fully understood once a student can derive or correctly manipulate it. This theory argues that deriving an equation and comprehending it are separate achievements — analogous to the difference between decoding text and reading it with comprehension. Adapting a reading-comprehension framework from literacy research, the theory identifies three levels of understanding a physics equation:

- **Textually explicit**: understanding what each variable and symbol directly means and how they are stated to relate (e.g., "kinetic energy depends on frequency and work function").
- **Textually implicit**: inferring relationships the equation doesn't state outright, through mathematical reasoning alone (e.g., inferring that higher frequency implies higher kinetic energy from the equation's form).
- **Scriptally implicit**: making inferences that integrate the equation with prior physical knowledge and constraints outside the equation itself (e.g., recognizing that kinetic energy cannot be negative implies a threshold frequency must exist).

A companion idea, anticipating mathematical formalisms through intuitive sensemaking, holds that reading comprehension at these levels is best built by first developing informal, mechanistic reasoning about a phenomenon's quantitative behavior *before* the formal equation is introduced, so the equation can later be read as a refinement of an already-existing intuition rather than an opaque symbolic object.

## Implications

### Context
- Most naturally studied in authentic, open-ended inquiry (e.g., a student deriving and interpreting an equation for a novel research question) rather than a single textbook problem, since scriptally-implicit understanding depends on integrating knowledge beyond the equation itself
- Distinguishes the time cost of derivation (often minutes) from the time cost of interpretation (often much longer)

### Target Learners
- Students engaged in extended, equation-centered scientific inquiry (e.g., a year-long research project), though the reading-comprehension levels are a general lens applicable to any physics-equation instruction

### Target Learning Objectives
- Moving beyond correct derivation or manipulation of an equation toward flexible interpretation of what it means and implies
- Building the capacity to use an equation as a tool for further inquiry, not just as an answer to a solved problem
