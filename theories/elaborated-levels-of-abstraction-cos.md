# Elaborated theory of levels of abstraction with a parallel set of levels for computational operation schemes

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article elaborates Battista's (2007) theory of levels of abstraction—perceptual/recognition, internalized, and interiorized levels—by adding an empirically supported parallel set of levels for computational operation schemes (COS), from perceptual abstraction through internalization, interiorization, second interiorization (operating on the COS as a conceptual symbol), third interiorization (algebraic generalization), and fourth interiorization (symbols as pointers to generalized computations). The authors state the new set "served to explain students' abstractions of computational operation schemes", occurring in parallel with the original set. This elaborated theory is used to explicate the fading mechanism central to CF.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analysis requires framing students' conceptual progressions in a specific mathematical domain, here permutation enumeration with spatial material
#### Constraints
- The additional levels were developed from data analysis of two students' combinatorial reasoning in one-on-one teaching experiments

### Target Learners
- undergraduate preservice middle school teachers

### Target Learning Objectives
- combinatorial reasoning and permutation enumeration
- transition from concrete manipulatives to symbolic reasoning
