# Seven-dimensional outcome comparison framework for analyzing implicit learning outcomes in summative assessments

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops and applies a comparison framework that identifies characteristics of summative assessments in seven dimensions: cognitive process, transferability, depth of analysis, interdependence, novelty, scaffolding and communication. It is used to compare implicit learning outcomes between Engineering and Engineering Technology programs by scoring final-exam questions. The authors state it was "adapted from taxonomies and outcome principles from the literature" and that "the framework we have developed is intended to allow that comparison" of course rigor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Comprehensive summative assessments such as final exams that address most course learning goals; content specialists trained and calibrated in the framework, with instructor input for the novelty, scaffolding and communication dimensions
#### Constraints
- The three dimensions of novelty, scaffolding and communication require instructor input and cannot be scored independently by content specialists
- The authors note the approach relies on final exams as a proxy measure of implicit learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- post-secondary Engineering and Engineering Technology students

### Target Learning Objectives
- introductory calculus and physics learning outcomes, including cognitive process, transferability, depth of analysis, interdependence, novelty, scaffolding and communication expectations
