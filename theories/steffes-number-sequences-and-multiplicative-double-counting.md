# Steffe's Number Sequences and Multiplicative Double Counting

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
Steffe's constructivist model describes children's numerical reasoning as progressing through three number sequences, each defined by what a child can mentally do with composite units (groups of ones treated as a single countable unit):

- **Initial Number Sequence (INS)**: the child operates only on units of one; numbers are counts built up one at a time, and the child cannot decompose or recompose a number into parts.
- **Tacitly Nested Number Sequence (TNS)**: the child recognizes that a smaller number is nested within a larger one (e.g., 7 is nested within 10) but cannot reverse this nesting — the parts cannot be disembedded while keeping track of the whole.
- **Explicitly Nested Number Sequence (ENS)**: the child can reverse nesting — mentally decomposing a composite unit into parts while retaining the relationship between the parts and the whole (e.g., knowing 7 = 5 + 2 while still holding onto "7").

This reversibility at the ENS stage is the conceptual prerequisite for **multiplicative double counting (mDC)**: simultaneously and intentionally tracking two unit types at once — the composite units (e.g., towers) and the ones within each composite unit (e.g., cubes per tower) — to produce a third unit (the total). mDC is unit-transforming (operating on two unit types produces a new one), in contrast to additive reasoning, which is unit-preserving (operating on one unit type produces the same unit type).

## Implications

### Context
- Number sequences are inferred from how a child solves single-digit addition or counting tasks, not taught directly as stages
- mDC is most cleanly elicited with composite-unit tasks (e.g., "6 towers of 3 cubes each — how many cubes?") that require simultaneously tracking towers and cubes

### Target Learners
- Elementary students (studied in fourth grade) still developing multiplicative reasoning after formal multiplication instruction has begun

### Target Learning Objectives
- Diagnosing a child's current number-sequence stage from ordinary addition behavior, without a separate assessment
- Building instructional bridges from additive to multiplicative reasoning by targeting the ENS transition (decomposing and recomposing composite units)

## Examples
- A child solving 8+7 by counting-on ("8; 9-10-11...15") is inferred to be at the Initial Number Sequence — reasoning on units of one.
- A child solving 8+7 by break-apart-make-ten ("take 2 from 7, give to 8 to make 10, then 10+5=15") is inferred to have reached the Explicitly Nested Number Sequence — reversibly decomposing 7 into 5 and 2.
- A child counting "one-tower-is-3-cubes, two-is-6, three-is-9...six-is-18" for 6 towers of 3 cubes is engaging in multiplicative double counting — a "many-for-1" count, as opposed to counting-on's "1-for-1" count.
