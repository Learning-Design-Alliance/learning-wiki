Framework of four logics of accountability crossing process-outcome and external-internal dimensions¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article theorizes accountability as four logics generated by crossing two analytical dimensions: process versus outcome accountability and external versus internal accountability. The authors state, "we use two analytical dimensions: (1) process versus outcome accountability (Patil, Vieider, & Tetlock, 2014) and (2) external versus internal accountability (Newmann, 1997)." Crossing these yields a 2x2 matrix of control-based, professional-based, test-based, and process-based accountability, each defined by where external control over outcomes and processes sits.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The framework requires examining control over four school functions: assessment, human resources, curriculum, and budget

Constraints¶

  • The framework is applied at the country level of analysis, and the authors acknowledge variations across regional contexts within countries

  • school leaders and principals

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding how accountability systems distribute control over school functions

Claims¶

  • A few countries follow relatively pure accountability logics, but most countries follow mixed forms [+W]
  • Distributions of external control scores differ across school-function domains, with curriculum skewing toward internal control [+W]
  • Country classifications of control type differ sharply by domain: mixed control dominates assessment, internal control dominates curriculum [+M]
  • Across 38 countries, external control is highest in assessment and lowest in curriculum and budget [+W]
  • Extreme country cases: Spain reports universal external control of hiring while the Netherlands reports universal internal control of HR, curriculum, and budget [+W]
