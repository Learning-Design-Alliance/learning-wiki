Goal-directed coaching process staged as joint planning, action/practice, observation, individual reflection, feedback, and reflective discussion in a loop¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article adapts Hanft, Rush, and Shelden's (2004) coaching stages framework and adds a stage to produce its own process model. "Successful coaching requires the progression through specific stages of the process; as such, the stages of coaching are also considered active ingredients within our framework." The stages are joint planning, action/practice, observation, individual reflection, feedback, and a newly added reflective discussion stage emphasizing the partnership's bidirectional nature, looping back to joint planning until goals are internalized. Coaches were provided a coaching protocol that gave structure as they went through the coaching.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A coaching protocol providing structure through the stages
  • Joint determination by coach and teacher of when to end coaching based on individual teacher progress

Constraints¶

  • The additional reflective discussion stage was derived from this qualitative study's analyses rather than the original framework

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 rural science teachers receiving technology-delivered instructional coaching

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Developing and internalizing new instructional practices, here scientific inquiry teaching

Claims¶

  • Teachers and coaches in the qualitative study perceived that coaching increased teacher knowledge and practice, with sustainable practices and positive student learning outcomes [+M]
  • Four relational components—positive feedback, rapport, mutual respect, and reciprocal trust—form the relational framework of coaching [+W]
  • Teacher buy-in and willingness to engage are viewed as strong mediators of coaching benefits [~W]
