Value-added modeling as a framework for measuring educator effectiveness¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (3 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Value-added modeling is a statistical framework for estimating the contribution that schools and teachers make to student achievement growth, operationalized here for use in a district educator evaluation system. The report frames its purpose as describing "the value-added models that are used as part of the BRIDGE teacher and principal evaluations," applying the framework at both school and teacher levels within one district's evaluation program.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal student achievement data linked to individual teachers and schools

Constraints¶

  • Applied within a single district evaluation context (Charleston County School District) and a specific school year

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in public school districts

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Quantifying educator contributions to student academic growth for evaluation purposes

Claims¶

  • Principals with higher FFL scores make larger estimated contributions to student achievement growth (concurrent validity) [+W]
  • FFL scores were not associated with school leaders' estimated contributions to student achievement growth [-W]
  • FFL evaluation scores are significantly and positively correlated with principals' contributions to student achievement (value-added) [+W]
  • FFL scores correlate more strongly with contributions to math achievement than to achievement in other subjects [+W]
