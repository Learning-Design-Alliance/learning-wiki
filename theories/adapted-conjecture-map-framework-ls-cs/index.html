Adapted conjecture map framework for learning scientists and computer scientists¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An adaptation of Sandoval's conjecture mapping tool, created by Chang and Roschelle, that supports interdisciplinary design-based research teams in connecting learning theory to technical design. It distinguishes back-end technical decisions (e.g., training data for an AI model) from embodied front-end decisions users directly interact with, and it explicitly includes both beneficial and harmful consequences of technology as embodiments or mediating processes. In this report the framework structured a one-hour collaborative session in which the PECAS Mediator team filled out the map together on a Jamboard template.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers from both disciplines contribute claims about their own work, which the map's fields then connect

Constraints¶

  • The authors caution that the visual linearity of conjecture maps may lend itself to technological determinism and urge treating the map as an evolving tool frequently re-visited and updated

Target Learners¶

  • interdisciplinary research teams of computer scientists and learning scientists

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • aligning technical goals with learning goals in technology-supported learning interventions

Claims¶

  • First-time computer scientist users' contributions fit naturally into the adapted conjecture map format [+W]
  • Collaborative conjecture map construction surfaced a tension between nuanced learning theory and limited technical capability [+W]
  • The adapted conjecture map's framing around unintended consequences proved generative for interdisciplinary discussion [+W]
