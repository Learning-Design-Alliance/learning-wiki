WWC attrition model: model-based, empirically supported bounds on attrition bias in randomized controlled trials¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The WWC attrition model is a framework for limiting attrition bias in assessments of intervention effectiveness: it establishes a standard for acceptable levels of sample attrition in randomized controlled trials by deriving bounds on the bias attrition can induce. The article explains "the WWC attrition model, how that model is used to establish attrition bounds," and notes the standard "may constitute the first use of model-based, empirically supported bounds on attrition bias in the context of a federally sponsored systematic evidence review." The bounds depend on parameter values, most importantly the maximum tolerable bias.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parameter values for the attrition model, including the maximum level of bias the evidence review is willing to tolerate, must be specified to establish attrition bounds.

Constraints¶

  • The standard was developed for randomized controlled trials reviewed in federally sponsored systematic evidence reviews such as the WWC, the Home Visiting Evidence of Effectiveness Review, and the Teen Pregnancy Prevention Evidence Review.

Target Learners¶

  • Researchers and evidence reviewers evaluating education and human-services interventions

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Judging whether sample attrition in an RCT threatens the validity of effectiveness estimates
