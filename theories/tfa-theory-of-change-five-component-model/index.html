TFA Theory of Change: a data-driven five-component program model from recruitment through alumni leadership¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review distills TFA's Theory of Change from TFA's 2010–15 Business Plan. TFA "uses a data‐driven program model comprising (1) recruitment, (2) a rigorous selection process, (3) intensive pre‐service training for selected corps members, (4) two years of ongoing professional development for corps members, and (5) programming that fosters alumni leadership". Selection identifies candidates likely to succeed; training is guided by a Teaching as Leadership approach codifying a goal-oriented approach to teaching; ongoing mentoring provides regular feedback on teaching impact; alumni continue to receive professional development and leadership resources. The model hypothesizes these skills and attitudes have a larger positive effect on achievement for students of TFA corps members and alumni than non-TFA teachers.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Corps members complete a five-week intensive summer training and agree to teach in their assigned school for at least two years

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students in high-poverty rural and urban public schools in the USA

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Academic achievement in math, English Language Arts, and science

Claims¶

  • Teaching by first- or second-year TFA corps members shows no significant effect on elementary-grade students' reading compared with non-TFA novice teachers [~W]
  • The evidence base for estimating TFA's effects on student academic outcomes reduces to just four studies [~W]
  • Prior experimental studies of TFA consistently showed a positive, statistically significant effect on student math achievement but no discernable effect on reading [~W]
  • TFA shows a small positive effect on reading for early elementary students (PreK to grade 2) but not in math [~W]
