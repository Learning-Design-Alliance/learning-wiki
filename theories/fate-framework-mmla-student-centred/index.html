FATE framework for MMLA: fairness, accountability, transparency, and ethics as student-centred evaluation dimensions¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes the evaluation of multimodal learning analytics around four dimensions synthesized from prior FATE research in AI and learning analytics. Fairness concerns unbiased analytics and "the equitable and impartial reporting and visualization of these analytics"; accountability concerns distributed responsibility and data access; transparency concerns articulation of data-driven insights so students can understand and trust them; ethics concerns privacy and informed consent. The study operationalizes each dimension through student interviews after an authentic MMLA deployment.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Evaluation of MMLA systems must engage students who actually used the system in an authentic learning setting, not laboratory mock-ups

Constraints¶

  • The framework was instantiated in one high-fidelity healthcare simulation context with nursing students; the article presents it as addressing a gap in authentic deployments rather than as a validated instrument

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate nursing students in healthcare simulation courses

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • reflective learning from multimodal analytics of collaboration and communication
  • ethical awareness of learning data practices
