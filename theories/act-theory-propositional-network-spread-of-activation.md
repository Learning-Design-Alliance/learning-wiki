# Anderson's ACT theory of memory: declarative knowledge as a propositional network activated by spread of activation

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report describes Anderson's ACT theory as a general theory of the architecture and basic processes of the human information processing system, one aspect of which is memory. It assumes two basic kinds of knowledge, declarative and procedural, with declarative knowledge stored as interconnected propositions. Activation spreads from currently active propositions to closely related ones, and long-term recall consists of getting target propositions into an active state. The project tested the theory's postulate that "elaborate processingof information should facilitateretrieval of information fromlong-term memory".
