# Elaborative Retrieval Account

## Description
Proposed by Carpenter (2009), this account holds that "semantic elaboration occurs during the process of retrieval and enhances subsequent recall". When given a cue, learners generate several semantically related items that are incorporated with the target into an elaborated, more recallable trace. The chapter credits it with a plausible mechanism and testable predictions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Retrieval cues must afford semantic elaboration, such as weakly associated cue-target pairs.
#### Constraints
- The chapter states attempts to directly induce the proposed elaboration have not produced results similar to retrieval practice effects.
- Retrieval practice effects with nonverbal materials that afford few semantic elaborations pose a challenge to the account.
- Mediator-strength data from Carpenter and Yeung (2017) provide equivocal evidence for it.

### Target Learners
- Researchers comparing theories of retrieval-based learning

### Target Learning Objectives
- Explain the mechanism by which retrieval enhances later recall
