# Three learning-theory areas organize cyberlearning theory work: collaborative learning, embodied learning, and identity

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report's learning-theories theme treats three areas: collaborative learning, where people 'learn socially through shared words, actions, and meanings' with social tools; embodied learning, where theories 'seek to understand how movements, gestures, and actions influence learning' and design integrates gesture with technology; and identity, where sociocultural theories examine how a person forms identity situationally and learning is about developing identity, not just knowledge or skill.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning theories are interwoven with emerging technology and research methods to uncover processes of how learning unfolds
#### Constraints
- Cognitive theories and neuroscience theories also inform the work but are treated in other themes or overlaps

### Target Learners
- Learners from young children to adults in the workforce, including ELL students

### Target Learning Objectives
- STEM topics, language learning, reading, writing, and workforce skills
