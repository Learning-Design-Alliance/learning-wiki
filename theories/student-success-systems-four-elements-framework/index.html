Student success systems framework: four essential elements for secondary schools¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The GRAD Partnership framework, summarized in this report, defines student success systems as "an evidence-based approach to organizing school communities to better support the academic progress, career and college transitions, and well-being of all students." It holds that "High-quality, student success systems combine four essential elements": strong supportive relationships, real-time actionable holistic data, strategic improvement actions, and student-centered mindsets, supported by team infrastructure practices such as clear roles, collaborative improvement planning, and regular data-review cadences. The report uses this framework as the lens for evaluating each district-led innovation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School-based teams with defined roles and protected meeting time
  • Integrated data combining predictive ABC indicators, well-being measures, and first-hand insights

Constraints¶

  • The report notes technology must strengthen, not replace, trust-based human connections

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • graduation on a pathway to adult success through higher education and job training

Claims¶

  • Ninth Grade Off Track Graduation Risk Disparities [+M]
  • Demopolis High School's Google Sheets-based student tracker was followed by a 7% drop in chronic absenteeism and over 3% change in graduation rate [+W]
  • Lynwood Unified's custom dashboards and intervention tools were followed by a decrease in high school D/F rates of over 8% and 593 interventions logged in one year [+W]
