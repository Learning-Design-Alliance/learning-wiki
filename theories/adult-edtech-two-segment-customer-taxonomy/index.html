Two-segment customer taxonomy for the adult learning ed-tech market: learning providers as primary segment, independent learners as secondary segment¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The guide organizes the adult learning market around two customer segments. The primary segment is learning providers — institutions and organizations serving low-skilled learners, including community colleges, K-12 districts, community-based organizations, workforce development centers, libraries, and correctional institutions. The secondary segment is independent learners, a direct-to-learner market the guide calls "yet unproven" but showing indicators of potential. The taxonomy structures the guide's analysis of customer objectives, pain points, and value propositions, with a table listing each segment's objectives (e.g., increase retention and completion rates) and pain points (e.g., limited funding, time and access constraints).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Products must be matched to the specific segment's objectives and pain points as identified in the guide's customer-objectives table

Constraints¶

  • The direct-to-learner model is described as yet unproven, and the guide states not enough research exists to know what approaches would work with which independent learners

Target Learners¶

  • low-skilled adult learners in the United States
  • adult basic education and ESL learners

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • basic skills, literacy, and numeracy
  • ESL and English language skills
  • high school equivalency preparation
  • job-based and digital literacy skills

Claims¶

  • 36 Million Low Skilled Adults 11 Percent Served [+M]
  • US federal adult education funding structures (AEFLA and Title I) are fragmented and lack purchasing leverage, making scale difficult for ed-tech entrepreneurs [+W]
  • Per-learner technology spending is far lower in adult education (about $50) than in K-12 (about $145), signaling stagnant innovation in the adult market [+W]
