# Theory of Mental Self-government as a five-dimension taxonomy of thinking styles

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study, `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes thinking styles measurement around Sternberg's Theory of Mental Self-government, which it calls a popular theory that "includes the most elements of thinking styles and structures that correspond with both Western and Eastern contexts." The theory "divides multidimensional styles of thinking into 5 dimensions": functions (legislative, executive, judicial), forms (monarchic, hierarchic, oligarchic, anarchic), levels (global, local), scope (internal, external), and leanings (liberal, conservative), producing a profile of styles rather than a single style.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement of the styles requires a self-report scale created according to the theory's concepts, since styles are characteristics hidden in each individual and difficult to measure directly
#### Constraints
- The article notes the original Thinking Style Inventory showed low reliability in Eastern society and culture contexts and could not clarify which style an individual has in each dimension

### Target Learners
- sixth grade students

### Target Learning Objectives
- understanding individual differences in learners' thinking styles to inform learning activities and educational measurement and evaluation
