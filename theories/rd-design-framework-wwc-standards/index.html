Regression discontinuity design framework for estimating intervention effects at a cutoff¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The document describes the RD design as a method for obtaining unbiased estimates of education intervention effects when a continuous scoring rule assigns units to treatment or comparison around a pre-set cutoff. The effect is estimated as the difference between the treatment and comparison regression lines at the cutoff: "an effect occurs if there is a 'discontinuity' in the two regression lines at the cutoff." The estimate pertains to units right at the cutoff.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The relationship between the outcome and forcing variable can be modeled correctly
  • The forcing variable was not manipulated to influence treatment assignments

Constraints¶

  • The estimate pertains to average treatment effects for units right at the cutoff, not the full score range

Claims¶

  • Regression discontinuity impact estimates for two education interventions were not significantly different from experimental impact estimates, though point-estimate differences were sometimes nontrivial [+W]
  • A regression discontinuity study found a positive but non-significant effect of +0.16 on standardized literacy growth [+W]
  • Robust RDD methods protect against model misspecification but require much larger sample sizes to detect small effects [+W]
  • Offering SES to eligible applicants near the cutoff in oversubscribed districts has no statistically significant impact on reading or math achievement [+W]
  • Participating in SES (averaging about 21 hours) shows no statistically significant impact on reading or math achievement for students near the cutoff [+W]
