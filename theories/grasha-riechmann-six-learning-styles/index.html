Grasha-Riechmann six student learning styles classification¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Student Learning Style Questionnaire developed by Grasha and Riechmann isolates six learning styles in pairs of two: competitive, collaborative, avoidant, participant, dependent, and independent. These are based on students' attitudes toward learning, views of teachers and peers, and reactions to classroom procedures. The scheme reflects orientation toward course content, toward teacher and peers, and classification based on aspirations and motives.

Claims¶

  • Preservice teachers' reflective thinking tendencies differ statistically by learning style in all seven dimensions [+W]
  • Preservice teachers' epistemological beliefs differ significantly by learning style in all four dimensions [+W]
  • Independent-style preservice teachers score higher on open-mindedness than avoidant, collaborative, and competitive styles [+W]
  • Avoidant-style preservice teachers hold stronger certainty-of-knowledge beliefs than participant-style peers [+W]
  • Collaborative-style preservice teachers score higher on continuous and intentional thinking than avoidant and dependent styles [+W]
