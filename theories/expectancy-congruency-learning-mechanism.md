# Expectancy-congruency learning mechanism updating lexical representations from context fit

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The chapter proposes that lexical organization is driven by an expectancy-congruency mechanism: the learner updates a word's lexical representation based on how well the current contents of memory match the information in the local environmental context. The abstract states this mechanism "updates lexical representations based on the fit between the current contents of memory and the information in the local environmental context," producing "a well-structured lexicon that is adapted to the demands of lexical retrieval and processing." It integrates the rational principle of likely need with mechanistic accounts in which information that is novel and surprising, rather than redundant with expectations, is encoded.
