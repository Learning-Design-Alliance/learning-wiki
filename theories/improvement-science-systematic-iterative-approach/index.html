Improvement Science is a systematic approach to identifying and solving educational problems through iterative testing, data collection, reflection and refinement¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The white paper defines Improvement Science as "a systematic approach to identifying and solving educational problems through iterative testing, data collection, systematic reflection, and refinement" focused on enhancing educational processes and systems. It frames this as a framework for continuous improvement in which educators identify root causes, prototype and test solutions, and refine practices based on data. The paper uses this framework as the organizing lens for integrating SEERNet's data capabilities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators engage in cycles of inquiry, reflection, and action with data collection to test innovations and inform decisions

Constraints¶

  • The paper notes Improvement Science often has had access only to administrative data such as student rosters, surveys, and course grades, not detailed records of how students learn with online resources

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 and community college students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improving educational processes and systems to achieve better outcomes for all students

Related Theories¶

  • The six principles of Improvement Science: problem-focused, variation, system analysis, measurement, disciplined inquiry, and networked improvement communities
  • Convergent science: integrating learning sciences and computer science expertise
  • Network-based continuous improvement in education

Examples¶

  • Run PDSA cycles in which researcher-practitioner teams test modified assignments against as-is versions across multiple classrooms
  • Five recommendations for integrating Improvement Science with SEERNet
  • Six-stage cycle of inquiry connecting indicators to supports
  • Ground annual improvement cycles in explicit theories of improvement and shared common achievement data rather than in vision-statement refinement alone
