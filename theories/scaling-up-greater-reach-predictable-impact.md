# Scaling up defined as achieving greater reach with predictable, measurable impact

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The chapter defines scaling up, building on McDonald et al. (2006), as "achieving greater reach with predictable, measurable impact." Greater reach captures expansion without prescribing how growth is counted, while predictable, measurable impact requires measuring learning for a large student population. The authors add "predictable" because variability across local settings is intrinsic to scale, and teams must make sense of and adapt to that variability. Unlike the source definition, they do not assume scaling occurs only after an innovation is proven.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measures of learning relevant to the innovation's goals must exist so impact can be assessed at scale
#### Constraints
- Progress in reach and impact does not always occur at the same time

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in inquiry-based science and mathematics programs

### Target Learning Objectives
- Inquiry skills such as critical thinking and problem solving
