# Sociocultural Model of L2 writing

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Sociocultural Model emphasizes the social and contextual situatedness of writing as an activity, stressing language as a social, cultural and interactional phenomenon rather than a property of the individual writer. Inspired by Vygotsky's Sociocultural theory, it holds that "learning to write is viewed as collaborative and socially constructed", with students and teachers co-creating meaning through interaction and reciprocal teaching. It also allows L2 writers to express social identity beyond linguistic fluency and mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must adopt the role of a facilitator rather than a content provider or authority figure, and students must transition from passive learners to active actors
#### Constraints
- The article states sociocultural studies have mostly been centered around tutoring and small-sized classrooms, and it is unclear whether the approach benefits all students or only active participants

### Target Learners
- Second language writers in classrooms and tutoring settings

### Target Learning Objectives
- Collaborative writing development and social identity expression in L2 writing
