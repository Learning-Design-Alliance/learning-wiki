Privacy as a plural, context-dependent concept operationalized as restriction of access to personal information¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames privacy as a concept lacking universal definition, quoting Solove that "privacy is not reducible to a singular essence; it is a plurality of different things." Definitions vary by time period, cultural norms, physical location, and field of study. For its case study, the article adopts the operational definition of privacy as "the restriction of access to an individual's personal information," and draws on Nissenbaum's contextual and Solove's bottom-up approaches.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Operational definitions must be chosen for the specific study context given the absence of a universal definition

Constraints¶

  • The operational definition was adopted specifically for this case study of a student success information system

Target Learners¶

  • higher education administrators and researchers studying learning analytics privacy

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • conceptualizing privacy for institutional learning analytics policy and practice

Claims¶

  • Conceptual unclarity in LA: privacy is used interchangeably or in overlapping ways with ethics, security, and anonymity [+W]
  • Most LA research articles (74%) address privacy without defining or conceptualizing it [+W]
