# Self-management (SM) intervention framework: eleven self-directed elements spanning assessment, monitoring, and evaluation of behavioral performance

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review defines SM as "a set of strategies that students are trained in to assess, monitor, and/or evaluate their own behavioral performance", drawing on behavioral psychology and cognitive-behavioral theories. An SM intervention comprises one or a combination of 11 elements, from self-selecting and self-defining a target behavior, self-determining a performance goal, and self-identifying reinforcers, through self-observing, self-recording, and self-charting observations, to self-appraising performance and self-administering primary or secondary reinforcers. The framework distinguishes a training stage from a procedural stage in which students enact these processes during implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should be trained in the skills needed for SM before they actually engage in following SM procedures
#### Constraints
- The review notes no manualized SM programs are available that sequence empirically supported elements of student training, so training type, quality, and degree vary greatly across studies
- Individuals with severe or profound intellectual disability may not benefit from SM

### Target Learners
- School-age students (5-21 years) with challenging classroom behaviors, regular and special education

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reducing challenging classroom behaviors (aggression, antisocial, insubordinate) and increasing prosocial, on-task, and direction-following behavior; improving academic outcomes
