REACH Students teacher evaluation system combining professional practice and student growth measures¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 12 claims (12 for) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

Description¶

REACH (Recognizing Educators Advancing Chicago's Students) is CPS's teacher evaluation system instituted in fall 2012, replacing a 1970s-era checklist system. It "incorporates a professional practice score and up to two measures of student growth in a teacher's evaluation score." Professional practice is evaluated through four observations using the CPS Framework for Teaching, a modified version of the Danielson Framework, with 19 components across four domains. Student growth includes value-added scores and performance tasks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Ratings from four observations conducted by principals or assistant principals who have been trained and certified on the rubric
  • Value-added scores require teachers in tested subjects and grades; most non-tested teachers receive a school-wide average value-added score in literacy

Constraints¶

  • Charter schools do not utilize REACH and are not included in analyses
  • Librarians, counselors, clinicians, and other education support specialists rated on a different framework were not included in observation analyses
  • In 2013-14, observations comprised 75 percent of a teacher's overall REACH score

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improving instructional practice through focused feedback
  • Identifying teachers who excelled or needed improvement

Claims¶

  • Teachers with National Board Certification or advanced degrees differ from other teachers only on observation scores, not value added [+W]
  • Schools with lower percentages of Proficient and Distinguished observation ratings tend to be higher-poverty schools [+W]
  • Teachers with the lowest value-added and observation scores are overrepresented in schools serving the most disadvantaged students [+W]
  • Male teachers have lower observation and value-added scores than female teachers [+W]
  • Minority teachers have lower observation scores than white teachers but no significant differences on value added [+W]
  • Observation scores have a stronger relationship with school poverty than value-added scores [+W]
  • Missed observations left 24 percent of non-tenured teachers without a REACH rating, concentrated in a smaller number of schools undergoing organizational change [+W]
  • REACH evaluation ratings rose over time, with nearly 9 in 10 teachers receiving one of the top two ratings by 2016-17 [+W]
  • Teachers rated classroom observation scores as the most accurate REACH measure of teaching effectiveness and value-added measures as the least accurate [+W]
  • Under REACH fewer teachers received ratings in the top two categories than under the prior checklist system [+W]
  • Fewer non-tenured teachers were rated in the top two categories under REACH (58%) than under the previous checklist system (87%) [+W]
  • Most CPS teachers and administrators reported the REACH evaluation process improved classroom instruction and student learning [+W]
