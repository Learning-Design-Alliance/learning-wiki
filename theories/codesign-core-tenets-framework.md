# Co-design framework with core tenets of power sharing, relationships, and capability building

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 5 studies (4 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines co-design as "a process in which different stakeholders come together to find a solution to a shared problem through inclusive research and development," bringing together school communities, edtech companies, and researchers. It names three core tenets: ensure power sharing and inclusion (everyone's ideas considered from research through evaluation), prioritize relationships (co-designers must share a meaningful relationship based on trust and respect), and focus on capability building (everyone has something to teach and something to learn, increasing collaboration and power sharing). The Warwick-Panorama partnership is presented as an application, with trust from an existing relationship enabling vulnerability and access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Relationship-building between districts, providers, and partners is foundational, not optional; rushed timelines undermine authentic collaboration
#### Constraints
- Co-design does not progress in a straight line; deliverables may look incomplete at various stages, which the article calls normal and healthy

### Target Learners
- District leaders
- Edtech solution providers
- Funders of co-design initiatives

### Target Learning Objectives
- Developing responsive, human-centered student success solutions through multi-stakeholder collaboration
