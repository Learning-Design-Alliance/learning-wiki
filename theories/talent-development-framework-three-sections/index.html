Talent Development Framework: a three-section model of state educator talent policy¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Talent Development Framework is a comprehensive model of the state policy and practice areas found in a strong educator talent development system. As the document states, "the framework groups state policy and practice into three key interdependent policy and practice sections": attracting talent into the profession, preparing future teachers and leaders, and developing, supporting, and retaining educators in the field. It is intended as a roadmap for state- and district-level policymakers to systemically address and improve educator quality across the career continuum.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Attention to how each policy and practice section is connected, so the sections function as a coherent, aligned system rather than separate efforts

Constraints¶

  • Implementation is heavily influenced by state context, such as rural versus urban population and economic conditions
  • Many talent development functions are implemented at the local district level, so the state-local relationship shapes operation

Target Learners¶

  • State and district education policymakers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Systemic improvement of educator quality and equitable student access to effective teachers and school leaders

Claims¶

  • Piecemeal Educator Policymaking Drains Talent [+M]
  • Unified Talent Data System Generates Prioritization Insights [+M]
