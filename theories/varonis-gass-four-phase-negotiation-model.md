# Varonis and Gass four-phase model of negotiation of meaning routines

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The chapter adopts the Varonis and Gass (1985) four-phase model of negotiation routines as its analytical framework: "The first attempt at modeling negotiation routines was made by Varonis and Gass (1985) who designed a four-phase model". Phase one is a trigger (lexical, grammar-based, syntax-based or content-based); phase two is a signal of non-comprehension (clarification request, confirmation check or comprehension check); phase three is the response (self-repetition, paraphrase or incorporation); and the optional phase four is a reaction to the response signalling the routine's end. The study operationalises this model as an annotation scheme (Table 2) applied in EXMARaLDA to all three CMC datasets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactions must contain identifiable trigger, signal, response and optional reaction moves that can be annotated on speaker-dependent tiers
#### Constraints
- The reaction phase is optional in the model, and the article found no combined trigger types in its data

### Target Learners
- non-native speaker university students of English (first year Master's biology students)

### Target Learning Objectives
- development of interactional competence and negotiation of meaning in a second language
