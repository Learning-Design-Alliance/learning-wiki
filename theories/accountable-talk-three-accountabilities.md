# Accountable talk: three accountabilities — to knowledge, reasoning, and community

## Description
The primer describes accountable talk (attributed to Resnick et al., 2018) as a concept emphasizing that productive dialogue holds students accountable to knowledge, reasoning, and community. Students "ground their claims in evidence and justify their reasoning based on available information," contributions go beyond simple agreement or disagreement to explore alternatives, and all students are positioned as valuable contributors so that ideas, not individuals, are challenged.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be accountable to achieving the positive outcomes of such discourse and ideally hold each other responsible for getting facts right and thinking through challenges together
#### Constraints
- The primer presents accountable talk as a framework from Resnick et al. (2018), not as a result it tested itself
