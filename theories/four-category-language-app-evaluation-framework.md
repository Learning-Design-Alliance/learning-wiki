# Four-category framework for evaluating language-learning apps: technology, pedagogy, user experience, and language learning

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents an evaluation framework grounded in prior app-evaluation frameworks and SLA task-design principles, offered as reflection questions rather than a scored rubric. As stated, "The new framework proposed here is divided into four primary categories: technology, user experience, pedagogy, and subject specific (in this case language learning), each with a number of criteria." Criteria overlap across categories (e.g. feedback appears in all four), and the questions are intended as a reflection tool for learners, teachers, developers, and researchers rather than a marking scheme.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The framework applies to commercially available self-contained apps installed on devices, not to all resources accessible through them such as eBooks or web resources
#### Constraints
- The article states there is no indication of how many criteria an app must meet, since relevance depends on learning preferences, location, personal circumstances, and the app's function
