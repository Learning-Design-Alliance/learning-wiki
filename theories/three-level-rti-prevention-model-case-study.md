# Three-level RTI prevention model for identifying learning disabilities

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The case study presents RTI as a multi-level prevention framework in which students move through primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention based on measured response. It states that "RTI uses response to intervention (or lack thereof), at various levels of a prevention system, to identify students with mild to moderate disabilities." Primary prevention is effective general education instruction with screening; secondary prevention is small-group research-based tutoring with frequent progress monitoring; tertiary prevention is special education with comprehensive evaluation and individualized programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Effective, research-based instruction must be in place at primary prevention so that nonresponse is not attributable to poor instruction
#### Constraints
- The number of levels in multi-level prevention systems varies from model to model; this case study uses the three-level model, with primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention corresponding to Tiers 1-3

### Target Learners
- Elementary students at risk for learning difficulties
- Students suspected of having learning disabilities

### Target Learning Objectives
- Early identification of academic risk
- Appropriate allocation of increasingly intensive instructional support
