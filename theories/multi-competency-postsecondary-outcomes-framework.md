# Multi-competency framework linking academic and nonacademic competency indicators to postsecondary outcomes

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report organizes student competencies into several indicator categories—math skills, work habits, leadership skills, teamwork and other sports-related skills, and attitudes about whether luck or effort determine success in life (locus of control)—and treats them as jointly related to postsecondary educational attainment and earnings. It states: "we examine whether the benefit of improving a given competency depends on a student’s existing strengths and weaknesses." This framing treats competencies as interacting rather than independent predictors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simultaneous measurement of multiple competency indicators in the same longitudinal dataset
- A flexible functional form to model interactions among competencies
#### Constraints
- The framework is estimated with observational NELS survey data, so estimates are associations rather than causal effects

### Target Learners
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Objectives
- development of academic and nonacademic competencies that predict postsecondary education and labor market outcomes
