# Comprehensive assessment system framework: summative, interim, and formative assessments with complementary purposes

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes assessment around a framework in which "multiple forms of assessment that each emphasize a different purpose and use" complement one another across levels of an education agency. Summative assessment samples tasks from a broadly defined curriculum to determine proficiency; interim assessment (MAP Growth) measures achievement and growth during terms; formative assessment consists of frequent instructional practices aligned to lessons. The framework assigns each type a distinct decision-making role.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each assessment type must emphasize a different purpose and use, with data appropriate for decision-making at every level of an education agency
#### Constraints
- Summative results are usually available after instruction has completed, giving them "very limited utility for measuring student progress during the year or informing instructional decisions"

### Target Learners
- K-12 students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Measuring achievement and growth in reading, mathematics, language usage, and science
