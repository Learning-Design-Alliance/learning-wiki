# Coombs' three-category typology of formal, informal and non-formal education, with informal learning as an overarching concept including incidental learning

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Coombs et al. (1973) classically grouped learning methods into formal education (hierarchically structured, chronologically graded), informal education (the lifelong acquisition of values, skills and knowledge from daily experience) and non-formal education (organised educational activity outside the formal system). The review reports Gunn's critiques and adopts informal learning as an overarching concept that can include incidental learning as a subset, following Watkins and Marsick, for whom "Both informal andincidental learning tend to occur in non-routine situations."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The typology requires deciding what counts as a formal learning context, a question the review identifies as carrying at least one further unexamined ambiguity.
#### Constraints
- Gunn argues the threefold categorisation is deficient: informal learning can be highly structured, dormant and action-based learning are neglected, and the informal/incidental distinction needs drawing.

### Target Learners
- adults in voluntary organisations, workplaces and community settings

### Target Learning Objectives
- classifying adult learning activities by context and intentionality
