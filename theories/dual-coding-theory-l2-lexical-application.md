# Dual coding theory (Paivio) as an account of visual facilitation of L2 lexical knowledge

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Dual coding theory holds that cognition comprises verbal and nonverbal (imagery) systems that are "functionally independent yet interconnected"; when both systems process information, the combination produces qualitatively different mnemonic representations, and verbal and visual codes "function additively" to enhance memory. The article applies DCT to L2 vocabulary, predicting that words presented with corresponding images are better recalled than single-code presentation, and extends it to more advanced lexical knowledge—awareness of errors and correct usage—beyond initial form-meaning links, and to adolescent learners previously overlooked in DCT research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning materials must present verbal information alongside corresponding visual code so the two systems can jointly process the content
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence covers awareness of correct usage of confusing lexical pairs in adolescent EFL learners, not all dimensions of vocabulary knowledge

### Target Learners
- adolescent EFL learners (ninth-grade middle school students)

### Target Learning Objectives
- accurate use of confusing L2 lexical items
- awareness of lexical errors
