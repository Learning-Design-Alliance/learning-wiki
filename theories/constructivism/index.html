Constructivism¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 26 claims (10 for, 14 mixed, 2 against) · 32 studies (8 causal, 6 quant-synthesis, 6 theoretical, 4 associational, 3 review, 3 design, 2 qualitative), q1–q4 · 5 of 32 report an effect size · 22 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Constructivism is the broad view that learners actively build understanding by connecting new experiences and information to what they already know. Rather than treating knowledge as something transmitted whole from teacher to learner, constructivist perspectives emphasize interpretation, prior knowledge, social interaction, and meaning-making.

In instruction, constructivism is less a single method than a family resemblance across approaches that value active engagement, explanation, inquiry, collaboration, and reflection. It is often contrasted with highly transmissive teaching, but in practice many effective designs mix constructivist processes with explicit modeling, sequencing, and feedback.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Especially relevant where interpretation, inquiry, discussion, and application matter
  • Supports designs that connect learning to prior knowledge, authentic tasks, and social interaction
  • Often used to justify active learning, problem-centered learning, and collaborative meaning-making

Target Learners¶

  • Learners building new understanding on top of prior knowledge and lived experience
  • Learners in contexts where dialogue, inquiry, and interpretation matter more than rote repetition alone
  • Groups that benefit from discussion and collaborative refinement of ideas

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Conceptual understanding through active sense-making
  • Transfer through connecting ideas across contexts
  • Learner agency in explanation, interpretation, and problem solving

Claims¶

  • Prompting learners to self-explain improves understanding and problem solving on immediate tests by a moderate average amount, with little evidence yet on delayed or classroom gains. [+S]
  • A training-design argument, not tested by any study recorded here, holds that practising whole complex tasks improves transfer of complex skills [~M]
  • Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M]
  • A constructivist, process-oriented syllabus was suggested to be more effective than a knowledge-based approach for cross-cultural learning (Wright, 2000, as reported) [+W]
  • Constructivism is the major learning theory reflected in Iranian Pre-University ELT textbooks [+W]
  • Iranian ELT textbooks show no smooth shift from behaviourism to constructivism across educational levels [~W]
  • Most prospective teachers see constructivist conceptions as highly valuable but not very applicable, and traditional conceptions as not valuable but highly applicable [~W]
  • Writing to the teacher led students to treat writing as evaluation and reproduce transmitted content rather than construct their own knowledge [+W] — attached 2026-10-17 from Kingir (2013), which proposed "Knowledge-transforming model of writing as a mode of learning in science".
  • Structural relations exist between students' conceptions of constructivist learning and their regulation and processing strategies [~W]
  • The authors argue that constructivists talking about constructivist learning environments is not necessarily a paradox [~W]
  • Some participants' ChatGPT interactions followed a pattern of inquiry-based learning with iterative prompt refinement [~W]
  • In a constructivist learning environment, students' perceptions of assessment shifted toward deeper demands but their actual learning approaches did not, and surface approaches increased [-W]
  • Persistence of inscriptions in the VMT environment supported students explaining postings and establishing collaborative activity [~W]
  • In a CSCL biology problem-solving session, procedural problem solving tended to dominate students' interactions, with conceptual knowledge construction present only where strictly necessary [-W]
  • Teachers oriented towards students and changing students' conceptions are associated with students reporting deeper approaches to learning [+W]
  • Dichotomous views of learning as individual or social continue to shape research and learning environments [~W]
  • The constructivist approach produces more positive attitudes towards lessons than traditional teaching, with an overall effect size of 0.728 across 43 Turkish studies [+W]
  • The effect of constructivist learning models on student outcomes varies significantly by location, with Turkey (ES=.87) and Indonesia (ES=1.07) showing high-level effects and China (ES=.58) and mixed-country samples (ES=.67) medium-level effects [~W]
  • Educational level does not significantly moderate the effect of constructivism on attitudes towards lessons [~W]
  • Geographical region does not significantly moderate the effect of constructivism on attitudes towards lessons [~W]
  • Type of publication does not significantly moderate the effect of constructivism on attitudes towards lessons [~W]
  • Teaching reading comprehension with prior knowledge questions improves primary pupils' reading comprehension performance [+W] — attached 2026-11-01 from Hanna Onyi Yusuf et al. (2013), which proposed "Learning as conceptual change: prior knowledge actively shapes how learners interpret instruction".
  • Fifth-grade students taught science through inquiry-based instruction supported by the 5E learning cycle achieved significantly higher post-test scores than students taught by traditional methods [+W] — attached 2026-11-01 from Ali Abdi (2014), which proposed "Inquiry-based teaching approach in science education".
  • Guided learning at workstations (G2) produced significantly higher post- and retention-test knowledge than conventional workstations (G1) and teacher-centered instruction (G3) in low achievers [+W] — attached 2026-11-01 from Heyne et al. Related Theories¶

  • Sociomaterial Agency of Tools — extends constructivism by treating the material substrate of a hands-on task, not just the learner's cognitive engagement with it, as consequential for what gets learned
  • Stages of Cognitive Development — Piaget's stage theory is the direct historical foundation constructivism builds on
  • Self-Regulated Learning — constructivist learning often depends on learners monitoring and revising their own understanding
  • Cognitive Load Theory — constructivist tasks still have to manage working-memory limits, especially for novices
  • Information Processing Theory — active construction still depends on attention, encoding, and retrieval processes
  • Cognitivism — constructivism grew out of cognitivism but rejects the assumption that knowledge is mind-independent and can be mapped onto a learner
  • Sociocultural Theory — Vygotsky is a direct historical source for constructivism's emphasis on social interaction and meaning-making
  • Situated Learning — shares constructivism's emphasis on authentic context, but centers participation in social practice rather than individual meaning-making
  • Constructionism — Papert's extension of constructivism, adding the requirement that learning takes the form of building a public, shareable artifact
  • Co-constructionism requires an active individual, an active environment, and culture
  • Constructivism as a theory of knowledge and learning with individualistic, social, and combined branches
  • Constructivist prescriptive principles for learning environments
  • Piaget's genetic epistemology studies how the human mind moves from less sufficient to higher knowledge
  • Vygotsky's social constructivism: psychological phenomena emerge from social interaction
  • Constructivism learning theory: knowledge as assumption, learning as active construction, students as subjects, teachers as helpers
