# Bayes factor testing of homogeneous within-person variance in hierarchical models

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A methodological framework that reframes measurement reliability as an individual-level property rather than a stable property of a questionnaire or task. It uses "a novel approach, using the Bayes factor, wherein a researcher can directly test for homogeneous within-person variance in hierarchical models", addressing the descriptive nature of ICCs that "depends upon the assumption of a common within-person variance". The framework leads into "the notion of individually varying ICCs" and is demonstrated on cognitive inhibition tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hierarchical models of repeated-measures data in which within-person variance can be parameterized and tested
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated in the article on cognitive inhibition tasks; the abstract presents ICCs as descriptive and assumption-dependent rather than invalid

### Target Learners
- Researchers assessing measurement reliability in repeated-measure designs

### Target Learning Objectives
