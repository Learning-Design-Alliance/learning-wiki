Double-dose algebra as a sorting-plus-supporting alternative to tracking and detracking¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 13 claims (10 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (5 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 13 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The double-dose strategy created homogeneous algebra classes for organizational reasons but ensured that low-track students received challenging coursework and high-quality instruction through extended time, curricular resources, and professional development. "the policy created homogeneous algebra classes for organizational reasons but ensured that low-track students received challenging coursework and high-quality instruction." This approach differs from traditional tracking (which provided low-level content and low expectations) and from detracking (which mixed abilities without additional support). The policy operated through three mechanisms: expanded instructional time, improved instruction from resources and professional development, and ability grouping into more homogeneous classes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires below-norm students to be identified by a clear skill threshold (eighth-grade ITBS scores below the national median)
  • Requires additional instructional time (two periods of algebra)
  • Requires curricular resources and professional development for teachers of support classes

Constraints¶

  • Some schools could not fully sort due to scheduling difficulties or insufficient numbers of students, resulting in heterogeneous classrooms with a simultaneous support class instead

Target Learners¶

  • Ninth-grade students with below-average incoming math skills
  • Ninth-grade students with above-average incoming math skills in sorted classrooms

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Algebra I proficiency and passing
  • Preparation for subsequent advanced mathematics courses

Related Theories¶

  • Three-mechanism conceptual framework for how double-dose algebra affects outcomes: expanded time, changed instruction, and unintended tracking

Examples¶

  • Double-dose algebra: tracking with supports as an alternative to detracking
  • Use double-period support courses to expand instructional time for low-skilled 9th-grade math students
  • Provide targeted support depending on sorting strategy: monitor students weakest relative to classmates, and support teachers of disruptive sorted classrooms with classroom management and challenging engagement
