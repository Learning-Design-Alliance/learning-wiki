# Taxonomy of pedagogical approaches in scripts of computational processes: tutoring vs. simulation, each with two sub-categories

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives a two-branch taxonomy classifying prospective teachers' Scratch scripts of computational processes by the pedagogical approach they reflect. The first branch, "Tutorial", contains scripts that "do not demonstrate dynamic computational processes but serve as instructional tools for learning or assessing the said processes"; the second, "Simulation", represents domain processes dynamically, with sub-categories for demonstration only and for demonstration plus exploration, where users "alter parameters, influencing the simulations’ execution". The authors state the primary distinction lies in the teaching approaches reflected: tutoring aligns with a traditional multimedia teaching model, while simulations involve active domain entities behaving by domain-specific rules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scripts must be classified by indicators of pedagogical approach: tutorial for learning or assessment, simulation for demonstration only or demonstration and exploration
#### Constraints
- The taxonomy was derived from one course's summative assignment submissions and the authors caution that generalization must be applied very carefully due to the convenience sample

### Target Learners
- prospective teachers across preschool, elementary, and secondary programs

### Target Learning Objectives
- identifying and expressing computational thinking through scripts of computational processes in any school subject
