# Communicative-cognitive framework for teaching pronunciation: description and analysis, listening discrimination, controlled practice, guided practice, communicative practice

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Celce-Murcia, Brinton, and Goodwin (1996), as presented in the digest, propose a framework supporting a communicative-cognitive approach to teaching pronunciation. A planning stage identifies learners' needs, pedagogical priorities, and teachers' readiness; the teaching stage structures lessons through description and analysis of the target feature, listening discrimination activities, controlled practice and feedback, guided practice and feedback, and communicative practice and feedback, moving learners from awareness to free communicative use.
