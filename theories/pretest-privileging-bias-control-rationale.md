# Theoretical rationale for privileging pretests in bias control: high correlation with both outcome and selection process

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article offers an explanatory account of why pretest measures of the study outcome are privileged tools for controlling selection bias in observational studies. The rationale rests on two correlations: pretests are "often highly correlated with the outcome," and "in many contexts, they are also highly correlated with the selection process," so measuring them should capture much of both the outcome signal and the selection into treatment. The article tests this rationale empirically and finds the resulting bias-elimination pattern more complex than the rationale predicts.
