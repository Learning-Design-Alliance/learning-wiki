Taxonomy of five educational philosophies for adult education¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Chapter 4 describes five educational philosophies that grow out of the general systems: essentialism, perennialism, progressivism, reconstructionism, and existentialism. Each is characterized by its stance on content, method, and purpose—essentialists emphasize subject matter and passing on culture, perennialists discipline the mind through great books, progressivists stress problem solving and the learning process, reconstructionists use the scientific method to pursue changing ends, and educational existentialism directs education toward individual self-fulfillment. Apps presents them as material against which educators take a personal stand.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators are asked to take a personal stand on the issues each philosophy raises.

Constraints¶

  • Apps states there is obviously no clear agreement as to which educational philosophy should be followed, and presents criticisms of each position.

Target Learners¶

  • adult educators

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • locating one's educational philosophy among established alternatives

Claims¶

  • In a survey of physical education and sports teachers, existentialist education is the most endorsed and essentialism the least endorsed of five educational philosophies [+M]
  • Graduates differ from undergraduates across all five philosophical beliefs at the .20 level [+W]
  • Pippi's specific behaviors in the series instantiate Pragmatism, Reconstructionism, and Existentialism, including rejecting imposed learning decisions and reconstructing assessment. [+M]
  • Physical education teachers studying in a graduate program endorse progressivism, perennialism and essentialism more than teachers with an undergraduate degree [+M]
  • Physical education teachers with more professional seniority hold weaker progressivism, existentialist education and reconstructivism beliefs and stronger essentialism beliefs, with no significant seniority difference for perennialism [+M]
  • Pippi Longstocking portrays a clash between contemporary educational philosophies (embodied by Pippi) and an Essentialist understanding (embodied by the other social agents). [+W]
