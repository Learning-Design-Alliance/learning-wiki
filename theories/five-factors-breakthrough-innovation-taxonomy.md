# Five-factor taxonomy distinguishing education innovations as breakthroughs

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that "Five factors can distinguish an education innovation as a 'breakthrough'": discontinuity; substantial improvement in outcomes; system reconfiguration; generativity and new opportunity spaces; and expanded participation and new forms of evidence. Each factor is defined and illustrated with a canonical example—kindergarten, high-dosage tutoring, competency-based education, open educational resources, and Sesame Street. The taxonomy serves as an evaluative lens for judging whether an education innovation qualifies as breakthrough rather than incremental.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An innovation should be assessed against all five factors, which draw across research and practice
#### Constraints
- The factors are offered as a working framework ('can distinguish'), not a validated measurement instrument

### Target Learners
- Education innovators, researchers, and funders evaluating innovations affecting pre-K-12 and broader learners

### Target Learning Objectives
- Evaluating whether an innovation produces breakthrough-level change in learning outcomes, access, and systems
