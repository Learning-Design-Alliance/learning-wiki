# Social constructionism as the basis for collaborative writing instruction

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study, `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper defines social constructionism as "a philosophy of knowledge whichasserts that truth is not something eternal" but a product of human activity, holding that "meaning is not privately constructed but is generated by social interaction". It draws the view from Vygotsky, Kuhn, Rorty, Geertz and Fish, and uses it to ground collaborative learning in the composition classroom.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Meaning is treated as generated through social interaction carried out in language, so learners need to take part in the conversation of a community; the paper cites Vygotsky's account that socialized speech is turned inward to become inner speech or thought.
- Learning is framed, following Rorty as the paper reports him, as a shift in a person's relations with others rather than a change inside the person.
#### Constraints
- The paper presents the view as a synthesis of other thinkers' positions and reports no empirical test of it.

### Target Learners
- Students in college composition courses

### Target Learning Objectives
- Writing effective discourse by learning the language of the community students hope to enter
