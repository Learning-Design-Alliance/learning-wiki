# Three-component decomposition of teacher grade effects: total, persistent, and mismatch

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article conceptualizes teacher influence on student grades in three ways: total effects on ninth-grade course grades, persistent effects on students' grades in the same subject the following fall, and grading mismatch between the grades given and students' subsequent performance. The authors adapt the long- and short-run value-added approach of Gilraine and Pope (2021) from test scores to grades, so that mismatch 'identifies differences in measurement (grading inflation/deflation) while persistent effects identify differences in effectiveness.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires longitudinal data linking students to ninth- and tenth-grade teachers and grades in the same subject, plus extensive student, course, and school controls
#### Constraints
- The methodology does not allow differentiation between lenient grading and temporary skill gains as explanations for positive mismatch
- School effects not captured by controls are incorporated into teacher effect estimates because school fixed effects are deliberately omitted

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade high school students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Accurate measurement of academic preparation through course grades
