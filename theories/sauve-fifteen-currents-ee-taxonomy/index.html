Sauvé's taxonomy of fifteen currents of environmental education¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article reports that, grounded in a review of the international EE literature, Sauvé (2005) established fifteen different "currents," each a general way of envisioning and practicing EE, divided into long-tradition currents (naturalist, conservationist/resourcist, problem-solving, systemic, scientific, humanistic, and value-centered) and emerging currents (holistic, bioregionalist, praxic, socially critical, feminist, ethnographic, eco-education, and sustainability). The article uses this mapping to frame the heterogeneous discursive formations circulating in the EE field, noting currents overlap and must not be reified.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Each current is understood as a theoretical proposition comprising a plurality of propositions, not a monolithic category

Constraints¶

  • A single proposition may be associated with two or three currents depending on the angle of analysis; currents share overlapping characteristics and must not be reified

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service science and biology teachers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • recognizing diverse approaches and discursive formations in environmental education

Claims¶

  • Brazilian EE legislation shifted from Law 6938/1981's naturalistic, conservation-restricted discourse to the socio-environmental perspective of Law 9795/1999 and the 2013 DCNEB [+W]
  • The biology teacher-training curricular matrix devotes most environment-related hours to biological perspectives, with few courses promoting EE from other perspectives [+W]
  • Pre-service biology teachers' discourses about environmental education are predominantly restricted to conservation or preservation, with some confusion between EE and ecology [+W]
  • Student discourses showed little association between EE and socioeconomic issues, contrary to the interdisciplinary approach recommended by Brazilian legislation [+W]

Examples¶

  • Foster citizenship and prevent silencing of environmental conflicts and risks in school EE
