Guided Self-Analysis System (GSA): teacher self-analysis of videotaped lessons as professional development¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Guided Self-Analysis System for Professional Development is a set of materials through which teachers analyze recordings of their own classroom teaching. The document states that "The Guided Self-Analysis System consists of materials which guide the teacher in analyzing his own teaching behavior, so that he can gain an objective understanding of his strengths and weaknesses." Teachers make 15-20 minute videotapes, replay each tape three or four times with a different workbook each time, code observed behaviors on coding forms, and build profiles of their habitual techniques that can be compared across successive tapings. The system is described as an "open system" adaptable to varied teaching situations and grounded in a synthesis of learning theory.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must have access to videotape/audiotape equipment and the GSA workbooks, plus a lead-teacher and workshop structure within the school district

Constraints¶

  • The document states the analysis is the teacher's private concern, with the teacher as the only observer of the tapes

Target Learners¶

  • teachers of preschool children and grades K-3
  • inservice and preservice teaching personnel

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • teachers' awareness and improvement of their own classroom teaching behaviors

Claims¶

  • Perceived discrepancy between actual teaching performance and goals motivates teachers to change their teaching [+W]
  • Knowledge gained by self-analysis is more likely to produce constructive change in teaching than insights given by an observer [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Implement GSA through a lead-teacher who runs introductory and regular inservice workshops with scheduled taping
